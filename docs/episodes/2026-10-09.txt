Good morning. It's Friday, October 9, 2026. Today: oil and rate-hike fears knock stocks from New York to Mumbai, the peso hits a record low, and the Fed signals it isn't done tightening.

Start with Wall Street. It was a split, defensive session. The Dow Jones Industrial Average edged up 0.1 percent to close at about 51,232. The S&P 500 fell 0.5 percent to about 7,765, and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 1.3 percent to about 27,193. The US 10-year Treasury yield slipped 5 basis points to 5.23 percent, helped by solid demand at a 30-year auction that drew buyers back to long-dated debt at yields near their highest in more than two decades.

The drag was concentrated in technology and artificial intelligence names. Nvidia fell 2.9 percent, leading a slide in AI-linked stocks including Oracle and CoreWeave, after CNBC confirmed OpenAI told investors it hit roughly 50 billion dollars in annualised revenue at the end of September — a figure that fell short of what chip investors had priced in. Amazon lost 2.3 percent even as it overhauled its devices line-up with a higher-priced Alexa tablet and dropped the budget Fire. Microsoft dropped 1.4 percent after the Labor Department suspended it and Adobe from the green card labour programme, citing multiple active federal investigations. Alphabet fell 0.6 percent, Tesla 0.7 percent, and Meta was flat. Apple was the standout, up 1.1 percent. Telecom shares were hit separately after SpaceX agreed to buy a nationwide spectrum portfolio, pushing Starlink deeper into US mobile.

Now central banks. Minutes from the Federal Reserve's September 15-16 meeting showed a unanimous rate hike and an appetite for at least one more this year, but no commitment on timing. Governor Christopher Waller said more hikes are needed while stressing flexibility on the pace, and other officials cast doubt on a move this month. The cost is visible in housing: the 30-year fixed mortgage rate has reached 7.40 percent. Why it matters: the Fed is still tightening into an oil-driven inflation shock, which keeps global yields high and emerging-market currencies under pressure.

For the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, there's no new decision, but the signals are clear. After a faster-than-expected September inflation print, Bank of America and local economists now expect another BSP hike at the next meeting.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi sank 2.2 percent to close at about 5,610, its weakest level in months, as a record-low peso, surging oil and firm expectations of both Fed and BSP tightening hit sentiment. The peso weakened 0.4 percent to 62.89 to the dollar, a new record low. Gross international reserves have fallen to a three-year low of about 100 billion dollars, partly on lower gold values and partly because the BSP has been smoothing peso swings — that limits how aggressively it can defend the currency from here. In corporate news, Robinsons Retail trading was suspended as the Gokongwei group completed a share transfer ahead of delisting, and Megawide secured about 12.8 billion pesos in new housing contracts under the 4PH programme in September. Two more to watch: Philippine Airlines has cancelled Riyadh flights after strikes on Saudi airports, disrupting a core overseas-worker route, and the International Criminal Court ruled former President Rodrigo Duterte is fit to stand trial. The Senate also ratified a visiting forces agreement with France.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed down 0.8 percent at about 22,603 and the BSE Sensex fell 0.6 percent to about 72,639, extending a slide that has taken the Sensex to roughly a 32-month low and the Nifty to its weakest levels since early 2025. The rupee was broadly flat at 96.78 to the dollar, near record lows. The pressure is coming from four directions: the Reserve Bank of India's hawkish repo rate hike, surging crude, rising US bond yields and relentless foreign institutional selling. The RBI has signalled more tightening may follow, which is lifting borrowing costs on home loans and making debt funds volatile. In stocks, Infosys and Wipro ADRs fell about 3 percent after Washington suspended major IT outsourcers — including Cognizant, TCS and HCL — from the green card programme tied to H-1B workers. And GQG exited ITC in a block deal worth about 9,395 crore rupees after a 30 percent fall in the stock this year. CNBC also flags an El Niño food-price risk ahead. Why it matters: India's growth data is strong, but its markets are hostage to oil and the dollar.

Now the world. Oil is the central story. Crude jumped again as Iran stepped up tanker attacks and a hurricane threatened US Gulf production. President Trump said the United States will not attack Iran before the November midterm election and called talks with Tehran productive, though his national security team has discussed restarting large-scale operations. US gas prices are expected to stay above 4 dollars a gallon on November 3, according to Kalshi traders. Why it matters: every dollar on crude feeds directly into the inflation that is keeping the Fed, the RBI and the BSP hawkish.

Related to that, Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan are activating what's being called the Mecca defence pact to counter Houthi attacks from Yemen, following strikes on Saudi airports. The United Nations says fighting has displaced more than 200,000 people in Yemen in five weeks. For Filipino listeners, this is also an overseas-worker and remittance risk in the Gulf.

In Ukraine, officials say a Russian strike on two crowded buses in the front-line city of Kramatorsk killed at least 30 people, part of a surge in attacks on civilians. Ukraine is hitting back at Russian infrastructure: internet giant Yandex halted operations at one of its major data centres after a drone attack.

Three quick ones. Israel has shut the British consulate in occupied East Jerusalem; London says it will keep a presence there anyway. In Spain, hundreds of protesters have pitched tents in central Madrid blaming so-called vulture funds for the housing crisis, and the prime minister has called a snap election — political risk returning to a major euro-zone economy. And the US has charged former Venezuelan leader Nicolás Maduro and his wife with conspiracy to commit torture of US citizens held during his time in power.

Finally, in business and culture: Adidas is suing Australian label White Fox over a four-stripes design, Starbucks is reported to be working with advisers on a takeover proposal for Chipotle, Alberta votes this month on whether to move toward a binding independence referendum, and Canadian poet Anne Carson has won the Nobel Prize in Literature.

That's your Morning Briefing for Friday, October 9. Watch oil and the peso today.
