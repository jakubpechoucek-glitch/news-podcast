Good morning. It's Sunday, October 25, 2026, and this is your Morning Briefing on Finance and Geopolitics. Today: a risk-off close on Wall Street driven by AI financing fears, a record low for the peso, and a Brazilian run-off vote.

To the markets. Wall Street ended the week on the back foot. The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at about 52,215, down 1.2 percent. The S&P 500 finished near 7,624, down 0.5 percent. The Nasdaq Composite held up best at about 25,955, down 0.2 percent. The US 10-year Treasury yield rose 3 basis points to 4.99 percent, right at the 5 percent line. Why it matters: with yields that high, equity valuations have almost no room for disappointment.

The driver was renewed anxiety over how the artificial intelligence build-out is being financed. So-called neocloud operators took a market hit, credit default swap spreads on AI-linked borrowers widened, and Solidigm pulled its planned IPO. Nvidia, which has moved from chip supplier to financier of its own customers, closed down 0.6 percent at about 211 dollars as investors questioned that concentration of risk. Alphabet fell 1.2 percent to about 320, Amazon dropped 1.0 percent to 248, Meta lost 1.0 percent to about 643, Tesla slipped 0.6 percent to about 372, and Apple was barely changed, down 0.1 percent at about 348. Microsoft was the lone gainer, up 0.7 percent at about 525. Why it matters: money is rotating out of AI names into defensives just before hyperscaler earnings, the next real test of whether this capital spending is sustainable. Bitcoin near 80,700 dollars and Ethereum around 2,370 dollars tell the same risk-off story.

Central banks. The Federal Reserve decides on Wednesday. The consensus is a pause this month, with ING and others flagging a possible hike in December. Note the direction of travel: markets are debating tightening, not easing. For the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, local markets are still absorbing its latest rate hike, and economist polls, plus Fitch unit BMI, point to another 25 basis points in December. BusinessMirror reports weather-driven food inflation could extend that tightening into the first half of 2027.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi fell 0.9 percent to about 5,729, a second straight sell-down, pressured by the rate hike and the currency. The peso touched a fresh record low of 63.615 to the dollar and is quoted near 63.58, weaker by 0.3 percent on the day. Why it matters: imported fuel and food costs feed straight back into the inflation the BSP is chasing.

Bank earnings are the bright spot. UnionBank's nine-month profit jumped 53 percent to 9.9 billion pesos, and it is folding UnionDigital and UBX back into the parent to streamline digital operations. PNB's nine-month profit rose 15 percent to 21.3 billion pesos, and Landbank's was up 7.6 percent. The equity pipeline is heavy: GCash is set for a record 92.3 billion peso listing, and PLDT is marketing a 24.2 billion peso data centre REIT, the first of its kind here. Separately, the World Bank says the Philippine wealth gap is widening even as poverty falls, and the Air Force confirmed four pilots were killed in an aircraft crash in Batangas.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed at 22,600, down 0.4 percent on the day and 2.4 percent on the week, falling in all four sessions. The Sensex ended at about 73,124, down 0.2 percent. The rupee weakened 0.2 percent to 94.94 to the dollar. Two pressures: crude nearing 114 dollars a barrel, and foreign institutional investors selling for a tenth consecutive week. The damage was worse below the large caps, with the Nifty Midcap 100 down 5.6 percent on the week and smallcaps down 7.3 percent.

Reserve Bank Governor Sanjay Malhotra said future rate moves will depend on growth and inflation trends, while the RBI bulletin argues macro fundamentals remain solid. Reliance Jio Platforms lists on Tuesday after an IPO subscribed 22.2 times, with grey-market pricing implying a 22 percent debut gain and a market value near 10.3 lakh crore rupees, potentially a top-five Indian company. On earnings, Kotak Mahindra Bank's consolidated profit rose 26 percent year on year, and AU Small Finance Bank's jumped 53 percent. Silver has broken below 2 lakh rupees a kilogram for the first time since December 2025.

Now to the world. France. Almost 1,000 people were arrested as clashes followed another day of mass protests, though officials avoided a repeat of last weekend's rioting and looting. French borrowing costs hit a 24-year high this week on budget and political risk, and Moody's held the rating at Aa3. That's relief, but investors are openly asking whether the stress spreads across the euro zone.

The Middle East. Iran blamed Washington for the collapse of nuclear talks, urged a fresh attempt, and warned that even limited strikes would bring a response several times greater. US embassies are tightening security after a memo from intelligence director Jay Clayton said Iran is in the advanced stages of planning attacks on Americans ahead of the midterms. Houthi missiles struck targets in Saudi Arabia and in Yemen, including Aden's airport and a coastguard base. Why it matters: that combination keeps a live risk premium in oil, already near 114 dollars.

Israel votes on Tuesday. Israeli forces demolished Palestinian homes in Hebron and are preparing a full lockdown of the occupied West Bank after two soldiers were killed on Friday. In Gaza, bakeries are closing for lack of flour and severe hunger is spreading.

Brazil holds its presidential run-off today, with Lula and Bolsonaro drawing thousands to election-eve rallies. It is a knife-edge vote on whether Latin America's biggest economy stays left or shifts right, and commodity markets will price the result on Monday.

Three quick ones. Ukraine says its Starlink connection is now stable after a week of speed limits that hampered military and drone operations; Elon Musk claims Ukraine violated the agreement, and Kyiv is hedging by partnering with Nokia and satellite maker ICEYE. From October 30, the White House lifts tariffs on some Canadian goods, including certain machinery, while adding others such as carpets, with the midterms clearly shaping trade policy. And in Albania, protesters fought running battles with police on a twelfth day of anti-government demonstrations.

That's your briefing for Sunday, October 25. Trade carefully, and we'll see you tomorrow morning.
