Good morning. It's Thursday, October 22, 2026. Wall Street closed higher on artificial intelligence optimism, the peso set another record low ahead of the Bangko Sentral's rate decision, and Indian stocks fell again as crude surged.

Start with markets. The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at about 52,856, up 0.7 percent. The S&P 500 finished near 7,757, up 0.6 percent. The Nasdaq Composite led, closing around 26,703, up 1.1 percent, powered by semiconductors and a 10 percent jump in SpaceX shares after an earnings beat. Weakness in Netflix and AT&T capped the broader advance after Netflix issued a soft fourth-quarter forecast. The US 10-year Treasury yield ended at 5.15 percent, up 3 basis points, recovering from session lows after a 13 billion dollar 20-year auction and a report of renewed Middle East hostilities that lifted oil.

Big tech was mixed but mostly positive. Tesla was the standout, up 3.4 percent to about 386 dollars, after third-quarter earnings and sales beat expectations even though auto revenue declined, and after Elon Musk floated a SpaceX-Tesla merger. Analysts say Tesla shareholders could seek a 20 to 35 percent premium, with Musk potentially controlling up to 58 percent of voting rights. Nvidia rose 2.5 percent to about 222. Apple gained 1.3 percent to about 344. Meta added 0.5 percent to about 698, shrugging off a 25 billion dollar New Mexico privacy penalty and a raised 2026 capital spending forecast of 135 to 145 billion dollars. Microsoft was up 0.2 percent, Amazon was flat, and Alphabet fell 1.6 percent to about 335, even as OpenAI agreed to put its models on Google's cloud.

There's a cautionary thread underneath. CoreWeave is down 29 percent this month with its credit default swap spreads widening, HSBC's chief executive warned not every data centre is a good bet, and Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley have taken roughly 80 percent of equity trading revenue this year, 52 billion dollars, partly on margin lending to hedge funds. Why it matters: the AI trade is increasingly a credit story, not just an equity one.

On central banks, there was no Fed decision overnight. Nearly all economists in a Reuters poll expect the Fed to skip October and hike again in December, with housing data already flashing warning signs. Bank of America estimates each 1 percentage point of hikes costs the Treasury an extra 95 billion dollars a year in interest, which is why the White House is pushing Kevin Warsh to cut instead. The Bangko Sentral has not yet decided, but term deposit yields rose again as the market prices a hike, and former BSP officials plus GlobalSource are urging a jumbo 50 basis point move to defend credibility.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi rose 0.3 percent to close at 5,821.51, extending its recovery on bargain hunting following the GCash listing. But the peso weakened 0.5 percent to 63.33 to the dollar, its 34th record low of 2026. Why it matters: a currency making serial record lows feeds directly into imported fuel and food inflation, and strengthens the case for a larger BSP hike. Separately, former Finance Secretary commentary warns rate hikes alone won't solve inflation, pointing to supply-side fixes. On weather, PAGASA says Super Typhoon Rosal, international name Champi, entered the Philippine Area of Responsibility at 2 a.m. today, a risk to agriculture and power supply. In energy, the Marcos administration says it is open to selling the Bataan nuclear power plant, Nido Petroleum is committing up to 100 million dollars to drill new wells off Palawan, and Enrique Razon has consolidated his interest in a West Philippine Sea exploration firm. Finally, China says it allowed a Philippine resupply mission to its grounded warship, while the Chinese embassy trades barbs with Filipino lawmakers ahead of the ASEAN Summit in Manila on November 15 to 17.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 fell 1.1 percent to about 22,889 and the Sensex dropped 1.2 percent to roughly 73,853, a second straight decline, with around 8 lakh crore rupees wiped off market value in volatile trade. The trigger was crude oil pushing above 110 dollars a barrel, alongside foreign investor outflows and weak breadth; IT stocks outperformed. The rupee was the bright spot, firming 0.3 percent to 94.45 per dollar, a six-week high, helped by Jio IPO inflows and visible Reserve Bank support. On policy, RBI minutes flagged widening inflationary pressures and effectively put rate cuts on ice, with two members opposing the hawkish stance. Governor Sanjay Malhotra said future hikes depend on growth and inflation trends, and described the economy as resilient at 7.1 percent growth with inflation a red flag. The RBI also rejected all bids at a treasury bill auction as short-term yields surged, and the IMF urged more currency flexibility as the central bank defends the rupee. In corporate news, Persistent Systems opened a 4,265 crore rupee institutional placement at a floor price of 5,550 rupees a share.

Now to the world. Russian missiles hit Kyiv just hours after Moscow said it was ready in principle to resume peace talks, with President Trump saying the world is the closest it has been to ending the war. US envoy Steve Witkoff is set to meet Vladimir Putin while Trump presses Kyiv to settle. The BBC's Steve Rosenberg's assessment is blunter: Putin does not feel under pressure, so imminent peace is unlikely. Trump also denied ordering a Starlink slowdown in Ukraine, saying he doesn't know who did.

Energy is the market transmission channel. Ukrainian drone strikes have crippled Russian refining, the world's second-largest diesel source, and Trump is blaming Kyiv for surging diesel prices with US midterms two weeks away. Moscow has dispatched its first diesel shipment to the United States. Why it matters: diesel is the swing factor for freight, food and headline inflation from Manila to Mumbai.

In Britain, inflation accelerated to 3.5 percent in the year to September from 3.1 percent, and government borrowing hit its highest in a year, tightening the vise on Chancellor John Healey ahead of next week's Budget. In the United States, a New Mexico jury found Meta lied to Facebook users about data access, and a judge ordered a 25 billion dollar penalty, the largest ever against the company and bigger than its 18 billion dollar California settlement. In Mexico, former prosecutor César Jáuregui Moreno has been charged with treason for allegedly letting US agents join an April drug raid, a sovereignty flashpoint in US-Mexico relations. And France's Emmanuel Macron accused foreign leaders of exploiting French riots, an apparent swipe at Benjamin Netanyahu and Donald Trump.

That's your Morning Briefing for Thursday, October 22. Stay safe, and watch the typhoon track.
