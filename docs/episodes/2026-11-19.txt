Good morning. It's Thursday, November 19, 2026. Today: hawkish Fed minutes knock the Dow, Anthropic's record IPO hits the Nasdaq, and Trump, Xi and Putin meet in Shenzhen.

Start with Wall Street, where the split was sharp. The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at about 50,198, down 1.1 percent, a fall of more than 500 points. The S&P 500 finished near 7,324, down 0.2 percent. But the Nasdaq Composite rose 0.4 percent to roughly 24,430. The US 10-year Treasury yield eased 4 basis points to 5.07 percent.

Big tech did the lifting on the upside. Amazon jumped 3.8 percent to about 267.74, the standout among megacaps. Alphabet added 0.8 percent to 282.44, Tesla rose 1.0 percent to 383.72, Microsoft gained 0.4 percent to 508.29, and Apple edged up 0.2 percent to 352.45. Nvidia was the clear loser, down 2.9 percent to 150.96, still under pressure after Jensen Huang's exit. Interim chief executive Colette Kress used her first all-hands meeting to tell staff "let's move forward," and offered pay raises. Jim Cramer argues Anthropic's listing is a fresh headwind for Nvidia. Meta slipped 0.4 percent to 616.38. Worth noting, Microsoft is down nearly 9 percent from its November 4 record after the Outlook outage Satya Nadella called a "perfect storm."

Now central banks. Minutes from the Fed's October 27 to 28 meeting, where rates were held at 3.75 to 4 percent, showed almost all officials expect further hikes, with some open to a half-point move. Governor Christopher Waller said the Fed may need to "shock people" with a 50 basis point increase, and Michael Barr said rate rises likely lie ahead. Officials also flagged artificial intelligence's effect on inflation. Markets now price roughly a 90 percent chance of a 25 basis point hike in December. The dollar is near a 19-month high, and gold bounced 0.6 percent off a one-year low. Why it matters: the world's benchmark rate is heading up, not down, and that tightens conditions from Manila to Mumbai.

No rate decision from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, but it has proposed Basel III-aligned risk weights: lower capital charges on qualified consumer and small-business loans, heavier requirements on riskier property and project finance.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi closed at about 5,488, down 0.4 percent, snapping a two-day rebound that had carried it to 5,512. The peso weakened 0.18 percent to 63.63 to the dollar. Politically, a heavy week: President Trump's Manila state visit produced what both sides call the biggest trade deal yet between the two countries, and the White House says it is eyeing 2 billion dollars in foreign military financing for the Philippines, with 400 million earmarked for operational readiness and South China Sea deterrence. Marcos said no Pax Silica agreement was signed during the visit. The ASEAN summit Manila chaired closed without a South China Sea Code of Conduct, despite Manila's pledge to deliver it this year; why it matters, the security premium on Philippine assets is not going away. Marcos has flown to Shenzhen for APEC. Also watch the 60-day pork import ban, which the US Department of Agriculture says could squeeze supply by early 2027, and BSP's plan to tighten rules on money service businesses, which touches remittances.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed at about 25,228, down 0.6 percent, and the Sensex at roughly 82,032, also down 0.6 percent, snapping a two-day rally as auto and metal stocks led profit-taking. The rupee was essentially flat at 93.44 to the dollar. GIFT Nifty at about 25,162 points to another soft open today on the hawkish Fed read-across. On the Reserve Bank: Goldman Sachs now expects 75 basis points of Indian rate hikes by the second quarter of calendar 2027. The RBI says inflows under its foreign exchange swap facility have reached 72.85 billion dollars, and it has told lenders to tighten API security as cyberattack risk rises. It also warned that the Yes Bank AT1 ruling could weaken future bank rescues. The core tension: inflation has risen for 12 straight months, a threat both to growth and to the foreign inflows behind the recent recovery, even as economists see July-to-September GDP growth near 7.6 percent.

Now the world. The biggest market story is Anthropic. The Claude maker priced its IPO at 83 dollars a share, raising 81.3 billion dollars, the largest first-time share sale on record, topping SpaceX. It begins trading on the Nasdaq today under the ticker ANT, at a valuation reported as high as 1.5 trillion dollars. Why it matters: it is the cleanest test yet of whether the AI trade still has buyers at these prices.

In Shenzhen, Trump, Xi and Putin are set for a rare trilateral meeting on the sidelines of APEC. Xi opened the summit calling for lower trade and investment barriers and pledging broader access to China's market and manufacturing sector. Putin used his speech to argue the EU is losing industry and to talk up Russia's resilience under sanctions. South Korean media, gaming and travel stocks rallied on hopes of wider China access under a trade deal.

Ukraine struck the Crimean bridge again, crippling the road link and forcing Russian officials to suspend traffic, timing that lands right before Putin's meetings. The fighting has also moved underground, with Ukrainian and Russian troops battling inside a Soviet-era gas tunnel in the Kharkiv region, below the reach of drones.

In the Middle East, the Israeli military launched a rare ground operation in Gaza beyond the Yellow Line, saying it was dismantling a terror tunnel. Hamas calls it a flagrant ceasefire violation. Separately, at least 8 Palestinians were injured in central Israel when a settler rammed a vehicle into people at several locations.

At The Hague, the International Court of Justice rules today on The Gambia's genocide case against Myanmar over the 2017 military crackdown that displaced at least 730,000 Rohingya. No state has ever been found guilty of genocide by an international court, so this is precedent-setting.

Two quick ones. US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth unveiled a new military deal with Venezuela on his first visit since the US capture of Nicolas Maduro. And in chips and cyber, Intel has signed its first external customer for its advanced 18A process, while Palo Alto Networks is buying AI startup Applied Compute for 4.5 billion dollars, with its shares up 7 percent on earnings.
