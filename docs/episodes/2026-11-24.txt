Good morning. It's Tuesday, November 24, 2026, and this is your Morning Briefing on finance and geopolitics. One story is driving almost everything today: Jensen Huang is back at Nvidia, and markets have exploded higher.

Start with Wall Street. The Dow closed at about 49,595, up 3.0 percent. The S&P 500 finished near 7,237, up 4.7 percent. The Nasdaq Composite closed around 24,090, up 6.5 percent. The US 10-year Treasury yield was 4.92 percent, up 1 basis point.

The trigger was Nvidia's board reinstating Jensen Huang as chief executive, days after he resigned amid a scandal over an extramarital affair. Nvidia shares jumped 31.5 percent to 165.05, adding close to 1 trillion dollars in market value in a single session. President Trump says he personally sealed the deal, telling Nvidia's board his administration would give assurances that Huang was not a national security threat. That claim has drawn a political backlash, and analysts are now openly questioning Nvidia's governance, calling Huang "too big to fire."

The rest of big tech followed. Tesla rose 11.5 percent to 385.46. Meta gained 6.6 percent to 597.60. Alphabet added 6.3 percent to 282.10. Amazon rose 3.5 percent to 255.39. Apple gained 1.9 percent to 349.08, and Microsoft 1.7 percent to 492.50. Asia joined in: South Korea's Kospi surged more than 7 percent, Japan's Nikkei about 4.5 percent, and Taiwanese chip names traded near their daily price limits.

Why it matters: this reverses a chunk of last week's tech-led rout, but strategists only call it a bottom "for now."

On central banks, New York Fed President John Williams said inflation is still too high and signalled that higher rates are likely over time, with inflation nearing 2 percent only in 2027. Rate-hike odds jumped, one bank economist is warning of three consecutive hikes starting next month, and the dollar is near a 20-month high. Bill Ackman says the Fed is "about to make another mistake."

Here at home, Bangko Sentral Governor Remolona says weak growth is unlikely to stop further rate hikes and is urging fiscal stimulus instead, though the BSP has signalled a less aggressive pace. S&P expects another 50 basis points of tightening, and some analysts see the hiking cycle running into 2027.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi closed at about 5,426, up 1.4 percent, rebounding on bargain hunting after Friday's record-low close. The Philippine Stock Exchange's message to investors was "don't panic, it's organic." The peso finished at 63.73 to the dollar, with the dollar down 0.26 percent on thin pre-US-data volume.

Three things to watch. First, the BSP has proposed new rules allowing non-bank companies to initiate fund transfers directly from customers' bank accounts, opening digital payments to more providers. Why it matters: it's a structural shot at the banks' grip on payment rails. Second, total resources of the Philippine financial system rose 8.6 percent to 38.99 trillion pesos as of end-September, a sign balance sheets are still expanding despite higher rates. Third, in corporate news, RCR will acquire Robinsons Manila in a 20-billion-peso share swap. And for households, another round of diesel and gasoline rollbacks takes effect this week, with price monitors pointing to further cuts for November 24 to 30 on soft demand. Politically, Senator Marcoleta's bail plea has been denied.

Turning to India. Indian markets are closed today for Guru Nanak Jayanti, so Monday's closes stand. The Sensex fell 1,484 points, or 1.8 percent, to about 82,436. The Nifty 50 dropped 1.8 percent to 25,350, dragged down by banking and metal stocks on weak global cues. The rupee weakened to 92.90 to the dollar, with the dollar up 0.24 percent.

Note the divergence: India sold off on the same day the AI trade roared back. Analysts warn that Huang's return could pull foreign money out of Indian equities and back into AI names, after a 1.3 trillion rupee wave of foreign inflows helped lift the Sensex nearly 12 percent this month.

From the Reserve Bank, two signals. It has proposed new safeguards against digital fraud, including a "trusted approver" mechanism and a payments "stop switch." And Indian corporates rushed to raise 2.7 billion dollars of debt ahead of an expected RBI rate hike. On the economy, S&P Global projects 7 percent growth in both FY27 and FY28, with manufacturing carrying the load as services lose steam. Separately, a US panel says China has shifted tactics and is now trying to contain India economically rather than militarily. And India is testing samples from every bottled water company after a Bisleri batch was ruled unsafe.

Now to the world. In the Strait of Hormuz, Iran says three oil tankers caught fire after hitting naval mines, despite US naval escorts, as the Treasury widens sanctions aimed at Tehran's financial lifelines. Why it matters: that's the world's most important oil chokepoint, yet crude has stayed soft enough to push Japanese 10-year yields to a two-week low of 2.715 percent and to keep delivering fuel price cuts in Manila. Watch for that gap to close.

On Russia and NATO, the NATO secretary general has vowed to defend against space threats after German media reported Russia plans to launch a satellite carrying a nuclear warhead within weeks. Separately, Ukraine sank a Russian battleship at a port in Libya, pushing the war well beyond its traditional theatre.

On tech and security, the United States has added Megaspeed and six other companies to its trade blacklist over concerns about diversion of advanced computing products to China. Megaspeed surfaced through the Nvidia scandal. Why it matters: Washington is tightening chip export enforcement even as it props up Nvidia's leadership. Meanwhile, UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres is calling for a global agreement on AI oversight within a year.

Elsewhere, the UK has sanctioned top Myanmar military leaders, joined by several other nations, after the International Court of Justice ruled Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya. Oxfam says Israeli attacks have left signs of contamination across almost all of Gaza, calling it a toxic wasteland. A car bomb struck the governor's office in Villavicencio, Colombia. And Pennsylvania has reported a ninth measles death in an outbreak now spanning 46 counties.

Two corporate items worth your attention. In London, Capita shares plunged 70 percent and its chief executive quit after a civil service pensions failure delayed payments to public sector workers. And in the US, Amazon faces an FAA probe after a delivery drone struck a fibre cable and a residential garage in Michigan and became entangled in power lines during takeoff. Bitcoin, meanwhile, failed to hold its Nvidia bump, slipping about 1 percent amid ETF outflows and Binance hack fallout.

That's your briefing. Trade carefully, and have a productive Tuesday.
