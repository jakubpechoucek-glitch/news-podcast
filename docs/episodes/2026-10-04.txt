Good morning. It's Sunday, October 4, 2026. Here's your Morning Briefing on finance and geopolitics: a strong Friday finish on Wall Street as a soft US jobs report cooled rate-hike bets, a G7 move to release oil and diesel, and Brazil votes today.

To markets. US stocks closed the week broadly higher. The Dow Jones Industrial Average finished at about 51,177, up 0.5 percent. The S&P 500 closed at roughly 7,723, up 0.7 percent. The Nasdaq Composite led, ending near 27,191, up 1.2 percent. But the bond market didn't echo the optimism: the US 10-year Treasury yield rose 4 basis points to 5.28 percent. Why it matters: equities are pricing relief on rates while the long end still demands a high premium, and that tension is the main risk to the rally.

Big tech did the heavy lifting. Tesla was the standout, up 4.7 percent to about 370.59 dollars after a better-than-expected quarterly vehicle deliveries report, though its core auto business faces pressure from cheaper Chinese and European electric vehicles, and its Cybercab robotaxi had a rocky first month in Austin. Alphabet rose 1.6 percent to about 343.50. Nvidia gained 1.3 percent to roughly 233.95, and Amazon added 1.3 percent to about 251.52. Apple was up 1.0 percent at about 333.69, Microsoft up 0.9 percent at roughly 517.53, and Meta lagged with a 0.3 percent gain to about 728.08, as it rolled out Muse, an agent that can shop and check out for you with Walmart, Gap, Sephora, Expedia and others. Not everyone shared the lift: Cerebras hit a post-IPO low, down 20 percent on the week on Nvidia competitive pressure and a lockup expiration. Why it matters: the AI trade is narrowing to the giants.

Now central banks. The Federal Reserve is the week's pivot. US employers paused hiring and unemployment ticked up in a sharp labour market slowdown, just a month before the midterm elections. Traders responded by slashing the odds of a Fed rate hike this month, and markets now expect an October skip, against expectations the Fed would keep tightening despite President Trump's objections. Fed minutes, unemployment data and a consumer sentiment update are due in the week ahead. Why it matters: the Fed is tightening into a cooling jobs market, and every data point now decides whether that continues.

On the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, no decision yet, but economists say the BSP is tipped to end its rate-hiking cycle at its October meeting. The complication: BPI warns Philippine inflation may breach 7 percent in the coming months. Separately, the BSP signalled it is unimpressed with RCBC's move to make InstaPay transfers free with conditions attached. Why it matters: the BSP is being asked to stop hiking just as price pressures peak.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi closed essentially flat at about 5,629, down 0.01 percent, after a run of losses that took the index to an 11-month low, with the Middle East conflict and fuel costs weighing on sentiment. The peso firmed 0.15 percent to 62.58 to the dollar. The biggest domestic story is energy: the government has declared a national energy emergency as Asia faces a supply crunch tied to the Iran war, and another gasoline price increase is expected on October 6. Why it matters: fuel is the main channel pushing local inflation toward that 7 percent warning line. In corporate news, GCash parent Mynt set its final IPO price at 6.60 pesos per share, well below the 10-peso indicative level, prompting debate over whether that's genuinely a bargain. And Toshiba's planned expansion of hard disk drive manufacturing here is projected to create 3,000 jobs, reinforcing the country's place in the global data storage supply chain. The Development Bank of the Philippines is also urging retail investors into the latest retail Treasury bond tranche, which offers a shorter tenor and higher rate.

Now to the world. Energy is the dominant thread. The G7 has agreed to a coordinated release of millions of barrels of oil and diesel after a threat from President Trump, aimed at heading off further price spikes and avoiding a US ban on diesel exports. European Union countries are heading into crisis talks on soaring diesel prices, with officials warning a US export ban would damage Europe's economic outlook. In the UK, diesel has topped 2 pounds a litre for the first time, and suppliers are pressing the government for immediate help with winter energy bills. Why it matters: diesel is the fuel of freight and farming, so this feeds straight into global goods inflation, including here.

Brazil votes today in the first round of its presidential election, with Lula da Silva and Jair Bolsonaro clashing over sovereignty, security and ties with Washington, and Brazil accusing the United States of election interference. Wall Street is braced for two wildly different market outcomes. Why it matters: it's the biggest emerging-market risk event of the quarter.

In Ukraine, Russia struck the Pivnichnyi, or Northern, Bridge in Kyiv, the second major bridge in the capital hit after repeated attacks on another crossing. Why it matters: targeting civilian infrastructure in Kyiv signals an escalation heading into winter.

In the Gulf, the UAE prosecutor general says a Flydubai co-pilot attacked the captain with a crash axe and tried to take control of an Israel-bound jet, calling it an attempted terrorist act. Why it matters: aviation security scrutiny on Gulf carriers is about to tighten.

And in Spain, tens of thousands joined more than 50 protests over the housing crisis after the government failed to pass emergency legislation. Meanwhile, South Korea is resisting Trump's push to join the Alaska LNG project, citing high costs and commercial risk, and community pushback against AI data centres is spreading from the United States into Europe and Asia.

That's your Morning Briefing for Sunday, October 4. Trade carefully, and enjoy your Sunday.
