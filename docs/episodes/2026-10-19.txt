Good morning. It's Monday, October 19, 2026, and here's your Morning Briefing on finance and geopolitics. Today: a tech-led slide on Wall Street, a BSP rate decision in view, and widening fallout from Washington's Russia policy shift.

Start with markets. Wall Street closed lower, and the damage was concentrated in technology. The Dow Jones Industrial Average finished at about 52,002, down 0.3 percent. The S&P 500 ended near 7,659, down 1.1 percent. The Nasdaq Composite took the hardest hit, closing around 26,140, down 2.2 percent. The US 10-year Treasury yield settled at 5.10 percent, up 1 basis point, keeping borrowing costs at levels that are squeezing equity valuations worldwide.

Among the megacaps, Meta was the worst performer, down 3.7 percent at about 709 dollars. Alphabet fell 2.5 percent to roughly 341. Tesla lost 2.1 percent to about 355. Microsoft slipped 1.9 percent to around 529, Amazon fell 1.9 percent to 250 even, and Nvidia eased 1.4 percent to about 214. Apple was the lone gainer, up 0.4 percent at roughly 343. The driver, according to the reporting, is a sinking artificial intelligence trade colliding with hotter inflation data, with investors rotating out of crowded tech names and into banks, where earnings have been solid. Jefferies strategist Chris Wood is telling clients to diversify beyond AI, arguing earnings growth for global AI stocks will slow sharply while valuations stay stretched. One counterpoint: South Korea's finance minister says the semiconductor market should stay solid at least through next year, because supply remains tight despite the chip sell-off. Why it matters: the AI trade is no longer a one-way bet, and the rotation is what's doing the damage to index levels.

On central banks, there is no fresh Federal Reserve decision, but the commentary is hawkish. Analysts are split on whether the Fed pauses in October, with a December hike still firmly on the table and some arguing more tightening is unavoidable even if officials hold this month. Hiring came in worse than expected alongside elevated inflation, which sharpens the trade-off. Separately, the Bank of England's deputy governor warned that hedge fund activity raises the risk of a global bond market shock. For the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, a rate decision lands this week, and polls point to another hike as near certain, with some forecasters flagging 50 basis points because of the weak peso. The BSP also reported net income of 170.2 billion pesos in the first seven months of 2026, more than double a year earlier, on surging foreign exchange gains and lower interest expenses.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi was the standout, closing at about 5,783, up 2.1 percent, and analysts expect it to test 5,800 resistance this week. The peso was essentially flat but soft at 62.95 to the dollar. Two local catalysts dominate: the BSP policy decision and the market debut of Mynt, the parent of GCash, with analysts seeing steady pre-IPO growth in the business. Also in the pipeline, PLDT's 24.2 billion peso data centre REIT, the first of its kind here, and a rebalancing of the MSCI Standard Philippine Index that could move passive flows. Meralco shares rose after the Energy Regulatory Commission approved a rate increase. On policy, a business leader is urging the Senate to wrap up the Vice President's trial by year-end to clear political overhang, while analysts are pressing for reforms to curb political patronage in cash aid after the flood-control scandal. The World Bank says the Philippines needs a productivity boost to hold upper-middle-income status. And Manila and Washington are exploring an oil stockpiling deal. Why it matters: a hawkish BSP plus a weak peso is the key variable for local equities this week.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed at about 23,146, up 0.7 percent, and the BSE Sensex at roughly 74,729, up 0.6 percent, capping the strongest weekly gain since April. The rupee weakened 0.45 percent to 94.95 to the dollar. The Reserve Bank of India has lifted the repo rate to 5.50 percent, and former governor Subbarao warned that defending the rupee against fundamentals is ultimately futile. Foreign portfolio investors have pulled 52,100 crore rupees out of Indian equities so far in October, more than September's outflow, blamed on high US yields, elevated crude and a stronger dollar. Domestic institutions are absorbing it: nine of the ten most valued firms added 3.65 lakh crore rupees in market value last week, led by TCS, Bharti Airtel and Infosys. Earnings are thick today, with 35 companies reporting including Bajaj Finance, where profit is seen up 29 percent, plus Hindustan Zinc, UltraTech Cement and IREDA. The Reliance Jio IPO opens October 21, and markets are shut Tuesday for the holiday. Why it matters: domestic money is currently outgunning a record foreign exodus.

Now the world. The biggest story for markets is Washington's pivot on Russia. President Trump has temporarily waived many of the sanctions on Moscow recently passed by Congress, citing national security, as his dispute with President Zelensky intensifies. Treasury Secretary Bessent warned Zelensky to be very careful about claims the United States is throttling Starlink, which is critical to Ukraine's defence. Analysts say Trump's Russia diesel deal may deliver Putin more leverage than cash, while eroding Ukraine's negotiating position. Meanwhile, overnight strikes hit oil facilities in St Petersburg, killing at least one person and damaging a data centre. Why it matters: Russian energy infrastructure and sanctions relief are now pulling oil prices in opposite directions.

Second, Iran. The secretary of its Supreme National Security Council says the military has a new operational plan ready if the United States resumes attacks, and shipping vessels are reporting strikes. That's a direct freight and crude risk premium story.

Third, Europe's streets. Macron addressed France after nationwide rioting and looting, speaking of anger and shame and promising more security, but stopping short of a state of emergency or curfew. In Albania, anti-government protests entered a fifth day, with offices at the Supreme Court in Tirana set ablaze and violent clashes in the capital. The common thread is youth-led unrest spreading across the continent, a political risk investors have largely ignored so far.

Also worth noting: European diplomats were attacked by Israeli settlers in the occupied West Bank, with the military condemning the violence while the prime minister's office called it part of a smear campaign. At least four people died in a major fire at a Canadian military base. Alberta's independence referendum looks set for defeat, though separatists are unlikely to fold. And Vietnam signed a free trade deal with four European nations, adding to regional competition for export market access.

That's your briefing for Monday, October 19. Have a productive week.
