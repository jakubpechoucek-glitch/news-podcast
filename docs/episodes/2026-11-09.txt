Good morning. It's Monday, November 9, 2026. Today: Wall Street splits, with Nvidia rallying even as its chief executive faces a spy probe; Philippine dollar reserves hit a four-year low as jumbo rate-hike bets build; and Houthi missiles strike the heart of Riyadh's financial district.

To the markets. The Dow Jones closed at about 51,286, down 0.5 percent. The S&P 500 finished at roughly 7,377, up 0.3 percent. The Nasdaq Composite led, closing near 24,580, up 1.1 percent. The split tells the story: chips and growth up, old-economy industrials down. A gauge of US semiconductor shares jumped 4.2 percent on Friday as speculation about tighter Democratic regulation of the artificial intelligence industry eased after the midterms. Why it matters: the AI trade is still the single biggest driver of global equity direction, including for Asian supplier stocks.

The US 10-year Treasury yield rose 2 basis points to 4.86 percent. Why it matters: yields near 4.9 percent keep a lid on equity valuations and keep pressure on emerging-market currencies, the peso included.

Among the big names, Tesla was the standout, up 4.2 percent to about 358 dollars. Nvidia rose 2.8 percent to roughly 187 dollars, despite its board opening an investigation into chief executive Jensen Huang's relationship with a woman alleged to be a Chinese intelligence asset. Huang admitted an affair in an email to staff and says he'll cooperate; lawmakers from both parties now want to question him. Why it matters: Nvidia is the most systemically important stock in the world, and a governance or China-security shock there moves every index you own.

Meta gained 0.8 percent to about 610, even as chief strategy officer Dave Wehner left to become finance chief at AI infrastructure startup Fireworks, valued at 17.5 billion dollars in July. Alphabet rose 0.8 percent to roughly 285. On the downside, Microsoft fell 1 percent to about 531, Apple slipped 0.5 percent to 342, and Amazon eased 0.4 percent to 259. Separately, Berkshire Hathaway said it more than doubled share buybacks in the third quarter as operating and overall profits fell.

On central banks: the Federal Reserve has raised interest rates for the first time since 2023, with hot consumer price inflation sealing the move, and firm jobs data is now fanning bets on another hike in December. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has been talking down the idea that the AI boom insulates the economy from policy, saying interest rates "get in the cracks." The Fed's balance sheet, at 6.75 trillion dollars, is growing again. Why it matters: a hiking Fed means a strong dollar and a higher global cost of capital into 2027.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi last closed at about 5,762. The peso weakened 0.57 percent to 63.10 to the dollar, and traders expect it to hold around 63 after this week's key US data. Why it matters: a weak peso is now showing up in corporate results, with local telcos booking nearly 18 billion pesos in foreign exchange losses.

At the Bangko Sentral, there's no fresh decision, but hot October inflation has fuelled talk of another jumbo rate hike, and economists say cuts are being pushed further out. Yields on BSP bills rose last week despite oversubscription. Gross international reserves fell to 97.4 billion dollars in October, the lowest since 2022. The BSP says banks remain resilient despite rising non-performing loans, while the Bankers Association warns loan growth will slow as weak activity and high rates squeeze borrowers.

This week's swing factor is third-quarter GDP, with corporate earnings alongside it. Brokers expect the index to move in a tight range; analysts warn weaker growth and high inflation threaten the rally, though holiday spending should lift fourth-quarter GDP. Firms are already bracing for tougher trading after Christmas. One bright spot: Monde Nissin shares climbed on stronger earnings and a higher dividend. And watch the weather: Typhoon Cempaka was outside the Philippine Area of Responsibility at 2 a.m. and is expected to enter today.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed at about 24,986, down 0.18 percent, and the Sensex at roughly 80,999, essentially flat, down 0.04 percent. That was the Diwali Muhurat session, and it ended in the red for the first time since 2017, snapping both a seven-day winning run and an eight-year Muhurat streak, with banking stocks the main drag. Context matters, though: the Nifty had surged 8.5 percent in the previous week, so this is profit-taking, not a reversal. The rupee strengthened 0.6 percent to 93.89 to the dollar, helped by softer crude. GIFT Nifty was pointing to a positive open this morning.

From the Reserve Bank, no policy move, but the central bank publicly dismissed as fake viral claims that new cheque book rules take effect in January 2027, and said its Technical Consultative Committee will focus on critical policy and operational matters. NSE chief Ashishkumar Chauhan says foreign capital is returning and growth is accelerating, with industry voices projecting above 7 percent expansion. On corporates, Bosch approved a 1-for-5 stock split and a 3-for-1 bonus issue, turning one share into 20, and Exide beat Amara Raja with a 54 percent profit jump. Why it matters: cheaper oil plus a firmer rupee is the cleanest bullish combination India has had in months.

Now to the world. In the Gulf, Houthi forces struck the Aramco Tower in central Riyadh, with footage authenticated by BBC Verify showing damage in the Saudi capital's financial district. Separately, a drone hit the Khor Mor gas facility, leaving more than 70 percent of northern Iraq's Kurdish region without power. President Trump says renewed US strikes on Iran are "highly unlikely" if nuclear and Strait of Hormuz talks progress, but warns he could still strike, and says an "Iron Gate" posture is in effect. Why it matters: despite attacks on energy infrastructure, crude has softened, which is capping gold and easing global inflation fears.

In Washington, Democrats are mapping a post-midterm agenda. Likely next House speaker Hakeem Jeffries has made ending Trump's tariffs a top priority and backs impeachment proceedings against Defense Secretary Hegseth, while Deputy Attorney General Blanche counters that he may investigate lawmakers. Why it matters: tariff rollback is the biggest potential upside risk for Asian exporters, and healthcare stocks are already struggling for direction ahead of the new Congress.

Elsewhere, at least seven people died and more than 100 were injured when two trains collided outside Giza in Egypt. Iran executed three men over alleged armed unrest linked to January's protests. Paraguay's former president Santiago Peña has been charged after the banking collapse that ousted him and froze millions of accounts. Germany's Chancellor Merz will nominate Ilse Aigner as the country's first woman president. And New Zealand delivered its tightest election in years amid an unusually sour national mood.

That's your Morning Briefing. Trade carefully, and have a productive Monday.
