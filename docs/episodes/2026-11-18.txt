Good morning. It's Wednesday, November 18, 2026, and this is your Morning Briefing on Finance and Geopolitics. Today: Wall Street slides as Fed officials talk about more hikes, Nvidia delivers without Jensen Huang, and Trump, Xi and Putin converge on Shenzhen.

Start with markets. US stocks closed broadly lower. The Dow Jones finished at about 50,759, down 1.6 percent. The S&P 500 closed at about 7,341, down 1.0 percent. The Nasdaq Composite held up best, at about 24,328, down 0.7 percent. The US 10-year Treasury yield eased 2 basis points to 5.10 percent, but it remains above 5 percent, a level Bloomberg calls a historic run driven by the Fed's tightening cycle. The dollar is sitting near a 19-month high, and gold has fallen to its lowest since October 2025. Why it matters: with cash yielding above 5 percent, equity valuations have no cushion.

In big tech, Microsoft was the heaviest drag, down 2.3 percent to about 506 dollars. Alphabet fell 1.2 percent to about 280, Meta fell 1.2 percent to about 619, Nvidia slipped 0.4 percent to about 155 in regular trade, and Apple eased 0.3 percent to about 352. Amazon and Tesla were effectively flat, at about 258 and 380 dollars. The action was after the bell: Nvidia beat estimates and guided to roughly 130 billion dollars of revenue for the fourth quarter, its first results since Jensen Huang's resignation, and the shares gained more than 4 percent in extended trading. The board chair said the company does not intend to reinstate Huang; Huang says he would be honoured to return if asked. Interim chief Colette Kress says employees and customers are staying put.

To central banks. The Federal Reserve has held rates steady with inflation at a 3-year high, but the message is hawkish: St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem says several more hikes — as many as four — may be needed before spring 2027. New Chairman Kevin Warsh stressed AI's importance to the economy while warning it could disappoint. Fed minutes are the next catalyst. There was no Bangko Sentral rate decision overnight. The live BSP story is regulatory: the central bank is moving to tighten capital and licensing rules for money service businesses and remittance firms. On policy, the split is stark — BPI argues BSP must keep tightening to contain inflation, while other forecasters see the Philippines leading Asia in rate cuts next year.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi ended its losing streak, closing at about 5,511, up 0.2 percent, after touching its lowest level since the pandemic. Bargain hunting in BDO and BPI did the rescuing. The peso strengthened 0.5 percent to 63.55 to the dollar. Why it matters: the index is near six-year lows, so this is stabilisation, not a turn. Politically, the big event is done — President Marcos has handed the ASEAN chairship to Singapore after the 49th summit, claiming more than 100 outcomes on trade and security, and co-chairing the ASEAN–US Special Summit with President Trump in Manila. Protests grew in the capital around the visit. Two regional deliverables matter for business: ASEAN members green-lit a 764-billion-dollar power grid plan, and the bloc is targeting a 2027 rollout of its digital trade pact. On the data, September remittances grew at their fastest pace in 8 months, a key peso support. Socioeconomic Planning chief Arsenio Balisacan flagged costly infrastructure delays, a new round of fuel price cuts took effect November 17, and Japan and the Philippines are set to sign an oil reserve cooperation memorandum.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed at about 25,387, up 0.4 percent, and the Sensex at about 82,532, up 0.5 percent — a 9-month high, led by banking stocks and helped by softer crude and record foreign inflows. The rupee firmed 0.5 percent to 93.46 to the dollar, and Reuters reports it is poised to extend its outperformance on swelling inflows after breaking a key technical level. Caution for today's open, though: GIFT Nifty was down more than 100 points, pointing to a weak start despite Nvidia's beat, and analysts flag resistance near 25,500 with narrowing market breadth. On the Reserve Bank of India, there's no decision, but Bloomberg frames it as having reasons to hike and reasons to wait — inflation pressure against a strengthening currency. Operationally, the RBI has issued its Domestic Money Transfer Directions for 2026, letting walk-in customers send up to 50,000 rupees by NEFT, and it has put a Tata Sons listing back on the table, telling the group to follow the rules. Why it matters: a forced listing of India's largest conglomerate holding company would be a landmark supply event for the market.

Now to the world. In Ukraine, part of the Crimean Bridge has collapsed after what Kyiv calls an unprecedented seven-stage missile and drone attack, weeks in the planning. Russia retaliated by striking bridges in Kyiv. Separately, a Russian military plane crashed in the Kemerovo region killing all 19 on board, with a technical malfunction the preliminary cause. And President Zelenskyy says Starshield satellite speed curbs over occupied territories have now been in place for 7 days. Why it matters: the war's logistics backbone on both sides is now the target.

The diplomatic set-piece is in Shenzhen, where Trump, Xi and Putin are meeting alongside the APEC leaders' summit — Trump's third meeting with Xi this year. Xi has called for removing trade and investment barriers ahead of it. Hanging over it is the Nvidia spy scandal, which Beijing fears will trigger tighter US chip export restrictions, leaving companies from Nvidia to Apple with a sharper China dilemma.

In commodities, US crude has declined to 85 dollars a barrel as Middle East exports recover and forecasters project a fourth-quarter surplus. That is disinflationary for oil importers like the Philippines and India.

Three more to watch. Widespread tech outages hit banks, air travel, the NSA and Apple — JPMorgan blamed a temporary software bug in consumer banking, with no sign of bad actors, and the FAA has paused new router installations after Friday's rare nationwide ground stop. Anthropic lists on the Nasdaq tomorrow under the ticker ANT, with pricing guided at or above the 70-to-78-dollar range and demand reportedly seven times supply; Amazon and Google will each own more than 14 percent. And JPMorgan warns leveraged-loan defaults will more than double to 4.5 percent in 2027 — the credit tell to watch if rates keep rising. One data point in Asia's favour: Japan's October exports surged 20.5 percent, with semiconductor shipments up almost 50 percent year on year.
