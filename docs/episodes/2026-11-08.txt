Good morning, it's Sunday, November 8, 2026. Here's your Morning Briefing on finance and geopolitics: a split Wall Street as the AI trade tries to steady itself, fresh Fed hike talk after a strong jobs report, a weaker peso, India's best week since 2021, and US strikes on Iran.

To the markets. Wall Street closed mixed. The Dow Jones finished at about 51,286, down 0.5 percent. The S&P 500 closed at roughly 7,377, up 0.3 percent. The Nasdaq Composite led, at about 24,579, up 1.1 percent. That bounce follows a brutal stretch in which, by one estimate, 5.7 trillion dollars was wiped off market values as AI bets unwound, and the Nasdaq slipped into correction territory. The trigger was political: investors read the Democratic sweep in the US midterms as a signal of tighter AI regulation and local resistance to new data centres. Why it matters: the AI trade is now a policy trade, not just an earnings trade.

In big tech, Tesla was the standout, up 4.2 percent to about 358 dollars, after Elon Musk appeared to signal the end of the Cybertruck to free up capacity for Cybercabs, its driverless taxis. Nvidia rose 2.8 percent to roughly 187 dollars. Meta added 0.8 percent to about 610, even as chief strategy officer Dave Wehner left to become finance chief at AI infrastructure startup Fireworks. Alphabet gained 0.8 percent. On the downside, Microsoft fell 1 percent, Apple slipped 0.5 percent and Amazon lost 0.4 percent. Watch the credit side too: CoreWeave's bond yield has topped 16 percent, a sign of stress in AI debt markets, and investors now call Anthropic's hoped-for 1.5 trillion dollar IPO valuation a stretch. Berkshire Hathaway, meanwhile, more than doubled buybacks in the third quarter as profits fell.

Bonds and central banks. The US 10-year Treasury yield rose 2 basis points to 4.86 percent. Fed policymakers are openly discussing a rate hike after payrolls rose 165,000, far above forecasts; market-implied odds of a hike next month are near 80 percent. San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly says Americans are on a treadmill of high inflation and more hikes are likely; New York's John Williams says inflation is simply too high. The dollar jumped and gold fell on that data. From the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, no rate decision this weekend, but the BSP reported bank lending and domestic liquidity grew in September, said banks kept strong buffers in the first half despite rising non-performing loans, and urged central banks to study how AI affects inflation.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi ended at about 5,762, down 0.3 percent, on profit-taking and macro headwinds. The peso weakened 0.45 percent to 63.03 to the dollar, slipping back through the 63 line as the dollar rallied on US jobs data. Why it matters: a weaker peso raises import and fuel costs just as inflation forecasts are being revised up. On that, Deutsche Bank has raised its 2026 Philippine inflation forecast to 5.9 percent. Growth is the other half of the story: a poll of economists sees third-quarter GDP accelerating to 2.8 percent on firmer spending, after second-quarter growth was revised to 2.4 percent, and manufacturing output picked up in September. But agriculture is a soft spot, with the farm trade deficit widening almost 25 percent in September on a double-digit drop in exports. In corporate and policy news, the Philippine Ports Authority booked 10.6 billion pesos in profit through September, pointing to another record dividend to the Treasury, while the franchising board hit ride-hailing app Move It with a 555.75-million-peso fine for onboarding riders beyond the authorized cap. Finance Secretary Recto is checking final preparations as the Philippines readies to host ASEAN leaders.

Turning to India. Indian equities ripped higher. The Nifty 50 closed at about 25,030, up 2 percent, and the BSE Sensex at roughly 81,028, up 2.2 percent. For the week, the Sensex gained 8.82 percent and the Nifty 8.58 percent, the best weekly performance since 2021, adding some 28.7 lakh crore rupees in market value, with the rally driven by relief over the US midterm outcome. The rupee strengthened 0.58 percent to 93.91 to the dollar. Why it matters: foreign inflows are back, but the gains are narrow. Analysts note heavyweights are doing the work while mid-, small- and micro-caps lag, and technicians flag the 25,000 to 25,200 zone as the next test, with some advising partial profit-booking. Markets hold a special Diwali Muhurat session today. From the Reserve Bank of India, no policy change, but a busy regulatory weekend: proposed tighter verification for gold-backed agriculture loans and new priority-sector lending calculations, consolidated cybersecurity rules that could raise costs for non-bank payment firms, and a flat denial of viral claims about new cheque book rules. The governor also said there's no plan to end the FCNR-B incentive scheme early. RBI data show Indian companies' overseas investments grew 41 percent in the last fiscal year, outpacing inward FDI.

Now to the world. The biggest story is Iran. President Trump says the United States carried out what he called minor strikes on Iran, after Iranian state media reported missiles hitting Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, plus Jask, Sirik and Bandar Abbas. Washington is also moving to block land supply routes and has offered a 20 million dollar reward for information on Iran's nuclear programme. Why it matters: strikes inside the Strait of Hormuz put the world's most important oil chokepoint back in play, and analysts say escalation risk stays high regardless of the US election result.

In Poland, six people were killed in a random shooting in Krakow's main square, with three more bodies found at the suspect's home. Prime Minister Donald Tusk called it an act of terrorism. It lands amid wider European jitters over suspected hybrid attacks, bomb hoaxes and cyberattacks that aviation authorities say are very hard to contain.

In Israel, the president has asked opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot, not Benjamin Netanyahu, to form the next government after last week's election left neither bloc with a majority. Why it matters: a possible end to the Netanyahu era would reshape regional diplomacy just as the Iran file heats up.

Elsewhere: New Zealand's election produced no clear winner, pointing to weeks of coalition talks. In Washington, Trump has promoted Stephen Miller to chief of staff, setting up clashes with the new Congress, and offered the press secretary job to UFC boss Dana White. Police clashed with Yellow Vest protesters defying a ban in Paris. The Houthis say at least 25 people were killed in a Saudi air strike in Yemen. Paraguay's ousted president Santiago Peña has been charged over the banking collapse that froze millions of accounts. And on markets-adjacent culture, Pop Mart's revenue has fallen as the Labubu collectible craze fades.

That's your Morning Briefing for Sunday, November 8. Trade carefully, and we'll see you tomorrow.
