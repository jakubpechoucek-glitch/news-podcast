Good morning. It's Friday, November 13, 2026. Today: Wall Street slips as Fed rate-hike bets harden, the Nvidia leadership crisis reshapes the US-China backdrop, and the peso weakens past 63.70 ahead of the ASEAN summit here in Manila.

Start with markets. US stocks closed lower across the board. The Dow Jones Industrial Average finished at about 52,297, down 0.4 percent. The S&P 500 ended near 7,592, also down 0.4 percent, and the Nasdaq Composite closed around 25,366, down 0.4 percent. The move that matters more is in bonds: the US 10-year Treasury yield jumped 10 basis points to 5.10 percent. Two-year yields have risen 34 basis points over two sessions, the sharpest two-day move in years, as traders price in a larger-than-usual Fed hike next month. The dollar is near a 19-month high and the yen is testing 160, raising intervention risk in Tokyo. Why it matters: a 5.10 percent US 10-year is the single biggest source of pressure on Asian currencies right now, including the peso.

Big tech was split. Apple rose 2.0 percent to about 355.63, Nvidia gained 2.9 percent to 173.73, Alphabet added 2.1 percent to 293.45, and Tesla rose 2.9 percent to 390.84. On the losing side, Microsoft fell 2.3 percent to 521.13 after a global outage took down Outlook, Teams, Copilot and other 365 services for tens of thousands of users. Amazon dropped 1.7 percent and Meta fell 2.0 percent. Nvidia's bounce follows a brutal session after CEO Jensen Huang resigned; retail investors poured a record 111 million dollars into the stock in the first half-hour of trading Thursday, trimming an opening loss of as much as 14 percent. Huang forfeits roughly 108 million dollars in unvested equity. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO.

On central banks, there is fresh Fed news. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee called October's hotter-than-expected CPI "borderline disturbing," New York's John Williams said the Fed has "work to do" on inflation, and Governor Christopher Waller floated a half-point hike. Separately, the Fed set a revised 2027 calendar: seven rate-setting meetings instead of eight, with the October 2027 session reserved for strategy discussion and no rate vote. At the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, no rate decision, but policy signals: the BSP said it will weigh the sharp slowdown in GDP in its next rate call even as elevated inflation argues for staying tight. It also kept the 3 percent monthly cap on credit card finance charges while raising caps on installment rates and cash advance fees by about 50 percent.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi closed at about 5,690, up 0.5 percent, a modest rebound after sinking to a more-than-one-week low in the prior session on growth worries and softer foreign direct investment data. The peso was the weak spot, falling 0.8 percent to 63.78 to the dollar, tracking that spike in US yields. On the corporate side, First Philippine Holdings is divesting its Rockwell Land stake for 15.9 billion pesos, and PLDT is targeting 26 billion pesos in fresh capital next year from another tower sale and a possible IPO of its data center unit. Earnings were mixed: Ayala Corporation's core income slipped 6 percent to 34.5 billion pesos on softer cyclical businesses, Cebu Pacific widened its nine-month net loss to nearly 13 billion pesos on higher jet fuel costs, and Cebu Landmasters' profit fell. Meat importers are demanding repeal of the 60-day pork import ban, warning of supply risks. And the 49th ASEAN Summit opens here in two days, with a US defense firm eyeing a 20-million-dollar drone hub in Aurora. Why it matters: a weaker peso plus elevated inflation narrows the BSP's room to cut, even with growth slowing.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed at about 25,255, up 0.8 percent, and the BSE Sensex at roughly 81,879, up 1.0 percent, a third straight session of gains, driven by foreign inflows, a 4.5 percent surge in realty, banks up more than 1 percent, and insurers rallying on strong October premium data. The rupee weakened 0.4 percent to 94.03 to the dollar on dollar buying. The key macro number: retail inflation hit 6.09 percent, breaching the Reserve Bank of India's 6 percent upper tolerance limit for the first time in two years on soaring food prices, which strengthens the case for rate hikes. Separately, the RBI is proposing to cap select overseas remittances at 50 lakh rupees to defend the currency, issued new domestic transfer guidelines, and is targeting polymer banknotes in circulation by next financial year. Longer term, Moody's sees India growing 7 percent in 2028, outpacing China by 3 percentage points. Why it matters: India now joins the US in facing an inflation problem that points to higher, not lower, rates.

To the world. The Nvidia scandal has become a geopolitical story. Lawmakers want to question Huang over whether one of his alleged relationships was with a Chinese spy; the woman, Alice Huang, reportedly held stakes in companies supplying computing power to Huawei, Nvidia's biggest Chinese competitor. China's Ministry of State Security is now in the spotlight, days before the Trump-Xi summit. Nvidia has hired Wachtell, Lipton and ex-prosecutor Joon Kim to investigate. Why it matters: the political climate going into that summit is drastically different from September, and Nvidia's web of ties makes it a systemic risk, not just a stock.

On Iran, oil prices rose after President Trump said no deal is needed, saying "we've got their Strait," while also revealing Gulf allies urged him to pause further strikes. Yemen's Houthis renewed drone attacks on Saudi oil and military infrastructure as the UN Security Council extended sanctions. Why it matters: higher crude feeds straight into Philippine fuel and electricity costs.

On Ukraine, President Zelensky welcomed Washington bringing European leaders into peace talks with Russia. At the same time, a Russian warship was hit by a Ukrainian drone in the Gulf of Finland, believed to be the first such attack in open Baltic waters, and a Russian court jailed a human rights lawyer for 12 years on treason charges.

Two quick ones. The UK economy grew 0.7 percent in the three months to September, its fastest in more than two years. And Anthropic's IPO is roughly six times oversubscribed, on track to be potentially the largest first-time share sale ever, while Nvidia-backed Nscale filed updated US IPO paperwork showing a widening loss. Why it matters: AI capital markets are still wide open, even with Nvidia in turmoil.

That's your Morning Briefing for Friday, November 13. Have a productive day.
