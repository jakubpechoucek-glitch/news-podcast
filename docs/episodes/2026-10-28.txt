Good morning. It's Wednesday, October 28, 2026. Today: a tech-led Wall Street rally ahead of the Fed decision, the peso at the 64 line, and a Ukraine ceasefire that's holding.

To the markets. US stocks closed higher, led by technology. The Dow Jones Industrial Average finished nearly flat at about 52,378, up 0.03 percent. The S&P 500 rose 0.6 percent to about 7,723. The Nasdaq Composite gained 1.2 percent to about 26,478. The driver was revived artificial intelligence momentum, with Anthropic's initial public offering prospectus giving the sector a relief rally. That 355-page filing disclosed a cash pile of 131.7 billion dollars, larger than every non-bank US company except Berkshire Hathaway and Alphabet. Why it matters: AI capital spending fears have been the market's main worry for weeks, and Anthropic's balance sheet calmed them, for now.

The US 10-year Treasury yield was 4.99 percent, up 1 basis point. Bond strategists warn bullish Treasury positions are exposed if the Fed sounds more hawkish today.

Among the big names, Tesla was the standout, up 4.0 percent. Amazon rose 1.6 percent, even as an internal memo told engineers to be pickier about using AI and stop wasteful token use. Alphabet gained 1.2 percent, Apple 0.9 percent despite confirming it will replace iPhone Duos affected by a screen fault on its first foldable, and Nvidia 0.9 percent. Microsoft slipped 0.2 percent and Meta fell 1.6 percent. Elsewhere, Boeing shares hit an 18-month low after it cut this year's 737 Max delivery target and said it won't significantly raise production until late 2027. Robinhood fell 4 percent as a crypto slump weighed on profits. And General Motors said it will move its stock listing from the New York Stock Exchange to Nasdaq.

On central banks: the Federal Reserve's two-day meeting concludes today and markets overwhelmingly expect no change after September's rate increase, with inflation at a three-year high. Watch Chair Warsh's language for signals on a possible December hike. In Manila, President Marcos met the Bangko Sentral governor and the economic team to discuss this month's policy decision. Deutsche Bank says the BSP may pause its rate hikes; others are urging continued tightening to protect long-term growth. Separately, the BSP barred five unlicensed money changers in Makati, Pampanga and Davao from registration, and pushed back on RCBC's conditional free InstaPay transfers.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi rebounded 2.1 percent to about 5,761, snapping a three-day losing streak on bargain hunting. But the peso weakened another 0.8 percent to about 63.99 to the dollar, closing past the 64 barrier for the first time. Why it matters: a weaker peso raises the cost of imported fuel, electricity and food, squeezing household budgets directly. That bites immediately, with a diesel price increase effective October 27, partly offset by a gasoline rollback. On the brighter side, Board of Investments-approved investments jumped 53 percent in the first nine months of the year. But DMCI Holdings chairman Isidro Consunji warns the government's new framework for granting natural resources contracts may be challenged in court, and the OECD says regulatory gaps could cost the Philippines nickel investment fleeing China. In banking, CIMB's Philippine unit sale is reportedly attracting BPI and Metrobank. And the stage is set for GCash's record 92.3 billion peso listing. On security, Philippine, Japanese and US forces have joined the Keen Sword exercise.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed about 0.7 percent higher at roughly 22,753 and the BSE Sensex rose about 0.7 percent to about 73,607, though gains were capped by elevated crude oil prices and caution before the Fed. Foreign portfolio investors bought shares worth 4,240 crore rupees. The rupee weakened 0.2 percent to 95.11 to the dollar. On the Reserve Bank, reports say the central bank has quietly delivered another effective tightening through tighter liquidity, countering the crude surge and the falling rupee. Former governor Raghuram Rajan cautioned India should not deplete its foreign exchange reserves defending the currency. Why it matters: it signals the RBI will let the rupee slide rather than burn reserves, so imported inflation is the pressure valve. Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta said growth momentum is holding and India could have exceeded 8 percent growth without external shocks, though Reuters reports economists see momentum fading after a strong start to the fiscal year. Governor-level guidance also urged states to adopt prudent borrowing strategies. In markets, grey market pricing points to listing gains of around 23 percent for the Jio Platforms IPO.

Now to the world. In Ukraine, the ceasefire across the Kherson region has been extended, with both Russia and Ukraine reporting no breaches. It was originally arranged for stranded residents of Oleshky. Separately, the leaders of Norway and Finland held a rare call with President Putin. Finland's Alexander Stubb said an end to the conflict "will not be achieved through silence." Why it matters: it's the first visible Nordic diplomatic channel to Moscow in this phase of the war.

On energy, the US energy secretary told CNBC the Trump administration may allow more Russian diesel onto the market under the Trump-Putin deal. Analysts say the volumes are small relative to demand and won't materially lower prices. Crude remains elevated, which is exactly what is pressuring Indian equities and Philippine pump prices.

In the United States, President Trump campaigned in Texas showcasing Venezuelan oil, as the eight-month war with Iran makes affordability the defining midterm issue. The US also said it killed five people in a strike on an alleged drug boat, bringing the toll of that campaign, which critics call extrajudicial killings, to 243 since September 2025. House Speaker Mike Johnson dismissed as "absurd" Democratic concerns about a contested transfer of power after the midterms.

Israel voted in a tight election, with tens of thousands of citizens flying home to cast ballots and Prime Minister Netanyahu rallying Likud voters with a megaphone in a shopping mall minutes before polls closed. Why it matters: coalition talks and policy uncertainty are the key swing factor for Tel Aviv markets and for regional risk. Elsewhere in the region, officials from Riyadh, Islamabad, Ankara and Tehran met in Iran to discuss a possible new Gulf security framework.

In Brazil, Lula's comeback has dented Washington's ambitions in Latin America, and Flavio Bolsonaro has fled the country after the election loss, with the US saying it will grant him a visa. That came a day after Brazil's Supreme Court ordered his father to surrender his passports in a money-laundering probe.

In Egypt, a court fined journalist Lina Attalah and ordered the closure of the independent news site Mada Masr, a verdict Amnesty International called a crushing blow to press freedom.

And in the UK, Chancellor John Healey delivers the Budget today, with a bank levy, energy bill help and a minimum wage rise all in play.
