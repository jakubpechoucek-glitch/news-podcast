Good morning. It's Thursday, October 29, 2026, and this is your Morning Briefing on finance and geopolitics. Today: the Fed holds but hawks dissent, Alphabet stumbles after the bell, the peso sits at record lows, and Spain counts 68 dead in a high-speed rail disaster.

Start with Wall Street. All three major indexes closed higher, but only just. The Dow finished at about 52,475, up 0.2%. The S&P 500 at about 7,744, up 0.3%. The Nasdaq Composite at about 26,550, up 0.3%. The lift came mainly from chipmakers, after SK Hynix posted a record third-quarter profit and revived the AI trade.

Bonds went the other way. The US 10-year Treasury yield rose 5 basis points to 5.05%, while short-dated yields fell after Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said policymakers will hold fewer rate-setting meetings from next year. Why it matters: a 10-year above 5% keeps global borrowing costs high and keeps pressure on emerging-market currencies, including the peso.

Big tech was mixed, and the real action came after the bell. Alphabet closed up 1.3% at about 325 dollars, then sank around 10% on its first top-and-bottom-line miss in nearly four years; it held 2026 capital spending unchanged and flagged a significant increase for 2027. Microsoft closed flat at about 531 but jumped roughly 4% on earnings, with Azure revenue up 48%. Its capex, though, came in below expectations, knocking the so-called neocloud stocks, CoreWeave, Nebius and IREN. Nvidia fell 1.2% to about 215 as the Justice Department opened an inquiry into its 12.9 billion dollar purchase of Hugging Face. Apple slipped 0.9% to about 344 after unveiling a touchscreen Mac, reversing a years-old stance, in the third major launch under new chief executive John Ternus. Meta fell 1.2%, Tesla fell 0.8%, Amazon rose 0.6%. Elsewhere, Oracle popped 11% after forecasting 225 billion dollars of revenue in fiscal 2030 and capping new fundraising at 20 billion, easing debt worries.

Now central banks. The Federal Reserve left its benchmark at 3.75 to 4% in a 10-2 vote, with both dissenters wanting a quarter-point hike. Warsh said the economy is not running too hot despite AI-driven demand, and that the committee is waiting for an accumulation of more information while inflation stays above the 2% target. Markets have pushed the next likely move, a hike, into December. The timing matters politically: the decision lands days before US midterm elections where affordability is the dominant issue.

There's no fresh Bangko Sentral rate decision. But Governor Eli Remolona and the economic managers met President Marcos in Malacañang on Tuesday to discuss inflation, El Niño and currency pressure, and BusinessWorld reports external headwinds are complicating the BSP's effort to temper peso volatility.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi fell 1.87% to about 5,654, slipping below the 5,700 level, with local reports blaming inflation fears, the weak peso and rising global fuel prices. The dollar bought 63.96 pesos, up another 0.2%. Marcos told the public "don't panic" after the peso's historic lows and pledged to "do all we can" to defend it; business leaders were blunter, with one Manila Bulletin headline summing up the mood as "rates can't grow rice" — the argument that BSP hikes can't fix food and energy supply shocks. Why it matters: with imported fuel and food driving inflation, higher rates mostly tighten the domestic economy without stopping the price pressure. Three other items. The Philippine Stock Exchange is nearing full control of PDS after acquiring DBP's stake. GCash's record 92.3 billion peso IPO is set to proceed. And the World Bank says the country's wealth gap has widened even as poverty has fallen. On defense, US Secretary Pete Hegseth, in Manila, pledged a stronger military framework and reassured Southeast Asia of US commitment, though reports note it was light on specifics.

Turning to India. Both benchmarks closed essentially flat but soft: the Nifty 50 at about 22,750, down less than 0.1%, and the Sensex at about 73,599, also fractionally lower, with IT and oil and gas stocks the main drag. The rupee was little changed at 95.21 to the dollar. The dominant force is crude near 115 dollars a barrel, which hits India's import bill directly; foreign holdings of India's index-linked bonds have fallen to a four-month low, and the Nifty is heading for its worst year in a decade. On the Reserve Bank, a deputy governor said India remains on a path toward 8% growth and is the only major emerging economy set to cut its public debt ratio, to 75.1% of GDP by the 2032 fiscal year, while warning global buffers are thinning. The RBI has also proposed a 50 lakh rupee sub-limit under the liberalised remittance scheme. In corporate news, Jio Platforms debuted at a 21% premium, taking its valuation above 12 lakh crore rupees. Industrial output growth eased to a four-month low in September as mining contracted.

Now to the world. In Spain, at least 68 people were killed and more than 100 injured when a high-speed train struck a stopped train in Toledo province south of Madrid. The transport minister called the cause an "inexplicable" error. It's the second deadly high-speed collision in Spain this year, and the government is facing immediate demands for answers.

In Israel, with 85% of ballots counted, Netanyahu's Likud and its allies are on track for 53 of the 120 Knesset seats — leading, but short of a majority. Analysts see an impasse, with fresh elections a plausible outcome. Why it matters: prolonged political deadlock in Israel adds another layer of uncertainty to a Middle East conflict that is already repricing energy.

On that, the World Bank now projects commodity prices will surge 23% in 2026, as the Middle East war disrupts energy supplies and shipping, with grain and metals also higher. The bank warned that further escalation, combined with a severe El Niño, would intensify inflation — a direct read-through to Philippine food and fuel costs.

In Britain, Chancellor John Healey delivered his first Budget: a 100-pound energy bill payment from January, a new bank tax, income tax thresholds frozen for three more years, and defense spending targets brought forward with billions pledged to Ukraine. The Office for Budget Responsibility says higher energy prices will weigh on growth and living standards.

Two more. In Brazil, Flávio Bolsonaro landed in Miami after losing the presidential election, having failed to comply with an order to surrender his passports. And in dealmaking, DoorDash's acquisition of Instacart could be announced as early as this morning, valuing Instacart at roughly 17 billion dollars, while Nelson Peltz's Trian has bid 7.35 dollars a share to take Wendy's private.
