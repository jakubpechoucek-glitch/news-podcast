Good morning. It's Friday, November 27, 2026. Here's your Morning Briefing on finance and geopolitics: a split Wall Street session driven by an Nvidia credit warning, a record Philippine trade gap, and oil edging higher as the Iran war drags on.

To markets. US stocks closed in two directions. The Dow ended at about 50,183, up 0.3 percent. The S&P 500 closed at about 7,183, down 0.3 percent. The Nasdaq Composite finished at about 23,586, down 0.6 percent. The 10-year Treasury yield fell 6 basis points to 4.85 percent. Why it matters: money rotated out of AI-linked tech and into bonds and old-economy names, not out of risk altogether.

Big tech was the story. Nvidia dropped 3.9 percent to 149.30 after S&P Global Ratings warned its credit rating could be cut by one notch, flagging rising management and governance risk following Jensen Huang's abrupt return as chief executive. Amazon fell 3.1 percent to 244.43, Alphabet lost 2.1 percent to 273.10, Microsoft slipped 1.3 percent to 489.93, and Tesla eased 1.2 percent to 380.14. Meta bucked the trend, up 3.4 percent to 609.89, and Apple added 0.4 percent to 355.58. Asian markets then stabilised, with Japan, South Korea, Australia and Hong Kong higher for a second day as investors fished for bargains.

On central banks. The Federal Reserve has raised rates for the first time since 2023, and markets are now pricing a meaningful chance of another hike next month as inflation worsens and new tariffs raise the odds of higher-for-longer policy. Reuters reports Fed policymakers are openly at odds over what is driving inflation. The dollar is hovering near a 22-month peak, and gold is steady and heading for a weekly gain. There was no Bangko Sentral rate decision overnight. Governor Remolona says he sees a fourth-quarter recovery but describes growth as still weak, and the Asian Development Bank notes Philippine bond yields have surged the most in the region amid BSP tightening and inflation pressure.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi closed at about 5,515, up 0.5 percent, recovering after profit-taking snapped a three-day rally earlier in the week. The peso weakened 0.2 percent to 63.67 to the dollar. Three stories matter. First, the trade deficit widened to a record 6.9 billion dollars in October. Why it matters: that is the single biggest source of pressure on the peso, and it limits how fast the BSP can ease. Second, the government has declared a national energy emergency as Asia faces an energy squeeze from the Iran war, and AirAsia Philippines is cutting its workforce, citing high fuel costs and currency pressure. Why it matters: imported fuel is now a direct corporate earnings and inflation risk. Third, the Treasury raised 1.26 billion dollars from its retail bond offer, and the Energy Regulatory Commission ordered a 1.98-billion-peso Meralco refund, worth about 52 pesos a month for a typical 200-kilowatt-hour household. Separately, Manila is targeting six Chinook helicopters by 2029 under a 2-billion-dollar US financing deal.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed at about 25,811, down 0.3 percent, while the Sensex rose 0.5 percent to about 84,329, near a 10-month high as tariff fears eased. The rupee weakened 0.4 percent to 92.57 to the dollar. The Reserve Bank of India kept its repo rate unchanged and projects real GDP growth of 6.9 percent this fiscal year, but swaps and treasury bill pricing now signal the possibility of an outsized RBI rate hike next week. Why it matters: a hawkish surprise would hit the rally that has given India its fifth straight weekly gain. Elsewhere, the RBI has brought another 60 tonnes of gold home, with 84 percent of reserves now held domestically, and flagged non-bank lenders' dependence on bank borrowing as a possible systemic concern. Mahanadi Coalfields, a Coal India unit, begins trading after a 969-million-dollar IPO. And Washington's proposed tariffs of 18 percent on Bangladesh, Cambodia and Thailand and 20 percent on China and Vietnam hand Indian exporters a relative edge, though the US is also questioning whether India's advantage rests on cheap Chinese inputs.

Now to the world. Ukraine. President Zelensky told the BBC the United States is too focused on the Middle East and is not pushing Russia hard enough, calling it "strange" that Donald Trump has described Vladimir Putin as a friend. He warned of a major incoming Russian attack, while Trump says he urged Putin to pull back and NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte cautioned Moscow against widening the war. Russian strikes on Ukraine's energy grid have made electricity and water shortages routine in Kyiv. Why it matters: a wider war is the main upside risk to European energy prices heading into winter.

The Middle East. Two Italians were killed in a drone attack in Kuwait; Iran's foreign minister Abbas Araghchi called the incident suspicious and "a precooked recipe for war." Trump told US troops in the Gulf that the Iran war ends "whenever I want it," and claimed the US holds close to 50 percent of the world's oil and does not need Middle East supply. Brent crude is trading near this month's high after tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz and strikes on Saudi energy infrastructure, with some analysts seeing a path above 100 dollars a barrel. Why it matters: that is the direct transmission line to Philippine fuel costs and the peso.

Trade and technology. The White House has unveiled a fresh tariff salvo aimed at 14 trading partners, offering lower rates to countries that sign deals, in a push to pry global supply chains away from China. Global stocks largely shrugged it off. Washington has also told Nvidia chip buyers they must rule out Chinese renters to purchase GPUs, a new Bureau of Industry and Security rule aimed at closing loopholes, which sources say threatens Chinese AI model development. Meanwhile China's industrial profits jumped 20.1 percent in October, a five-month high, powered by AI-related manufacturing and exports even as household consumption lags.

Three quick ones. Spain's Pablo Hernández de Cos is set to lead the European Central Bank, succeeding Christine Lagarde next year. New Zealand's National-led coalition has fallen one seat short of a parliamentary majority, and New Zealand stocks dipped on the uncertainty. And news agency AFP has shut down its content platform after a cyberattack, as France's cybersecurity chief warns of unprecedented threat levels.

That's your Morning Briefing for Friday, November 27. Trade safely, and we'll see you Monday.
