Good morning. It's Saturday, October 31, 2026. Today: an AI-driven selloff on Wall Street, an 8 percent inflation warning here at home, and the biggest crypto heist on record.

Start with Wall Street, where Friday was ugly. The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at about 51,770, down 1.1 percent. The S&P 500 finished near 7,580, down 1.8 percent. The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.9 percent to about 25,437, its biggest one-day drop since June 5 and its lowest level in more than two months. The trigger: disappointing results from Intel and Amazon reignited fears that spending on artificial intelligence, now approaching 1 trillion dollars, is burning cash faster than it generates returns. Intel plunged about 30 percent after guiding to weak fourth-quarter revenue and negative cash flow for the year, dragging the whole chip complex down with it. AMD fell about 12 percent. Reddit crashed 29 percent, its worst day ever, after warning that Google's AI search features are cutting its referral traffic.

Among the megacaps, Nvidia was hit hardest, down 4.2 percent to about 205 dollars. Microsoft fell 1.6 percent to about 522, Meta 1.5 percent to about 615, Amazon 1.4 percent to about 240, Alphabet 1.4 percent to about 280, Apple 1.2 percent to about 345, and Tesla 0.9 percent to about 400. S&P Global revised its outlook on Meta to negative, citing heavy capital expenditure and free cash flow concerns. For the month, the Nasdaq 100 has shed roughly 2 trillion dollars and entered its third technical correction of 2026. Money moved to safety: the US 10-year Treasury yield fell 6 basis points to 4.93 percent. Why it matters: this is a repricing of the AI trade, not a growth scare, and it is hitting the handful of stocks that carried global indices all year.

Central banks. The Federal Reserve held rates steady this week and attention now shifts to December. Unusually, officials including Musalem and Logan are publicly making the case that the next move may need to be a hike, not a cut, given persistent inflation. Why it matters: higher-for-longer funding costs are exactly what the AI capital spending story can least afford.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi closed at about 5,680, down 0.4 percent, slipping back below 5,700 on profit-taking ahead of the Undas long weekend, with a looming oil price surge weighing on sentiment. Gasoline prices are set to rise again next week. The peso firmed 0.3 percent to 63.65 to the dollar. The headline number is inflation: the Bangko Sentral says October inflation may have landed between 7.1 and 8.1 percent, and commentary here is already warning the BSP cannot fight this alone, because the pressure is energy and supply driven. Moody's cut its 2026 Philippine growth forecast to 3.3 percent from 3.6 percent, citing elevated energy prices and weak investment. The September trade deficit widened to 5.15 billion dollars even with record exports, and national government debt hit a record 19.66 trillion pesos, partly on peso weakness. One bright spot: BDO posted record nine-month profit of 63.6 billion pesos. Why it matters: slowing growth with rising prices squeezes households and leaves the BSP very little room to ease.

Turning to India, where markets went the other way. The Nifty 50 rose 1.1 percent to about 22,937 and the BSE Sensex gained 1.2 percent to about 74,151, a second straight day of gains led by IT and pharma, though the broader midcap market underperformed. October snapped a two-month losing streak. The rupee strengthened 0.3 percent to 94.94 per dollar. From the Reserve Bank of India: foreign exchange reserves fell for a seventh straight week to a more than two-month low, the central board reviewed risks to growth and inflation, and the RBI is demanding proof of cyber-resilience compliance from banks after a rise in attacks. On the economy, the finance ministry estimates July-to-September growth at 7.35 percent and has raised its full-year forecast. In earnings, Reliance Industries posted a record quarterly profit of 29,550 crore rupees on an oil-to-chemicals recovery, while Sun Pharma's profit fell 36 percent on litigation and acquisition costs. Why it matters: strong domestic growth and IT strength are, for now, offsetting crude above 116 dollars and a soft currency.

Now the world. The biggest story is the hack of crypto exchange Binance, which lost 6.3 billion dollars in what is believed to be the largest cyber-attack of its kind in history, linked to North Korea. Binance has frozen assets, and investigators describe a cat-and-mouse race to trace the funds before they are laundered into cash. Co-founder Changpeng Zhao publicly asked Elon Musk to stop trolling, pointing to Binance's billion-dollar-plus stake in SpaceX. Why it matters: a state-linked theft of that scale is a sanctions and systemic-risk event, not just a crypto story.

Energy next. Iran has sharply escalated attacks on shipping, but tankers are still running the Strait of Hormuz because freight rates have soared enough to make the risk pay. Crude is trading above 116 dollars a barrel. Why it matters: that one price is driving Philippine pump prices, 8 percent inflation, and the Fed's hawkish turn.

Diplomacy. G20 foreign ministers met in Atlanta with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio hosting and urging cooperation to defend the economic order. The traditional group photo was scrapped after European ministers refused to stand with sanctioned Russian foreign minister Sergei Lavrov, with Iran, Hormuz and Ukraine splitting the group. Why it matters: the forum built to coordinate the global economy cannot agree on a photograph. Separately, the EU and Canada signed a digital trade deal as Washington's allies hedge against Trump's trade threats.

Quickly elsewhere: hundreds of people were evacuated from Russian-occupied Oleshky during a rare Ukraine ceasefire, though many stayed in the front-line town. In Russia, the independent newspaper Novaya Gazeta has been shut under official pressure, its founder saying they were strangled. In Poland, police were deployed during a standoff over control of the top court after the government suspended two senior justices. In Spain, transport minister Óscar Puente refuses to resign after a high-speed train collision that killed at least 69 people. In the United States, the Ninth Circuit again rejected Trump's bid to void 20,000 California ballots ahead of midterms that will decide control of Congress. And closer to home, China has accused the Philippines of erecting obstacles to its ASEAN attendance, while North Korea's Kim Jong Un urged drone dominance and greater military use of AI.

That's your Morning Briefing for Saturday, October 31. Have a safe and restful Undas weekend.
