Good morning. It's Sunday, November 15, 2026, and this is your Morning Briefing on finance and geopolitics. Today: a Nasdaq-led pullback as Nvidia's leadership crisis rolls on, rising odds of a December Fed hike, the PSEi near a one-year low, and a planned Trump-Putin-Xi meeting in China.

Start with markets. US stocks closed split. The Dow added 0.1 percent to about 52,348. The S&P 500 fell 0.4 percent to roughly 7,562, and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.9 percent to about 25,136. Zoom out, though, and it was still a strong week: the S&P 500 gained 2.5 percent and the Nasdaq 2.3 percent. The US 10-year Treasury yield rose 5 basis points to 5.15 percent. Why it matters: a 5-handle on the 10-year keeps pressure on equity valuations and on emerging-market currencies, the peso included.

In big tech, Nvidia was the drag, down 2.0 percent to about 162.85 after Chief Executive Jensen Huang resigned following reports of multiple affairs, including one with a suspected Chinese spy. Two investors holding roughly 2.13 billion dollars of stock are now urging the board to run a deliberate leadership search and to consider Huang's potential return. Tesla fell 1.8 percent to about 390, Alphabet lost 1.0 percent to about 296, and Amazon slipped 0.3 percent to about 267. Meta bucked the trend, up 2.9 percent to about 642, even as Italy fined it 805 million euros for failing to tell 20 million users it had access to their photos. Microsoft rose 0.2 percent to about 524 after restoring Outlook, Teams and Copilot following a global outage lasting more than 24 hours across 70-plus countries; the company ruled out a cyberattack. Apple added 0.4 percent to about 356. And Berkshire Hathaway hit a fourth straight record close, with a 19 percent fourth-quarter rally.

On central banks: the direction of travel is up. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said the central bank "has definitely got work to do" on inflation and will likely need to act. Bloomberg reports the Fed is on track to raise rates next month and possibly again in January, with some analysts now flagging a larger-than-normal 50-basis-point move in December. Separately, Kevin Warsh is pushing to remake the Fed with fewer rate votes and more deliberation. No rate decision from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas this weekend; the BSP reaffirmed its price stability mandate, while Citi now sees Philippine inflation staying above target until 2027.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi fell 1.2 percent to about 5,593, a near one-year low, with traders citing peso weakness, oil, and disappointing third-quarter GDP and corporate earnings. The peso weakened 1.2 percent to 63.70 to the dollar. Why it matters: imported fuel and food costs rise with a weaker peso, reinforcing Citi's above-target inflation call. The BSP has eased rules on cash withdrawals above 1 million pesos through January 2027 for the holidays, and signed agreements to link QR payments with Cambodia and to deepen ties with Bank Indonesia. World leaders are in Manila as the Philippines wraps up its ASEAN chairmanship, and ASEAN has concluded free-trade talks with Canada, which expects trade with the Philippines to triple over the next decade. Ecozone investments hit a 14-year record, but analysts warn President Trump's visit is no quick fix for tariff troubles. President Marcos has signed an executive order creating a national AI council, as the country ranks 53rd of 70 in IMD's digital and AI competitiveness table.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed down 0.6 percent at about 25,144, and the Sensex fell 0.4 percent to roughly 81,592, snapping a three-day rally on rate-hike fears, though both indices still logged a third straight weekly gain on foreign buying. The rupee eased 0.2 percent to 94.24 to the dollar. Technically, the Nifty sits below resistance near 25,400 after rebounding from its 200-week moving average. From the Reserve Bank of India: revised capital adequacy norms for banks, and in-principle approval for Paytm to revive its wallet business, sending the stock to a record high. Watch also the RBI's revised gold-lending rules, which lenders say are pushing non-bank financiers into repayment stress. Elsewhere, Yes Bank has asked the Supreme Court to review the order quashing its write-off of 8,415 crore rupees of AT-1 bonds, and Mahanadi Coalfields has filed for an IPO of nearly 9,500 crore rupees, a 10 percent stake sale by Coal India. Household inflation above 6 percent is the key risk to the rally.

Now the world. The White House says Trump, Putin and Xi plan an unprecedented joint meeting in China next week, with reduction of nuclear weapons on the agenda. US officials say Trump does not plan to raise the Nvidia spy affair during the trip. Why it matters: a three-way summit of that scale could reset tariff, tech-export and security expectations across Asia in a single week. On Ukraine, Volodymyr Zelensky says Washington has floated Kyiv ceding the Donbas in exchange for NATO membership; a US official would only say "many ideas continue to be discussed."

In the Middle East, an Israeli air strike in Khan Younis killed Hamas's Rafah brigade commander Samir Abu Younis, and Israel's caretaker government has vowed to keep an "offensive policy" in Gaza, with Benjamin Netanyahu pressing for a unity government that rival Gadi Eisenkot dismissed as "tricks". Iraq's prime minister has dismissed nine senior security officials in a probe into an oil-smuggling network worth up to 30 million dollars a month, a reminder of the supply risk premium still embedded in crude.

In the US, the transport secretary says a contractor's copper wire upgrade to an L3Harris telecommunications system preceded the nationwide flight ground stop, though the FAA says the root cause is still unknown and that routers and networking gear may be involved. Pressure is also building on Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick over his family firm's ties to Nvidia and the delayed entity-listing of Megaspeed.

In crypto and commodities, Bitcoin tumbled to a three-month low just above 63,000 dollars as leveraged long positions were liquidated, while Binance says it will fully reopen withdrawals by the end of next week after recovering 95 percent of the 6.3 billion dollars lost in October's hack. Base metals lost momentum, with the dollar index at 103 to 104. And SpaceX made history, catching both the booster and the upper stage of Starship on test flight 15.

One final note from home: the Philippines' Carmelle won the first Eurovision Song Contest Asia in Bangkok with her anthem Sobra-Sobra.

That's your Morning Briefing for Sunday, November 15. Markets reopen Monday; we'll see you then.
