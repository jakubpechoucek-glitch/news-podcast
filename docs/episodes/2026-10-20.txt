Good morning. It's Tuesday, October 20, 2026. Today: a tech-led rebound on Wall Street with bond yields still rising, a Bangko Sentral rate decision now two days away with the peso at a record low, and India's biggest listing of the year opening tomorrow.

To the markets. US stocks closed mixed but with a clear risk-on tilt. The Dow Jones ended at about 51,966, down 0.1 percent. The S&P 500 closed near 7,691, up 0.4 percent. The Nasdaq Composite was the standout at roughly 26,431, up 1.1 percent, as chip and AI-linked names bounced back from last week's sharp selloff. CoreWeave led the recovery after a Goldman Sachs upgrade to buy, with Intel, Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom all higher. Why it matters: the AI trade is bruised, not broken, but it is now headline-driven rather than trend-driven.

In bonds, the US 10-year Treasury yield rose 4 basis points to 5.14 percent, as the rebound in tech shares drained demand for havens. Among the megacaps, Amazon was the big winner, up 5.0 percent to about 262 dollars after unveiling TorchNeuron, a tool that lets PyTorch code run on its own Trainium chips — a direct challenge to Nvidia's software moat. Tesla rose 3.8 percent to about 368. Nvidia still gained 1.4 percent to about 217, Alphabet 1.1 percent to about 344, and Microsoft 0.8 percent to about 534. Apple slipped 0.2 percent to about 343 even as it announced an October 28 launch event titled "Pro and tell," with analysts expecting a touchscreen laptop, and despite reports of strong iPhone Duo preorders. Meta fell 0.6 percent to about 705.

Two warning lights. Goldman Sachs data shows hedge funds cut technology exposure at the fastest pace in at least a decade last week. And S&P Global has flagged potentially hidden AI-related debt at the hyperscalers, tied to data centre financing structures and smaller AI firms, though it sees no immediate need for ratings changes. Meanwhile Anthropic is now seen as the last major IPO of 2026 after OpenAI delayed its debut.

On central banks, there's no Fed decision this week, but the signal has shifted: traders eased bets on a near-term rate hike after a speech by Kevin Warsh, pushing expectations toward December. Gold edged higher on softer oil and that repricing. Separately, Fed officials have flagged that the AI investment boom is adding to the inflation problem. For the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, the decision lands Thursday, October 22. Economists polled by local outlets overwhelmingly expect another hike as inflation pressures broaden and the peso stays weak, and one former BSP official is publicly urging a 50-basis-point move. The Bureau of the Treasury has already trimmed its borrowing as domestic yields climb into the meeting.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi closed at about 5,798, up 0.3 percent — a third straight advance, but it again failed to clear the 5,800 resistance level traders are watching. The peso set another record low, finishing at 63.01 to the dollar, 0.1 percent weaker. Why it matters: a record-low peso is doing a lot of the arguing for a BSP hike this Thursday.

Politically, Manila summoned China's ambassador, Jing Quan, over remarks the Department of Foreign Affairs says conveyed a threat involving the use of force. Coast Guard spokesman Rear Admiral Jay Tarriela has demanded an apology, while Beijing says relations are at a "critical juncture." Why it matters: South China Sea risk premium is back on the table for peso and equity investors.

On the corporate side, Vena Energy is eyeing a 245-billion-peso expansion in the Philippines, and LNGPH is weighing a 6-billion-peso onshore gas storage tank in Batangas. Fintech lender Salmon Group secured 140 million dollars, about 8.8 billion pesos, to expand lending. Rice tariff collections hit nearly 16 billion pesos in the first nine months, up a fifth, helped by a weaker peso, and the Department of Agriculture has opened a safeguard investigation into imported pork.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed at about 23,146, up 0.7 percent, and the BSE Sensex at about 74,729, up 0.6 percent — a rebound after the previous session's slump, when the Sensex fell 876 points and the Nifty dropped more than 1 percent on selling in private banks and elevated oil prices. The rupee was marginally firmer at 94.92 to the dollar.

The dominant story is the Jio Platforms IPO, which opens tomorrow, October 21. The company has already raised about 8,110 crore rupees from 212 anchor investors at 1,119 rupees a share, with LIC taking the largest allocation alongside BlackRock, Fidelity and GIC. Jio aims to raise roughly 30,213 crore rupees at a valuation near 108 billion dollars — enough, on some estimates, to lift Reliance Group's combined market capitalisation above Tata's and make it India's largest conglomerate. Why it matters: it is a live test of whether global money still wants Indian paper after a weak run for equities.

From the Reserve Bank, the focus is currency defence: the RBI has tightened scrutiny of overseas investments above 1 billion dollars amid pressure on the rupee, and is providing liquidity support. Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman attributed part of Indian equities' underperformance to how easily investors can exit, naming fuel, fertiliser and access to capital as 2027 risks. And IMF data suggests the weak rupee pushes India's 5-trillion-dollar economy milestone out to 2029.

Now to the world. Saudi Arabia has postponed its flagship investor conference to 2027 as Iran-backed Houthi forces escalate missile and drone attacks on the kingdom following a deadly airport attack earlier this month. In Yemen itself, a Houthi missile strike on a camp in government-controlled Shabwa province killed 50 security recruits. Egypt's parliament delayed a vote on military support for Saudi Arabia after an opposition party told members to abstain. Why it matters: a widening Gulf security crisis is the main upside risk to oil, which has been softening.

France is facing its worst unrest in years, with thousands arrested, officials weighing curfews, and President Macron vowing an "uncompromising" response ahead of a critical budget vote. French stocks fell and bond yields rose. Why it matters: Europe's second-largest economy is now a sovereign credit story, not just a political one.

In Ukraine, an adviser to President Zelensky told the BBC that Ukrainians are dying because the United States is restricting intelligence sharing, leaving too little time to warn civilians of incoming missiles. The White House says nothing has changed.

In tech geopolitics, Washington has banned the Chinese model DeepSeek from US government use, and Saudi national Abdulrahman Alfozan, accused of planting a recording device at OpenAI's San Francisco headquarters, is being held without bail after the Saudi government offered to pay it. Elsewhere, more than 160 people were reported killed in escalating drone attacks across Sudan, Alberta votes today on whether to hold a referendum on separating from Canada, and a Reuters/Ipsos poll puts President Trump's approval at a record-low 31 percent.

That's your Morning Briefing. Markets reopen shortly — trade carefully.
