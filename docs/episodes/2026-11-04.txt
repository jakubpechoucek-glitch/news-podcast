Good morning. It's Wednesday, November 4, 2026. Today: US midterm results start reshaping Washington, the Nasdaq climbs while megacap tech slips, and both the Fed and the Bangko Sentral keep talking tightening, not cuts.

Start with markets. Wall Street closed split. The Dow Jones Industrial Average finished at about 51,787, down 0.4 percent. The S&P 500 closed at roughly 7,569, up 0.4 percent. The Nasdaq Composite led, at about 25,372, up 0.8 percent. But the strength was not in the usual names. The US 10-year Treasury yield rose 6 basis points to 4.95 percent, a reminder that bond investors are pricing higher-for-longer rates, not relief.

Among the big names, Microsoft was the worst performer, down 2.8 percent to about 507 dollars. Apple fell 1.8 percent to roughly 338 dollars after its annual filing warned that AI agents could exceed their permissions, resist instructions, or take actions that are hard to reverse. Nvidia slipped 1.3 percent to about 202 dollars, Meta fell 1.3 percent to around 607, and Alphabet eased 0.3 percent to about 279 amid political backlash over cutting off search at schools that ban AI; Google says search can be switched back on with AI Overviews. On the upside, Amazon jumped 2.2 percent to about 246 dollars and Tesla added 1 percent to roughly 404.

The real action was elsewhere in tech. AMD surged 8 percent after third-quarter results beat expectations, with data centre revenue nearly doubling on AI chip sales. CrowdStrike launched an AI kill switch that can shut down rogue internal AI agents and cut their network access. And Blackstone president Jon Gray warned the AI build-out needs more equity funding, because debt investors are taking on concentrated risk. Why it matters: the AI trade is broadening out from the megacaps into chips, security and infrastructure financing.

On central banks, the message is hawkish on both sides. The Federal Reserve left rates unchanged at its latest meeting despite elevated inflation, the Dallas Fed president is publicly pushing for hikes, and BNY expects one more increase in 2026 before the path gets murkier. The Fed also plans to vote on rates less frequently, which means fewer, bigger decision points for borrowers. President Trump, meanwhile, is linking rate cuts to balancing the federal budget. Bangko Sentral governor says the Philippines is still in a tightening phase, with high rates here to stay and no cuts yet, though officials hinted they'd ease off if the output gap turns too negative.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi closed at about 5,656, up 0.6 percent, a modest bounce after Tuesday's slide driven by fears that October inflation accelerated again. The peso weakened 0.3 percent to 63.45 to the dollar. The data backdrop is soft: S&P Global's manufacturing PMI showed factory activity in contraction for a second straight month in October, with output and new orders falling on weak demand. Why it matters: a weak economy plus rising inflation is the worst mix for a central bank already in tightening mode.

On the corporate side, Petron's nine-month profit fell 44 percent to 5.4 billion pesos, and DigiPlus profit dropped 12 percent to 8.9 billion. PLDT parted ways with SGV after 24 years and hired Isla Lipana, PwC Philippines, after SGV resigned saying it could no longer be relied on to independently check the telco's finances. Groups backed by Ramon Ang and Enrique Razon are among nine winners in the country's first waste-to-energy auction. And state auditors flagged 532 billion pesos in delayed or inefficient public works projects, fuelling calls, including from former central bank governor Jose Cuisia, for an austerity budget.

Turning to India. Indian equities ran hard. The Nifty 50 closed at about 23,441, up 1.7 percent, and the BSE Sensex at roughly 75,693, up 1.7 percent, both at around two-month highs on a fourth straight day of gains. The driver is foreign portfolio investors turning net buyers, plus easing crude prices. The rupee was flat at 94.73 to the dollar, holding on after oil slipped from six-month highs. GIFT Nifty was pointing to a softer open this morning ahead of US midterm clarity.

From the Reserve Bank, liquidity management is the story. The RBI's 50,000 crore rupee variable rate reverse repo drew a muted response, with banks bidding only about 23,905 crore, showing surplus cash is unevenly distributed across the system. At the same time, RBI foreign currency assets have dropped 55 billion dollars in seven weeks as the bank sells dollars to defend the rupee, while its dollar-rupee swap facility has pulled in 73 billion dollars in 11 weeks. Corporate bond issuance this week tops 17,000 crore rupees as companies lock in yields below 7.3 percent. And Reliance shares keep rallying on expectations the Jio Platforms listing unlocks value. Why it matters: India is importing global rate pressure but still attracting flows.

Now the world. US midterms are the big one. Democrat Roy Cooper flipped the North Carolina Senate seat, beating Republican Michael Whatley, and Jon Ossoff held Georgia for a second term, both key to the party's bid to retake the Senate. Separately, the Trump Justice Department has expanded its criminal probe into Alaska's Senate race, sending target letters to Democrats over allegations of a spoiler candidate. Why it matters: control of Congress, and the legitimacy of the count, both now sit in play, and history shows midterms often precede a strong year for the S&P 500.

In the Middle East, the US struck Iran's Qeshm Island just before the vote, with officials describing the attacks as limited and defensive. Oil has eased from six-month highs, but any escalation in the Strait of Hormuz area is an immediate inflation risk for importers like the Philippines. Trump also says he plans to end the annual 3.8 billion dollar US military aid package to Israel by year-end, a claim met with widespread scepticism.

In Ukraine, at least 38 people were killed in 24 hours as Russia struck an aid centre, a hospice and a hospital, with President Zelensky warning more may be trapped under rubble.

Europe is dealing with a security cluster. Seven flights across Poland, Spain, Bulgaria and Italy, plus an Israel-bound cruise ship, received bomb threats; searches found nothing. Cyberattacks on supermarket freezer systems hit 10 countries from Estonia to Portugal, forcing retailers to dump food. And Spain's transport minister resigned days after a train collision south of Madrid killed at least 69 people.

Finally, two business items. Binance says its 6.3 billion dollar crypto hack began with fake job interviews used to compromise an engineer, putting custody standards at the centre of the crypto infrastructure debate. And the UK and EU agreed a draft deal to scrap Brexit red tape on food exports ahead of a summit later this month.
