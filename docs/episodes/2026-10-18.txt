Good morning. It's Sunday, October 18, 2026. Today: a sharp AI-led selloff on Wall Street, a 2 percent surge on the PSEi ahead of the GCash listing, and Trump waiving Russia sanctions as cost-of-living riots spread across Europe.

Start with markets. US stocks closed lower, with the damage concentrated in technology. The Dow Jones Industrial Average finished at about 52,002, down 0.3 percent. The S&P 500 ended near 7,659, down 1.1 percent. The Nasdaq Composite was the weak link at about 26,140, down 2.2 percent. The US 10-year Treasury yield settled at 5.10 percent, up 1 basis point, and that stubbornly high yield is the backdrop to everything else: expensive money is a direct tax on long-duration tech valuations.

Big tech was almost uniformly red. Meta fell 3.7 percent to about 709 dollars, Alphabet dropped 2.5 percent to roughly 341, Tesla lost 2.1 percent to about 355, Amazon fell 1.9 percent to 250, Microsoft slid 1.9 percent to about 529, and Nvidia eased 1.4 percent to about 214. The lone gainer was Apple, up 0.4 percent to about 343, helped by reports of early demand for its 1,999-dollar foldable iPhone Duo. The driver of the selloff is the unwinding AI trade plus hotter inflation data. A Jefferies report flags rising borrowing costs for AI infrastructure, with Oracle and CoreWeave facing higher credit risk, while Morgan Stanley argues the binding constraint is electricity, not chips, projecting a 32-gigawatt power shortfall by 2028. Why it matters: the AI build-out is now a financing and power story, not just a demand story. One counterweight: South Korea's finance minister says semiconductor growth stays solid at least through 2027 on tight supply.

On central banks, the Fed picture is genuinely muddled. One report says the Federal Reserve has raised rates for the first time since 2023, while other coverage frames the October meeting as a closer call than markets think, with most still expecting a hold. Chair Kevin Warsh said price stability is the priority but offered no fresh signals on rates. Treat the October decision as live. There is no new Bangko Sentral rate decision. Analysts previewing the BSP expect a focus on curbing price pressures, the BSP has asked the Development Bank of the Philippines to show strong justification to extend regulatory relief, and it has warned the public about scammers impersonating BSP officials.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi jumped 2.11 percent to close at about 5,783, led by International Container Terminal Services, with foreign buying returning after a run of outflows. The rally was driven by bargain hunting, lower yields, and fund reallocation flows after an MSCI warning on Indonesia, with traders also positioning for the rebalancing of the MSCI Standard Philippine Index. The peso was essentially flat at 62.96 to the dollar, easing back from a record low. Why it matters: foreign money is coming back to equities even while the currency sits near its weakest-ever level.

Three stories to watch. First, Mynt, the parent of GCash, lists on the Philippine Stock Exchange this Tuesday, October 20, with brokers reporting oversubscribed allocations. It is the biggest test of domestic appetite for a fintech name this year. Second, the government has declared a national energy emergency as the war involving Iran drives Asian energy costs higher, and solar installations are booming in response. Third, the Bureau of Internal Revenue reported 2.47 trillion pesos in gross collections as of end-September, and there is a legislative push to cut OFW remittance transfer costs to 2 percent. On weather, PAGASA says Typhoon Champi is being monitored outside the Philippine Area of Responsibility.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed at about 23,146, up 0.7 percent, and the BSE Sensex at about 74,729, up 0.6 percent, capping a strong week in which the Nifty gained 2.8 percent and the Sensex 3.1 percent. The move was powered by a record 11.5 percent weekly surge in the Nifty IT index and domestic institutional buying, with the Nifty reclaiming its 200-week moving average, a level technicians treat as a trend line. The rupee was softer on the day at 94.96 to the dollar, with the dollar up 0.5 percent, though it rebounded over the week after the Reserve Bank of India stepped in to support the currency.

On the RBI, no policy move, but a busy approvals week: it issued a no-objection certificate for the PFC-REC merger and approved the Emirates NBD and RBL Bank merger effective December 1. Prime Minister Modi inaugurates the RBI's Bhubaneswar data centre on October 25, and markets are watching for further RBI tightening. Bank earnings were mixed: ICICI Bank posted second-quarter net profit of 14,843 crore rupees, up 20 percent, Central Bank of India's profit rose 21 percent on 30 percent loan growth, while IDBI Bank's profit fell 39 percent on a prior-year one-off. Why it matters: credit growth is strong, but a hawkish RBI and the looming Jio IPO will compete for liquidity.

Now to the world. The biggest geopolitical move: President Trump has temporarily waived many of the new Russia sanctions Congress passed in September under the Graham Bill, citing national security. President Zelensky called the decision wrong, and analysts say the accompanying diesel deal may do less for Moscow's finances than it does for Putin's leverage. Why it matters: it signals a softening of US pressure on Russia just as Ukraine's bargaining position weakens.

Second, cost-of-living protests swept Europe. More than half a million people marched in France, where disorder, looting and arson broke out across several cities and more than 1,000 people were arrested. Parallel demonstrations hit Belgium, Italy and Portugal. In Albania, a fifth day of clashes saw riot police fire rubber bullets at protesters demanding Prime Minister Edi Rama's resignation. Why it matters: inflation politics is now a street-level risk to European governments.

Third, energy. Iranian state television aired footage it says shows an oil tanker exploding after striking a naval mine in the Strait of Hormuz. Any disruption at Hormuz feeds straight into the Asian energy costs behind Manila's energy emergency.

Also worth your time: European diplomats were attacked by Israeli settlers in the occupied West Bank, with the Israeli military condemning the violence while the prime minister's office called it a smear campaign. At least 17 people were killed in a crush at a Hindu festival gathering in north Kolkata. And in Bangkok, US officials pressed China on market access for Mastercard and Citigroup in bilateral talks. In the US, No Kings rallies urged early voting ahead of midterms where Democrats lead candidate fundraising while Elon Musk funds Republican campaigns. Week ahead: earnings from Meta, Tesla and Netflix.

That's your Morning Briefing. Have a good Sunday.
