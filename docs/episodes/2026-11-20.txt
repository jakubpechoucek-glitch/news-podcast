Good morning. It's Friday, November 20, 2026. Today: a global AI-driven selloff hammers Wall Street and Manila, the Federal Reserve signals rate hikes rather than cuts, and Trump, Putin and Xi issue a joint peace declaration.

Start with Wall Street, where the damage was broad. The Dow closed at about 49,097, down 2.2 percent. The S&P 500 finished near 7,176, down 2.0 percent. The Nasdaq Composite ended around 23,879, down 2.3 percent. The US 10-year Treasury yield was 5.05 percent, down just 1 basis point. Why it matters: stocks are falling without a bond rally to cushion them, because inflation worries are keeping yields pinned near 5 percent.

Big tech led the decline. Amazon fell 3.9 percent to about 257 dollars, Alphabet dropped 3.5 percent to roughly 273, Nvidia lost 3.3 percent to about 146, and Tesla fell 2.4 percent to around 374. Microsoft slipped 2.0 percent to about 498, Apple eased 0.9 percent to roughly 349, and Meta was nearly flat, down 0.4 percent at about 616.

The trigger was Anthropic's record-setting Nasdaq debut. The company priced at 83 dollars a share, raising 81.3 billion dollars, the largest initial public offering ever, and the stock surged on day one to a valuation reported near 1.8 trillion dollars. Instead of lifting the sector, it pulled money out of it: SoftBank plunged more than 16 percent in Tokyo, and Asian tech sold off hard. Nvidia has its own problem. California Attorney General Rob Bonta has issued a subpoena in a probe of chip diversion to China, following CEO Jensen Huang's resignation, and Congress is pushing tighter export controls after a lawmaker said Commerce approved sales of more than 879,000 AI chips to Chinese buyers. AMD closed down over 7 percent on a short-seller report alleging its firmware appeared at a blacklisted Chinese company; AMD says it complies with US law. IMF chief Kristalina Georgieva said she expects further correction in AI-driven markets.

On central banks, minutes from the Fed's October 27-28 meeting, where rates were held at 3.75 to 4 percent, show officials expect hikes ahead and worry about AI's effect on inflation. Governor Waller said the Fed may need to "shock people" with a half-point hike, and J.P. Morgan now sees hikes at the next three meetings. That pushed the dollar to near a 19-month high and put gold on track for its worst week since March. From the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, no rate decision this week, but Barclays is warning of more BSP hikes, and Fitch says the BSP's new capital plan could raise bank risks.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi closed at about 5,314, down 3.0 percent, its weakest close since May 2020, a 6.5-year low, erasing two days of gains. The peso weakened 0.45 percent to 63.86 to the dollar. Why it matters: a 5-handle index and a 63-peso dollar together signal foreign investors pricing in slower growth and higher import costs.

Barclays has cut its Philippine growth forecast and flagged deeper debt problems, though UBS sees growth rebounding to 5.1 percent in 2027 on a recovery in infrastructure spending. One bright spot: approved building permits rose 13 percent year-on-year in September. On politics and investment, Donald Trump has arrived in the Philippines to open his Asia trip with economic deals, and US Ambassador Lee Lipton told the Pilipinas Conference that Washington remains committed to the Luzon Economic Corridor. Elon Musk is also in the country and reportedly plans projects on one or two islands. Separately, a powerful earthquake shook the Philippines, and the Agriculture sector is flagging a possible pork shortage in the first quarter of 2027. The BSP has debunked a viral video claiming banks would close today.

Turning to India, which went the other way. The Nifty 50 closed at about 25,514, up 1.1 percent, and the Sensex at roughly 83,022, up 1.2 percent, both nine-month closing highs, with Bank Nifty hitting a record. Banks, autos and consumer stocks led, helped by record foreign portfolio buying, with financial services taking about half of November's inflows. The rupee firmed 0.2 percent to 93.28 to the dollar. Why it matters: India is currently the main beneficiary of money rotating out of US and Asian tech.

The caution is inflation. Retail inflation hit a two-year high of 6.1 percent in October, and Elara Securities expects it to average above 6 percent through March, forecasting two quarter-point hikes from the Reserve Bank of India. RBI Governor's office says there is no proposal to end the FCNR(B) incentive scheme early, and Deputy Governor Murmu pushed banks on technology risk and mis-selling. Bloomberg notes rising bond yields are the key threat to the equity rally. Today's open looks weaker: GIFT Nifty is signalling a gap-down start after the global tech selloff.

Now to the world. The biggest story is diplomatic. Trump, Putin and Xi held their first trilateral meeting and released a joint "peace declaration," with Trump praising his "great relationship" with Putin. In parallel, European leaders meeting in Berlin say they have a united position and are ready for talks with Russia on ending the war in Ukraine. Why it matters: a credible path to a Ukraine settlement would reshape European energy and defence pricing.

Energy is the live risk. Houthi missile fire has struck Riyadh's financial district, escalating the Iran war, and oil prices edged higher on supply concerns, though Brent and WTI are still lower on the week as Strait of Hormuz shipments return to pre-war volumes. Trump says there is no rush on an Iran decision and is looking to next year. The International Energy Agency says the energy-security response to the conflict could cut global oil demand by 4 percent by 2035. For a net oil importer like the Philippines, that is the single biggest swing factor for inflation and the peso.

In a landmark legal ruling, the International Court of Justice found Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya in the 2017 military crackdown, the first such verdict against the country. And on corporate climate, Meta is rethinking its 2030 emissions targets as AI data centre power demand rises, part of a wider corporate retreat from climate goals. Elsewhere, Intuit is selling Mailchimp to Bending Spoons for 3.68 billion dollars, less than a third of what it paid in 2021, and Rockstar's GTA 6 has topped 2 billion dollars in sales, the biggest entertainment launch on record.

That's your Morning Briefing for Friday, November 20. Watch the peso at 63.86 and the Fed's December meeting.
