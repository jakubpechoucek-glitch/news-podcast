Good morning. It's Wednesday, October 21, 2026, and this is your Morning Briefing on Finance and Geopolitics. Today: a split Wall Street session, a record-low peso alongside the biggest IPO in Philippine history, a sharp sell-off in Indian equities, and oil easing on Middle East diplomacy.

Start with markets. Wall Street finished mixed, with value stocks doing the heavy lifting. The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at about 52,496, up 1.0 percent, led by healthcare shares that hit a record high. The S&P 500 added 0.3 percent to roughly 7,713. The Nasdaq Composite was essentially flat, down 0.02 percent at about 26,425, as technology paused after the previous session's rebound. Why it matters: money is rotating out of the AI trade and into defensives, without leaving the market altogether.

The US 10-year Treasury yield eased 2 basis points to 5.12 percent. That is still a punishing level for borrowers, and it frames everything else.

In big tech, Apple fell 1.0 percent to about 339.50, even as it added a US titanium supplier for the iPhone Duo and expanded domestic camera-component production. Alphabet dropped 1.1 percent to roughly 340, Meta was the weakest of the group, down 1.6 percent to about 694, Microsoft slipped 0.3 percent, and Nvidia lost 0.3 percent to about 216. Amazon edged up 0.1 percent. Tesla was the standout, up 1.5 percent to about 374, after Elon Musk said a Tesla–SpaceX merger would be a "good idea" and confirmed special committees at both companies are studying it. SpaceX reported third-quarter revenue up 161 percent year on year, beating estimates on AI and Starlink growth. Elsewhere, Salesforce agreed to buy Braze for 5.66 billion dollars at 47.50 a share, sending Braze up more than 50 percent, and Netflix beat on third-quarter earnings but fell on soft advertising and revenue guidance.

Now central banks. No Fed meeting this week, but the signalling is clear: a Reuters poll finds nearly all economists expect the Fed to skip October and raise rates again in December. That is after the first hike since 2023. The problem is housing. September data showed sales, construction and sentiment all falling more than expected, a market one report called in a deep freeze. Bank of America estimates each additional 1 percentage point on rates adds 95 billion dollars to national debt servicing costs. Why it matters: the Fed is tightening into a visibly weakening housing market, and the fiscal bill is mounting.

Here at home, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas is being read as one of Southeast Asia's most hawkish central banks, according to Nomura, with stubborn inflation keeping policymakers focused on higher borrowing costs despite a soft domestic economy. Deutsche Bank expects a fourth consecutive BSP rate hike. The BSP also said dollar reserves remain adequate despite a decline.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi rose 0.14 percent to about 5,806, clearing the 5,800 mark on a third straight day of gains, helped by bargain hunting and retreating oil prices. The peso, though, is the story: 63.03 to the dollar, weaker by 0.13 percent and at a record low, having broken decisively through 63. Why it matters: a record-weak peso raises import and fuel costs just as the BSP is already leaning hawkish, making another hike more likely.

The headline corporate event was Mynt, the parent of GCash, making its debut on the Philippine Stock Exchange in a record 60.9-billion-peso IPO, with President Marcos leading the listing ceremony. Shares closed flat on day one. Unicapital cautioned that Mynt's valuation needs sustained lending growth to hold up.

Three more to watch. The government is weighing privatisation of the mothballed Bataan Nuclear Power Plant after an encouraging feasibility study, and higher electricity demand is forecast for 2027. The SEC says ICTSI, San Miguel and Ayala are being courted by foreign exchanges for offshore listings, a potential drain on local liquidity. And PAGASA is tracking Super Typhoon Rosal, international name Champi, inside the Philippine Area of Responsibility, strengthening slightly over the Philippine Sea.

Turning to India. Indian equities were hit hard. The Nifty 50 fell 1.1 percent to about 22,889, and the BSE Sensex dropped 1.2 percent, or 876 points, to roughly 73,853, dragged down by private bank selling, weak global cues and earnings uncertainty. Analysts warn a break below 22,800 on the Nifty could trigger another leg down. Crude above 108 dollars a barrel is a direct headwind for an oil-importing economy.

The rupee firmed 0.24 percent to 94.77 to the dollar, with the Reserve Bank of India stepping in to support it. Rising overnight rates are expected to drive strong demand at the RBI's liquidity auction. The RBI also disclosed that a Russian court has ordered it to pay 342 million euros in penalties, and it is weighing a ban on revolving credit, prompting Bajaj Finance to launch new term-loan variants from November 1.

On the corporate and policy side, the Jio Platforms IPO, India's largest, has locked in global anchor investors and is now chasing retail money, with brokerages and non-banks offering short-term financing amid a strong grey-market premium. And the India–New Zealand free trade agreement has come into force, bringing zero-duty access for exporters and new visa categories. Why it matters: a successful Jio listing would reopen India's stalled big-ticket IPO pipeline.

Now to the world. Oil prices fell as traders bet on diplomatic progress in the Middle East, though attacks on commercial ships transiting the Strait of Hormuz have escalated in recent weeks. That retreat in crude is what lifted Philippine stocks and steadied Wall Street. UK police have re-arrested two men suspected of assisting Iranian intelligence, linked to an incident at a US-run airbase used in strikes on Iran.

On Ukraine, President Zelensky said US intelligence sharing "works now" after meeting Trump envoy Steve Witkoff in Oslo. Kyiv had accused Washington of withholding key intelligence, a claim the US called scandalous. Despite the public warmth, Trump and Zelensky still appear at odds on substance.

France is the flashpoint in Europe. Marseille saw what officials described as apocalyptic disorder, and the Interior Minister says Sentinelle military troops will be deployed for this Saturday's protests. The anger is spreading across Europe over living costs, schools and housing.

On trade, Trump has been sued over the 50 percent tariffs on Canadian goods, with a US food importer arguing he bypassed Congress and misused Section 338 of the 1930 Tariff Act, causing immediate harm to American businesses. Separately, Alberta voted overwhelmingly to reject separatism, which Prime Minister Carney called a great result for Canada.

And on US politics, Trump has committed more than 400 million dollars ahead of November's midterms, while Michael Bloomberg has spent 80 million dollars backing Democrats. Control of Congress will shape the final two years of this presidency. Finally, Washington has banned DeepSeek technology from federal civilian agency systems, widening the US–China AI rift.

That's your briefing. Trade carefully, and stay safe if you're in Rosal's path.
