Good morning. It's Wednesday, October 7, 2026. Wall Street closed at fresh records as oil cooled and yields slipped, while Manila went the other way on a hot inflation print — and India waits on the Reserve Bank.

To markets. The S&P 500 climbed 0.6 percent to about 7,819, a record closing high. The Nasdaq Composite rose 0.5 percent to roughly 27,600, also a record close, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 0.5 percent to about 51,521. The driver was a combination of stabilising crude prices and a pullback in borrowing costs: the US 10-year Treasury yield fell 4 basis points to 5.27 percent, easing back from its highest level since 2002. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent reinforced the move by insisting the government's debt load can be tamed, though BNP Paribas warned that scrapping the 20-year bond, as some have urged, could push yields higher instead. Why it matters: the entire equity rally is resting on yields staying off their highs.

Among big tech, Amazon was the standout, up 2.0 percent to about 256 dollars after winning global rights to stream the Emmy Awards free on Prime Video for six years starting in 2027, pulling the gala off US broadcast television. Microsoft gained 0.8 percent to about 529, Tesla rose 0.5 percent to about 381, Alphabet added 0.4 percent to about 348 even as Finland ordered a halt to work on two planned Google data centres over forest clearance concerns, Apple edged up 0.2 percent to about 334, and Nvidia was nearly flat at about 239. Meta slipped 0.4 percent to about 739, the lone decliner, though demand for its Muse personal agent is lifting AMD and Intel shares to fresh highs. Also worth noting: Marvell raised its 2028 revenue forecast to around 20 billion dollars on AI data centre demand, and its shares jumped roughly 9 percent.

On central banks, there was no Federal Reserve rate decision, but the signals are hawkish. Vice Chair Michelle Bowman announced an overhaul of bank supervision, creating five new geographic supervisory regions to improve accountability. The San Francisco Fed president warned that AI-driven power demand could extend the energy shock, and commentary is building that high rates simply aren't slowing the AI boom — a problem for policymakers. Meanwhile the US trade deficit widened 13.7 percent from July to 105.6 billion dollars, the widest since just before last year's tariffs and above the 102 billion consensus. At home, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas has not moved, but Standard Chartered now expects one more hike this October after September's inflation spike. Separately, the BSP ordered a freeze on funds linked to two designated terrorists, giving banks until October 12 to report related transactions.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi fell 1.2 percent to about 5,677, dropping back below 5,700 after September inflation sizzled to a more than three-year high, fuelling bets on a BSP rate hike. The peso was essentially unchanged at 62.58 to the dollar. Two forecast cuts landed on the same day: AMRO lowered its 2026 Philippine growth forecast to 3.3 percent from 4.1 percent, and the World Bank also trimmed its outlook, citing war-driven energy costs and an AI divide that leaves the Philippines paying more for power while gaining less from the AI boom. Why it matters: slower growth plus rising prices narrows the BSP's room to manoeuvre. On the brighter side, PEZA investment approvals surged 92 percent from January to September, already beating the full-year 2025 tally and closing in on the 300-billion-peso target. Investors are also watching the GCash parent Mynt IPO, at 60.9 billion pesos the largest in Philippine history. President Marcos heads to Singapore to court investors, the BIR has imposed a 2 percent withholding tax on electricity sales, and Moody's completed its purchase of a 19.99 percent stake in PhilRatings.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 rose 0.6 percent to about 22,556 and the BSE Sensex gained 0.7 percent to about 72,382, a second straight session of recovery, with the Sensex up 685 points and nearly 5 lakh crore rupees added to BSE market capitalisation. The rupee was steady at 96.36 to the dollar. The rally was driven by crude dropping below 100 dollars a barrel, strong second-quarter business updates and domestic institutional buying, led by capital goods, banks and pharma. All eyes are on the Reserve Bank of India, whose Monetary Policy Committee decision lands today. A hike is widely expected — the debate is 25 or 50 basis points — and swap markets signal real risk of an outsized move as West Asian conflict feeds inflation. The RBI is also draining surplus liquidity with a 3 lakh crore rupee variable rate reverse repo auction, and foreign investors have been paring Bank Nifty shorts ahead of the decision. Elsewhere, the World Bank raised its India growth forecast to 7.1 percent for the 2027 fiscal year, while Moody's warned India will become an aged society within 16 years, getting old before it gets rich.

To the world. Energy stays the central geopolitical risk. The US Energy Information Administration lifted its Brent forecast to about 98 dollars a barrel for 2026 as the Iran war tightens supply, and nearly 20 commercial ships, mostly tankers, have been attacked in the past month transiting the Strait of Hormuz. Goldman Sachs says tight refining capacity will keep diesel prices elevated through 2027. Why it matters: Philippine and Indian inflation are both hostage to that chokepoint.

France is the other pressure point. UBS chief executive Sergio Ermotti says "hard measures" are needed to tackle France's spiralling debt crisis as turmoil worsens, while tear gas was fired in Paris and Marseille as student protests over school funding spread nationwide. The euro has plunged against the dollar.

In corporate news, Paramount completed its 110-billion-dollar takeover of Warner Bros, putting CNN and CBS News under one roof. The combined company, Skydance, began trading Tuesday — expect scrutiny over competition and streaming prices. In defence tech, Anduril won a 2.9-billion-dollar Navy submarine contract days after founder Palmer Luckey joined a Pentagon weapons group, and will invest 3.7 billion dollars in a shipyard.

Elsewhere: a ship sank with crew missing after a drone attack in the Black Sea as Russia intensifies strikes. Germany arrested former spy chief August Hanning on espionage and treason charges. Saudi Arabia's Jazan and Najran airports were hit as Yemen fighting escalates. Brazilian stocks jumped with Jair Bolsonaro now seen as the favourite ahead of the October 25 runoff against Lula. And on health, Kenya confirmed its first Ebola death as the outbreak spreads from DR Congo, where more than 4,000 have died this year, while the US CDC says it is closely monitoring a suspected plague case involving a Siberian lab worker.
