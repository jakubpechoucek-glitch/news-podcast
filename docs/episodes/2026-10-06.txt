Good morning, it's Tuesday, October 6, 2026. Here's your Morning Briefing on finance and geopolitics: a tech-led rally on Wall Street, a 2 percent bounce in Manila, and a plague scare in Siberia that has Washington watching.

Starting with markets. US stocks closed higher across the board, with the gains concentrated in technology. The Dow Jones Industrial Average finished at about 51,268, up 0.2 percent. The S&P 500 closed near 7,774, up 0.7 percent. The Nasdaq Composite led, ending around 27,477, up 1.1 percent. But bonds did not play along: the US 10-year Treasury yield rose 3 basis points to 5.31 percent, keeping borrowing costs and the dollar firm. Why it matters: equities are rallying in spite of a 5-handle on the 10-year, which leaves valuations exposed if yields keep climbing.

Among the megacaps, Tesla was the standout, up 2.2 percent to about 379 dollars. Nvidia rose 2.1 percent to roughly 239, even as its 20 billion dollar licensing deal with Groq drew a lawsuit alleging the startup's stockholders were squeezed out at a lowball price. Meta gained 1.9 percent to about 742, and Microsoft added 1.5 percent to roughly 525 amid renewed focus on whether Satya Nadella can repeat his cloud transformation in the AI era. Alphabet rose 0.9 percent to about 346. The laggards were small: Apple slipped 0.2 percent to about 333, and Amazon was essentially flat, down 0.1 percent at about 251. Elsewhere in tech, Cerebras jumped 9 percent after OpenAI's Sam Altman called the chipmaker a close partner, and SpaceX shares hit their highest since June, up almost 60 percent from their August low and returning Elon Musk to trillionaire status. Nokia's chief executive said data centres would be built twice as fast without supply constraints. Why it matters: the AI trade is still the single biggest driver of index direction.

On central banks, there is no fresh decision from either the Federal Reserve or the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas. The market story is that traders have sharply cut bets on an October Fed rate hike, which helped stocks and steadied gold, though a stronger dollar and high yields capped the metal's gains. Inflation worries persist, and there is growing debate about whether higher rates risk a doom loop in US housing. In Manila, Manulife Investments Philippines argued the BSP should avoid hiking rates primarily to defend the peso and keep policy focused on inflation. Why it matters: both central banks are being squeezed between sticky inflation and currency pressure.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi snapped a five-day losing streak, jumping 2.0 percent to close at about 5,743 as bargain hunters picked up index heavyweights, helped by those easing Fed hike bets. The peso went the other way, weakening slightly to 62.58 to the dollar. Three things to watch locally. First, inflation: a poll of economists sees September inflation hitting a five-month high on higher oil prices and storm damage, and that print is the key input for the BSP's next move. Second, fuel: gasoline and kerosene prices rose effective today, October 6, adding to that same inflation pressure. Third, capital markets: GCash has earmarked 5.7 billion pesos of its IPO proceeds for lending, while Robinsons Retail trading was halted as the Gokongwei group completed a share transfer ahead of delisting. Also on the radar, government infrastructure spending eased to 89.6 billion pesos in July on weak public works disbursements, and the Chinese coast guard blocked a Philippine team pursuing a suspected blast fisher in disputed waters. Why it matters: a weak peso plus rising fuel costs makes the BSP's job harder just as growth support is needed.

Now to the world. First, defence. President Trump said a threat prompted the Pentagon to pull US B-1 long-range bombers out of RAF Fairford in England and return them to home stations in the United States. The Pentagon confirmed the withdrawal on Sunday. Those aircraft have been used in strikes on Iran. Why it matters: it signals an unusual force-protection concern inside a core NATO ally.

Second, a public health story with market relevance. Russian authorities have quarantined a hospital in Irkutsk in eastern Siberia after a lab technician died of a suspected pneumonic plague case, with as many as 189 people under medical observation and around 200 contacts being traced. Trump said the US is watching very closely and is ready to help. Why it matters: any sign of spread from a research facility is a tail risk markets price violently and late.

Third, Brazil. The presidential first round on Sunday delivered major gains for Bolsonaro, setting up a tough runoff against incumbent Lula da Silva on October 25. Brazilian stocks jumped with Bolsonaro now seen as the heavy favourite. Why it matters: it is the clearest emerging-market risk trade of the month.

Fourth, energy and shipping. Zelensky condemned a Russian strike on a Turkish-owned vessel carrying corn in the Black Sea; the captain was killed and 11 crew were rescued. Separately, Middle East crude exports have exceeded pre-war levels even as tanker attacks increase. Why it matters: grain and crude are flowing, but the risk premium on freight and insurance is building again.

Briefly on the rest. In Britain, the average five-year fixed mortgage rate hit 6 percent for the first time in three years as lenders face higher funding costs, and BT agreed a rescue deal to buy broadband operator TalkTalk. In Washington, Trump named his national intelligence chief to head a new AI taskforce, while the Pentagon has stopped using Anthropic's AI tools after blacklisting the company as a supply chain risk in February over its refusal to remove safety guardrails. France braces for a national day of school protests after injuries and mass arrests. And the Nobel Prize in medicine went to Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel for revealing the inner workings of the brain.
