Good morning. It's Thursday, October 15, 2026. Today: a hot US inflation print knocks the AI trade, the PSEi slips, Jio sets its IPO price band, and Iran's attacks on Hormuz shipping claim another vessel.

Start with Wall Street. The Dow closed at about 51,621, down 0.1 percent. The S&P 500 fell 1.1 percent to about 7,719, and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 1.9 percent to about 26,693. The trigger was September's consumer price index: inflation ran at 3.7 percent year on year, above the 3.6 percent consensus, driven by higher motor fuel costs. The 10-year Treasury yield actually slipped 3 basis points to 5.19 percent, as money moved out of equities and into bonds. Why it matters: at 5.19 percent, borrowing costs are punishing anything that runs on leverage, and that is now the market's core anxiety.

Big tech took the hit. Meta fell 3.3 percent to about 738 dollars, Nvidia 3.2 percent to 221, Tesla 2.2 percent to 367, Microsoft 1.8 percent to 534, and Alphabet and Amazon each 1.7 percent, to 348 and 254. Apple bucked the trend, up 0.5 percent to 343 alongside a new home hub launch. The real damage was in AI infrastructure: CoreWeave sank 15 percent with its bond yields pushing toward 15 percent, and Dell plunged 11 percent after Barclays downgraded it along with HPE and NetApp on lagging enterprise AI adoption. Roughly 80 billion dollars of AI-linked market value was erased in a single session.

In banks and earnings, Morgan Stanley beat on both lines, with equities trading revenue 909 million dollars above expectations. Bank of America fell on a revenue miss plus a 1.2 billion dollar Treasury fine. And Netflix is cutting about 1,000 jobs, 6 percent of its workforce, its steepest since 2022.

Central banks. No Fed meeting this week, but the signals are split. New York Fed President John Williams sees one more hike in 2026 and none in 2027. Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman, speaking at the IMF meetings in Bangkok, said there's no urgency for another increase. Traders are more hawkish than both, and the next decision lands on the eve of the US midterms. At the Bangko Sentral, no rate move this week, but markets are now pricing two more BSP hikes with domestic inflation heading toward a three-year high.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi fell 1.5 percent to about 5,617, snapping a two-day rally, with a sell-off in ICTSI leading the decline and an IMF warning on growth risks weighing on sentiment. The peso firmed 0.1 percent to 62.73 to the dollar. The Bangko Sentral adopted a new global standard for operational risk capital, forcing banks to hold more against fraud, cyberattacks and operational failures, with full implementation by December 2027. Why it matters: higher capital charges for the biggest lenders, phased in over two years. The BSP also forecasts a balance of payments deficit of 9.2 billion dollars for 2026 and another in 2027, which is a standing source of pressure on the peso. Elsewhere, InstaPay and PESONet transfers have now hit 25 trillion pesos, with the BSP unimpressed by RCBC's conditional free-transfer offer, and the SEC has warned the public against the Maharlika Play investment scheme.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed up 1.6 percent at about 22,722 and the Sensex up 1.5 percent at about 73,034, rebounding on hopes of long-term capital gains tax relief after losses driven by crude, inflation and foreign selling. The rupee firmed 0.15 percent to 95.14 to the dollar after the Reserve Bank unveiled currency-defence measures, including a special forex facility for state refiners Indian Oil, HPCL and BPCL. The RBI's policy committee raised the repo rate 25 basis points to 5.5 percent and said near-term cuts are off the table; new cash reserve rules knocked non-bank lender shares down as much as 5 percent. The dominant corporate story is Jio Platforms: a price band of 1,065 to 1,119 rupees, the offer running October 21 to 23, raising up to 30,213 crore rupees. That would be India's largest-ever IPO, with grey-market premium near 20 percent. Zetwerk, which pulled in 2,251 crore rupees from anchors including BlackRock, Goldman Sachs and Nomura, opens today.

Now the world. A container ship sank in the Strait of Hormuz as Iran escalates strikes on commercial vessels, trying to choke off a rebound in crude exports. Why it matters: that is the thread running straight back to US pump prices and the 3.7 percent inflation print.

On Ukraine, Elon Musk said any limits on Starlink access are a decision for the US government, not his company. Kyiv says service has improved but speed limits are still disrupting battlefield missions. Separately, Germany has put Ukrainian national Serhii Kuznetsov on trial over the Nord Stream pipeline explosions four years ago.

In Lebanon, an Israeli quadcopter drone dropped a bomb on the outskirts of the southern village of Zibqin, a reminder that the ceasefire there remains fragile.

In the Americas, families of Trinidadian men killed in a US boat strike are demanding accountability. At least 239 people have now been killed in those strikes, which critics call extrajudicial killings. And in Bogota, thousands marched against President de la Espriella's austerity cuts and ties with Israel, with former president Gustavo Petro joining.

In tech and legal news, OpenAI is being sued by 8 US states over ChatGPT safety, particularly child protection, while Saudi engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan has been arrested by the FBI over an alleged bugging plot, after booking a one-way ticket to Saudi Arabia with his family. Meanwhile JPMorgan has blocked outside AI agents from directly accessing customer accounts, with Jamie Dimon warning about rogue agents at scale, and Bank of America hired 1,000 contractors to patch flaws exposed by Anthropic's Mythos. Why it matters: that's a rare, concrete price tag on AI defence.

Finally, Kenya has confirmed a second Ebola case, a relative of the first victim who had travelled from the Democratic Republic of Congo. Wildfires have turned the sky orange across Indonesia's Central Kalimantan. And in Tirana, Albanian student protests over university conditions and job prospects turned violent, with government buildings set alight.

That's your Morning Briefing for Thursday, October 15. Watch Hormuz, watch the AI credit market, and have a good day.
