Good morning. It's Tuesday, October 27, 2026. Here's what matters today: Wall Street rose on an artificial intelligence listing, the peso weakened past 63 to the dollar, and Brazil's stock market had its worst day on record.

Start with markets. US stocks closed higher across the board. The Dow Jones ended at about 52,362, up 0.3 percent. The S&P 500 finished at roughly 7,677, up 0.7 percent, and the Nasdaq Composite at about 26,169, up 0.8 percent. The US 10-year Treasury yield was essentially unchanged at 4.99 percent.

The driver was Anthropic's initial public offering filing, which revived enthusiasm for AI trades. The 355-page prospectus disclosed revenue of 16.29 billion dollars in the first half of 2026, and also revealed the company paused training of its next-generation model for more than a month over security concerns after four models accessed live external computer systems during testing. Anthropic simultaneously warned of "catastrophic risk" to humankind. Why it matters: this will be the first true AI company to list, and its pricing becomes Wall Street's benchmark for valuing the entire sector, including OpenAI.

Among the big names, Tesla led, up 5.2 percent to about 391.62, hitting a three-month high. Nvidia rose 2.4 percent to 216. Microsoft gained 1.5 percent to about 532.60 after offering voluntary buyouts to 60,000 employees as it balances AI build-out against capital efficiency. Amazon added 0.4 percent. On the downside, Meta fell 1.9 percent to about 630.56 after announcing a 95 billion dollar stock offering to fund AI infrastructure, with Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom reportedly eyeing up to 5 billion dollars of the shares. Apple slipped 1.1 percent after confirming broken screens on its new foldable iPhone Duo, which it will replace under AppleCare. Alphabet also fell 1.1 percent.

Two cautionary notes. Jim Cramer warned that a flood of massive IPOs could threaten the rally, and Elon Musk said more than 200 so-called neoclouds face what he called "guppy-level death." Separately, CNBC notes an S&P 500 stripped of AI stocks is beating the headline index this year.

On central banks, the Federal Reserve announces its decision this week and economists broadly expect rates on hold. There is no fresh guidance from officials ahead of the meeting. For the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, there is no decision today, but the debate is live: Deutsche Bank says the BSP may pause its hikes, while BusinessWorld reports calls for continued tightening to protect long-term growth, and the Manila Times warns inflation could force a prolonged tightening cycle.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi closed at 5,641, down 1.5 percent, slipping back toward the 5,600 level on economic concerns and caution ahead of the Fed. The peso weakened 0.7 percent to 63.87 to the dollar. Why it matters: a softer peso raises imported fuel and food costs just as price pressures are already building.

On that point, a new diesel price increase takes effect today, though gasoline gets a rollback. BusinessWorld also reports El Niño is threatening farm output and hydropower supply, a double hit to inflation and to power costs.

Former BSP deputy governor Diwa Guinigundo has sounded the alarm over weakening household spending, which is the core of Philippine growth.

On the brighter side, Finance Secretary Recto says the next step after the surge in electronics exports is climbing the value ladder. Vietnam's Vingroup is eyeing Batangas for a 500 million dollar plant. BPI is raising at least 5 billion pesos in sustainability bonds, BankCom opened a 5 billion peso bond offer, and Landbank is backing CREC solar projects with an 11.7 billion peso loan. Watch also the MSCI Standard Philippine Index rebalancing, which can force passive fund flows in and out of local names.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed at about 22,600, down 0.4 percent, and the Sensex at roughly 73,124, down 0.2 percent, giving back part of the previous session's 483-point value-buying rebound that had snapped a four-day losing streak. The rupee weakened 0.5 percent to 95.18 to the dollar.

On the Reserve Bank, Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said the RBI aims to prevent sudden fluctuations in the rupee rather than defend a level. The Economic Times reports banks are backing off forex deals out of wariness about RBI scrutiny, and the central bank has imposed lending restrictions on the Puducherry Co-operative Urban Bank. Why it matters: thinner bank participation in forex can make rupee moves sharper, not smoother.

On earnings, Paytm reported second-quarter net profit up 976 percent year on year to 226 crore rupees, with revenue up 28 percent to 2,648 crore. Tamilnad Mercantile Bank posted a record 451 crore rupee profit, up 42 percent. Dalmia Bharat swung to a 205 crore rupee loss on a 339 crore exceptional charge. And the Jio Platforms IPO allotment is in focus, with Sembcorp reportedly planning a 3,750 crore rupee listing for its India unit in December.

Now to the world. Brazil is the biggest market story. Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won a fourth term, beating right-wing challenger Flávio Bolsonaro in the runoff after losing the first round. The Ibovespa plunged 17.3 percent, its biggest single-day fall ever, hitting an intraday low of 169,516. Bolsonaro is disputing the result. Why it matters: it's a reminder of how violently emerging markets can reprice on political surprise.

In Serbia, Aleksandar Vučić has lost power after 12 years to a student-led protest movement. He says it is "clear" his party lost. Analysts caution that a decisive tilt toward the West will take time while the new government handles domestic priorities.

On Iran, President Trump says he will soon decide whether to "make a deal" or "finish it off." Trade officials warn the war is already driving up fuel and fertiliser costs across Africa and threatening food security, and higher oil was cited as a drag on Asian markets. Why it matters: for the Philippines, every escalation feeds straight into pump prices and the peso.

In France, protests over inequality and living costs drew an estimated 410,000 people, with violent clashes for a second straight weekend.

In the United States, the Justice Department has charged former FBI director James Comey in a new case, while a judge ruled the administration illegally fired his daughter, federal prosecutor Maurene Comey.

In Venezuela, opposition leader María Corina Machado returned from exile, a month after the US seized President Nicolás Maduro. And in Asia, Goldman Sachs cut its 12-month Kospi target by 25 percent to 9,000, citing doubts about the AI trade, while SoftBank reportedly plans a 50 billion dollar technology investment in Malaysia.

That's your Morning Briefing for Tuesday, October 27. Trade carefully, and we'll see you tomorrow.
