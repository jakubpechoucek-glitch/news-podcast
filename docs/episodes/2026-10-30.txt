Good morning. It's Friday, the 30th of October, 2026. Today: Alphabet's worst session on record drags Wall Street lower, the Federal Reserve holds rates steady, and the peso claws its way back toward 63.

Start with the US close. The Dow Jones finished at about 52,343, down 0.3 percent. The S&P 500 closed at about 7,720, also down 0.3 percent. The Nasdaq Composite took the real damage, ending at about 26,185, down 1.4 percent. The US 10-year Treasury yield fell 6 basis points to 4.99 percent, a bid for safety as equities wobbled. Why it matters: the selling was concentrated in artificial intelligence names, not spread across the market, so this is a positioning story rather than a growth scare.

The single biggest mover was Alphabet, down 12.6 percent to about 284 dollars, its worst day ever, wiping roughly 500 billion dollars off its value and taking the stock to a six-month low. The trigger: a revenue miss, a larger-than-expected negative free cash flow, unchanged heavy spending guidance for the year, and an admission that its AI search summaries are eating into advertising revenue. A regulatory filing also flagged nearly 98.5 billion dollars in contingent commitments, including data-centre leases and chip repurchase obligations. Amazon fell 4.3 percent to about 244 dollars after posting negative free cash flow of 42.4 billion dollars and holding 2026 capital spending unchanged, even though AWS revenue grew 39 percent and signed deals with Meta and OpenAI.

The rest of big tech split. Meta rose 2 percent to about 625. Apple gained 1.5 percent to about 349. Microsoft slipped 0.2 percent to about 530, Tesla eased 0.2 percent to about 403, and Nvidia fell 0.8 percent to about 214 while disputing a New York Times report that US intelligence surveilled a person close to chief executive Jensen Huang over spying concerns. Nvidia says the diversion allegations were investigated and debunked. Elsewhere, ServiceNow collapsed 27 percent, its worst day ever, on results that revived AI disruption fears; Intel dropped 16 percent, blaming a weak PC market for soft guidance; and Reddit sank on weak sales guidance as Google search traffic shrinks. Starbucks missed on overall sales but beat on US comparable sales. Rivian beat estimates and raised its 2026 outlook.

On central banks: the Federal Reserve left interest rates unchanged, with inflation still stubborn and the US economy still growing — and with midterm elections looming. Chairman Kevin Warsh is also shaking up the Fed's process, cutting the number of scheduled rate decisions each year; investors warn that "forward silence" could mean bigger swings in the 32 trillion dollar Treasury market. The European Central Bank held at 2.5 percent, read as a hawkish pause, with markets pricing a 67 percent chance of a December hike. There was no fresh policy decision from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, though BSP officials continue to flag external headwinds complicating efforts to steady the peso.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi rebounded 0.9 percent to close at about 5,706 on bargain hunting, with the Fed's hold giving local risk appetite some cover. The peso strengthened 0.4 percent to 63.73 to the dollar, back at the 63 handle, though analysts note the external pressures behind the recent slide are unresolved. Three stories to watch. First, PAGCOR revenues fell 23.4 percent from January to September, with online gaming posting the steepest drop — a direct hit to a government revenue line. Second, the operator of hard-discount chain DALI slipped into a capital deficit after more than 7 billion pesos in losses as of 2025, raising real questions about whether the discount model works here. Third, politics: WR Advisory Group's new country report warns of a deteriorating outlook for political stability and fiscal risk, the Supreme Court has ordered the Senate and House to comment on the challenge to the Vice President Duterte impeachment vote rule, and the Judicial and Bar Council has sent President Marcos a shortlist of five chief justice nominees. On the brighter side, Jollibee is eyeing 1,500 areas for expansion and the Sy group has pitched a 700 billion peso plan to cut power costs.

Turning to India. Indian equities gave back part of Thursday's big rally. The Nifty 50 closed at about 22,699, down 0.2 percent, and the BSE Sensex at about 73,246, down 0.5 percent — this after Thursday's rebound of 905 points on the Sensex and 238 on the Nifty, driven by the Fed hold, softer crude and institutional buying. The rupee was essentially flat at 95.21 to the dollar. The Reserve Bank of India has been intervening to support the currency, and commentators are now debating the cost of its October rupee-defence package. Separately, the Supreme Court told the RBI that its rules must compel banks to compensate "digital arrest" scam victims by not less than the amount lost, and Punjab National Bank received an RBI penalty order. On the economy, the Finance Ministry estimates July-to-September growth at 7.35 percent, a moderation but still strong, while flagging oil price volatility as the key risk. In corporate news, Jio Platforms reports its first results since listing today; Ambuja Cements profit slumped 90 percent to 236 crore rupees on higher fuel and logistics costs; and Sagility's promoter is selling a 12 percent stake worth about 2,377 crore rupees.

Now to the world. In Israel, the final count confirms no party reached the 61 seats needed for a majority, and Netanyahu's coalition has lost its majority. His Likud party alleges foreign interference and says Arab-led parties "stole" the election, while the opposition accuses him of trying to delegitimise the result and explores its own coalition options. Why it matters: a prolonged deadlock in Israel adds risk to an already unstable region.

In Ukraine, a Russian drone attack on a train station in Dnipro killed at least 14 people and wounded 14 more, including an 11-year-old boy, even as a temporary ceasefire brought rare quiet to Kherson. On Iran, an official says the latest mediation effort to end the war contains only unacceptable ideas — the main live upside risk for oil, and the reason Indian officials keep naming crude as their top vulnerability. Big Oil earnings land next, with analysts saying guidance matters more than the headline numbers.

In Europe, leaked audio from Spain's deadly high-speed train crash appears to show one train was cleared to move at normal speed while another had stalled ahead. And at a Montreal summit, EU chief Ursula von der Leyen laid out a vision for a new partnership with Canada, still using the contested term "associate member" over objections from Germany and others. Vladimir Putin, meanwhile, hosted African leaders in Moscow. In Brazil, defeated presidential candidate Flávio Bolsonaro called himself "leader of Brazil" and says he will run the opposition from the United States.
