Good morning. It's Monday, October 26, 2026, and here's your Morning Briefing on finance and geopolitics. Today: a bruising Friday on Wall Street ahead of the Fed, the peso slips past 63.50, and Serbia's long-time strongman concedes.

Starting with markets. US stocks closed broadly lower, with the damage concentrated in the old economy rather than tech. The Dow Jones Industrial Average finished at about 52,215, down 1.2 percent. The S&P 500 closed near 7,624, down 0.5 percent. The Nasdaq Composite held up best at roughly 25,955, down just 0.2 percent. The US 10-year Treasury yield rose 3 basis points to 4.99 percent, pressing right up against the 5 percent line. Why it matters: a 10-year at 5 percent raises the discount rate on every risk asset on the planet, including Philippine equities.

Among the megacaps, Microsoft was the lone gainer, up 0.7 percent to about 524.51. Apple was effectively flat, down 0.1 percent at 347.73. Nvidia fell 0.6 percent to 210.96, Tesla lost 0.6 percent to 372.22, Amazon dropped 1.0 percent to 248, Meta fell 1.0 percent to 642.62, and Alphabet was the worst of the group, down 1.2 percent to 320.21. The driver in the headlines is what CNBC calls AI funding fears: a rotation out of AI names and into defensives, with particular scrutiny of Nvidia's new role as a financier of the AI buildout. As so-called neocloud operators take a market hit, some investors argue Nvidia is carrying outsized risk tied to customers it is also helping to fund. That thesis gets tested this week, with third-quarter results due from Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon.

Now to central banks. The Federal Reserve meets tomorrow and Wednesday, October 27 and 28, and the consensus in the coverage is a hold, described by one wire as a hawkish pause, delivered just days before the US midterms. Economists remain genuinely split on whether the Fed's next move this year is a hike rather than a cut. There is no fresh guidance from Fed officials over the weekend beyond that expectation.

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas is the more active story. After a surprise 50-basis-point hike, foreign banks now expect more tightening: BPI says the cycle is likely not over, Nomura sees another 50 basis points in December and further tightening into 2027, while a separate poll points to a 25-basis-point move. Rate cut hopes for this cycle have effectively been shelved as the BSP prioritises its inflation fight. Why it matters: borrowing costs in Manila are going up, not down, and that is already showing in equities.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi closed at about 5,729, down 0.9 percent, underperforming the region. The peso weakened 0.26 percent to 63.58 to the dollar. Analysts quoted locally expect another vulnerable week, with BSP tightening weighing on sentiment and traders waiting on the Fed. There is also an MSCI Standard Philippine Index rebalancing in focus, which can force passive flows regardless of fundamentals.

Three stories to watch. First, the fiscal picture: the Supreme Court has declared controversial allocations in the 2024, 2025 and parts of the 2026 national budgets unconstitutional, and the question now is what happens to those funds and to agency spending plans. Second, infrastructure spending slowed again in August, with the Public Works department held back by ongoing project bidding, uncashed checks and flooding across parts of Luzon. Why it matters: public construction has been a main growth engine, and two drags at once, judicial and operational, trim the fourth-quarter outlook. Third, the BSP confirms it remains in active talks with British fintech Revolut over a digital banking licence, with no firm timeline. Elsewhere, the Department of Agriculture wants to put about 80 billion pesos of farm-to-market roads under public-private partnership, and the World Bank says the wealth gap is widening even as poverty falls.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed at 22,600, down 0.4 percent, and the BSE Sensex at about 73,124, down 0.2 percent. The rupee weakened 0.23 percent to 94.93 to the dollar. Last week was ugly: Indian equities fell over 2 percent, the Sensex shed more than 1,600 points, and the Nifty is now down about 13.6 percent year-to-date, described locally as the worst Samvat of the Modi era. Eight of the top 10 listed firms lost a combined 1.55 trillion rupees in market value, led by HDFC Bank and Bajaj Finance. Foreign portfolio investors have pulled roughly 48,056 crore rupees from Indian equities in October alone, driven by elevated crude prices. Technical desks put Nifty support near 22,300 to 22,500 and resistance at 22,850 to 22,900, with a Brent crude spike capping any upside.

On the Reserve Bank of India, no rate signal, but Prime Minister Modi inaugurated the RBI's new data centre and cybersecurity training institute in Bhubaneswar, with the governor stressing that all data centres will remain sovereign and Modi warning that cyberattacks can target the economy itself. On the corporate side, allotment for the Jio Platforms IPO lands today; the deal was subscribed more than 22 times, so many applicants will get nothing and see blocked funds released. And a bright spot: India's business activity hit a four-month high in October, though hiring has nearly stalled.

Now to the world. In Serbia, Aleksandar Vučić has conceded, saying it is clear his party lost a vote billed as a referendum on the country's future, ending 12 years of rule and handing victory to a student-led protest movement. Why it matters: a rare peaceful turnover in the Balkans, and a signal to markets about political risk repricing in emerging Europe.

In the Gulf, Saudi Arabia says it intercepted missiles and drones launched by Iran-backed Houthis, who claim attacks on airports and energy sites. That is the clearest proximate driver of the crude spike now hurting Indian equities and importers like the Philippines.

In France, protests over inequality and living costs drew an estimated 410,000 people, with clashes in several cities for a second straight weekend, while Marine Le Pen launched her presidential campaign promising a French renaissance. Why it matters: the far right contesting the Élysée against a backdrop of street unrest is a live sovereign risk for the euro area.

Brazil voted in a closely watched runoff between President Lula and Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, with voting extended by two hours. In Ukraine, Elon Musk says SpaceX restricted Starlink access, accusing Kyiv of violating terms of service through military use; Ukraine disputes that, saying commercial limits already prevented long-range strike use. Service has since been restored. And Venezuelan opposition leader María Machado has returned from exile, a month after the US seized President Nicolás Maduro. Also notable for Manila: US Secretary of War Pete Hegseth visits the Philippines for the ASEAN defence meetings, days after Philippine, Japanese and US forces wrapped up drills in the West Philippine Sea.

That's your Morning Briefing for Monday, October 26. Trade carefully.
