Good morning. It's Thursday, October 8, 2026, and here's your Morning Briefing on finance and geopolitics. Today: Fed minutes point to one more hike, the PSEi bounces back, and the RBI turns hawkish.

Start with Wall Street, which closed lower and off record highs as Treasury yields climbed. The Dow Jones finished at about 51,180, down 0.7 percent. The S&P 500 ended at about 7,802, down 0.2 percent, pulling back after returning to an intraday record on Tuesday. The Nasdaq Composite closed at about 27,539, also down 0.2 percent. The US 10-year Treasury yield settled at 5.28 percent, up 1 basis point, backing off a 24-year high after a solid 10-year note auction eased demand fears. Why it matters: the entire global risk trade right now is hostage to that yield, and 5.28 percent is the highest cost of money in a generation.

Among the big-tech names, Meta was the standout loser, down 2.4 percent. Tesla fell 0.8 percent and Nvidia 0.7 percent. On the upside, Amazon rose 1.4 percent, helped by news the FCC will vote on auctioning 25 megahertz of prime spectrum, a move that could benefit both Amazon and SpaceX in direct-to-device satellite services. Apple gained 0.9 percent and Alphabet 0.8 percent, despite Finland ordering Google to halt work on two data centres. Microsoft was essentially flat, up 0.1 percent, as it unveiled a 2,599-dollar Surface Laptop Ultra containing an Nvidia AI chip. Elsewhere in AI financing, Apollo and banks are in talks to fund SpaceX's 40-billion-dollar Nvidia GPU purchase, and China's DeepSeek is weighing doubling its funding round to as much as 15 billion dollars.

Now to central banks. The Federal Reserve released minutes from its September 15 and 16 meeting, the one where it raised rates for the first time since 2023. Most officials see another hike coming before year-end, but there was no urgency for an October move and no appetite for a series of increases. Policymakers were openly divided on the logic for tightening. Why it matters: a hawkish-but-unhurried Fed keeps yields elevated and the dollar strong, which is exactly what is squeezing Asian currencies. Related: foreign investors pulled 26.3 billion dollars out of emerging-market stocks and bonds in September, the first outflow since June. Gold fell 2.3 percent to 4,066 dollars an ounce, a two-month low, on the stronger dollar and higher yields. Oil settled lower after the IEA agreed to accelerate the release of stocks, and copper edged higher on a Chilean mine strike. Singapore's Temasek warned that an unwinding of the AI trade is the single biggest risk facing markets, while IMF chief Kristalina Georgieva said AI is lifting growth hopes while also pushing up inflation and yields.

As for the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, there's no decision yet, but a domestic inflation shock has revived bets on tightening. BusinessWorld and ABS-CBN report the BSP is seen delivering a fourth straight rate hike at its next meeting, and BPI expects two more hikes before the end of 2026.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi rebounded 1.1 percent to close at about 5,737, back above the 5,700 level, driven by bargain hunting, lower unemployment figures and firmer Wall Street cues. The peso was effectively unchanged, weakening a touch to 62.68 to the dollar. The big corporate story is Mynt, the GCash parent, opening its 53-billion-peso initial public offering, the largest local listing in years and a direct test of domestic appetite for risk at these interest rates. On the data side, factory output growth accelerated in August, led by electronic products and transport equipment, a rare bright spot against elevated inflation. Politically, the National Security Council is looking into China-linked remittances to a Duterte family firm, while President Marcos heads to Singapore to court investors and deepen ties. Also notable: Robinsons Retail trading was halted as the Gokongwei group completed a share transfer for delisting, ACEN secured a 50-million-euro ING loan for renewables, and BPI Wealth's assets under management breached 2 trillion pesos. Why it matters: capital is rotating into fixed income as inflation bites.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed at about 22,776, up 1.0 percent, and the BSE Sensex at about 73,068, up 0.9 percent, recovering after Wednesday's selloff when the Sensex dropped 429 points. The trigger was the Reserve Bank of India raising the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50 percent and shifting its stance to calibrated tightening, while ruling out near-term cuts. The rupee weakened a further 0.4 percent to 96.76 to the dollar, back near record lows. RBI Governor's message: markets can be irrational, the rupee is undervalued, and the central bank will ensure it finds its correct level. An RBI survey showed household inflation expectations rising on higher food and fuel costs. Why it matters: India is tightening into a currency slide, not out of one. On the brighter side, the World Bank raised India's growth forecast to 7.1 percent. In corporates, Sun Pharma's board meets October 12 to approve up to 15,000 crore rupees of debentures to fund its Organon acquisition, and Adani Enterprises won its highest-ever credit rating at AA stable.

Now to the world. Israel marked three years since the October 7 Hamas-led attack, with memorial events and mounting public demands for accountability; Prime Minister Netanyahu has refused to take personal responsibility or order a state inquiry. Separately, Israel's deadline for Britain to close its consulate in East Jerusalem is approaching, with a former British consul calling the demand unlawful. Pro-Palestine student groups marched in London on the anniversary despite police calls to postpone.

In Ukraine, a Russian missile strike destroyed 30 flats in the north of the country, killing 19 people including five children as families slept. Separately, President Trump says he will speak to President Putin about the death of a Russian plague lab worker, amid fears of pneumonic plague and calls for transparency.

In France, the government has halted police use of stun grenades after a boy's hand was blown off during student protests. Student unrest paused on Wednesday, but bond market pressure on Paris intensified ahead of budget talks. Why it matters: France is now the euro zone's clearest sovereign-risk flashpoint.

In business, Paramount completed its mega-merger with Warner Bros to create Skydance, and Britain's Boots chain was sold for 7 billion pounds to a Canadian billionaire family. Royal Mail plans 2,500 job cuts. South Korea's President Lee said AI appears to have been used in recent bank hacks, a warning shot for financial cybersecurity everywhere. And Webull shares plunged 18 percent after a US House committee report flagged structural ties to China.

That's your briefing for Thursday, October 8. Trade carefully, and we'll see you tomorrow morning.
