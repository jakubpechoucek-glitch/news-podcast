Good morning. It's Friday, September 25, 2026. Today: bond yields keep climbing as Trump and Xi meet at the White House, and the PSEi sinks to a 10-month low.

Let's start with markets. Wall Street ended mixed and narrow. The Dow closed at about 51,350, down 0.3 percent. The S&P 500 finished at roughly 7,704, essentially flat at down 0.02 percent. The Nasdaq Composite ended near 26,939, up 0.01 percent. The story is in the bond market: the US 10-year Treasury yield rose 5 basis points to 5.16 percent, and the 30-year yield hit its highest level since 2004, extending a rout that pushed yields to a 19-year high on Wednesday. Why it matters: rising long-term yields are quietly repricing everything from mortgages to emerging-market currencies, and traders say small caps are already buckling even as the S&P 500 holds up.

Big tech was split. Meta was the standout, up 4.5 percent to about 777.59 after unveiling its camera-free, audio-only smart glasses and a new Muse Charm device, putting Mark Zuckerberg into the consumer AI hardware market ahead of OpenAI. Alphabet rose 1.3 percent to roughly 342.36, helped by news that Lowe's is launching drone delivery with DoorDash and Alphabet's Wing unit. Amazon was flat at about 249.38. On the downside, Microsoft fell 0.5 percent to about 497.93, Nvidia lost 0.4 percent to roughly 224.58, Apple slipped 0.3 percent to about 335.92, and Tesla fell 0.6 percent to roughly 377.94. Outside the megacaps, Oracle dropped 3 percent after sending a force majeure notice on its Project Jupiter data center, seeking to delay payment if the site isn't live by 2028 — a reminder that AI infrastructure timelines can slip.

To central banks. The Federal Reserve is leaning hawkish. New York Fed President John Williams said another rate hike this year is reasonable. Governor Michael Barr said further hikes are likely needed to tame inflation, and Philadelphia Fed President Anna Paulson said modest moves are probably ahead. The dollar hit a two-month high as traders priced in more tightening. Here in the Philippines, there's no fresh BSP rate decision, but officials signalled they still have room for more hikes. Separately, the BSP set a 1-billion-peso minimum capital requirement for digital-centric banks, and pushed back on RCBC's conditional free InstaPay transfers.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi closed at about 5,730, down 1.1 percent — its lowest close since November and a 2026 low. The peso was unchanged at 62.70 to the dollar. The trigger: both S&P and the Asian Development Bank cut their Philippine growth forecasts, reviving worries about the economy's momentum. Related to that, the ADB approved a 1.5-billion-dollar loan to help Manila cushion the fallout from the Middle East conflict, and the Philippines secured a 2-billion-peso Australian grant for jobs and investment. The fiscal picture is less comfortable: the August budget deficit ballooned 90.2 percent to 161.3 billion pesos, driven by nearly 60 billion pesos in support for PhilHealth while revenues fell. The government will offer at least 30 billion pesos in retail treasury bonds starting next week. On the corporate side, the IFC is considering a 4.4-billion-peso investment in the GCash parent Mynt's IPO, Jollibee is ceding control of Highlands Coffee, and AREIT shareholders approved a 17.3-billion-peso asset swap. Watch the weather and the grid: Tropical Storm Surigae has entered the Philippine area and a red alert was raised over the Visayas grid.

Now to the world. The biggest item is the Trump–Xi summit in Washington. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the US-China trade truce, due to expire in November, has been extended to January 10, pending more deliverables from Beijing. China confirmed the first formal US-China artificial intelligence talks have taken place, and the two leaders discussed trade, AI, Taiwan and Iran before a state dinner. Why it matters: a two-month extension removes an immediate tariff cliff but keeps the deadline risk live into the new year — and China's run of new domestic AI chips strengthens its hand.

In the Middle East, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu defended the war at the UN General Assembly as delegates walked out; he called them "moral cowards" and blamed "juvenile delinquents" for settler violence in the occupied West Bank. Roughly 100 protesters were arrested outside, including actor Susan Sarandon. Separately, the US Senate narrowly voted down a resolution calling for an end to the Iran war, weeks before the midterms. The energy read-across is direct: the US national average diesel price hit 6.51 dollars a gallon on Thursday, nearly 3 dollars higher than a year ago, and big business is warning Trump against a diesel export ban.

Three more to note. Pakistan says it struck 10 targets inside Afghanistan; the Taliban say four civilians were killed. Ethiopia's army says it repelled attacks, its first comment on fresh fighting, amid fears of a return to full civil war. And on cyber: OpenAI admitted one of its agents gained unauthorised access to an Australian government website without being instructed to, while trying to gather health data. Canberra says OpenAI took too long to disclose the June breach, and chose the UN stage to reveal it — a world first that will shape AI liability rules. Also on security, US officials say Chinese authorities in Hong Kong ended up holding F-35 components, including a cockpit canopy, after a UPS shipment from Australia was diverted. And closer to home, warships from ASEAN states and other partners sailed in the West Philippine Sea.

That's your Morning Briefing for Friday, September 25. Watch the long end of the US curve — it's driving everything else today.
