Good morning. It's Wednesday, November 11, 2026. Hot US inflation has flipped the rate debate from cuts to hikes, Nvidia is in the middle of a spy-linked scandal, and Anthropic has priced the biggest tech listing on the calendar.

To the markets. US stocks closed lower across the board. The Dow finished at about 52,232, down 0.3 percent. The S&P 500 ended at about 7,521, down 0.4 percent. The Nasdaq Composite closed at about 25,066, down 0.7 percent. The trigger was inflation: October consumer prices rose 0.4 percent on the month, hotter than expected, driven by shelter, food and energy. The US 10-year Treasury yield jumped 12 basis points to 4.98 percent, back at the doorstep of 5 percent. Why it matters: global borrowing costs, including Philippine ones, take their cue from that number.

Big tech was split. Nvidia was the worst performer, down 5.5 percent to about 168.89, after the company hired law firm Wachtell Lipton to investigate chief executive Jensen Huang's disclosed affair with a woman linked in press reports to Chinese intelligence. Attention has also turned to Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia chip buyer previously probed over allegedly supplying chips to China. Huang returned to the office projecting business as usual, and prediction markets put low odds on him leaving soon. Microsoft fell 2.2 percent to about 533, Apple slipped 0.6 percent to about 349, and Alphabet eased 0.3 percent to about 287. On the upside, Meta jumped 5.1 percent to about 641, Amazon rose 1.5 percent to about 271, and Tesla added 0.5 percent to about 380.

Two AI datapoints cushioned the mood. CoreWeave beat on revenue and lifted guidance, easing fears of an AI slowdown and lifting Asian shares and Nasdaq futures overnight. And Anthropic, the maker of the Claude chatbot, set an IPO price range of 70 to 78 dollars, below expectations, with trading expected to start on November 19. It is targeting a fully diluted valuation of about 1.4 trillion dollars and raising up to 90.4 billion dollars, on a revenue run rate of 81 billion. For contrast, OpenAI posted third-quarter revenue of 10 billion dollars with operating losses of 10.9 billion.

Central banks. The Federal Reserve has opened the door to faster tightening. Governor Christopher Waller said he is leaning toward a December increase and could support a half-point move depending on the data. Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan wants at least two more quarter-point hikes and says the pace could accelerate this winter. Governor Michelle Bowman warned of a sharp equity correction if AI investment returns disappoint. There is no fresh news from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas in the last 36 hours.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi closed essentially flat at about 5,714, down 0.04 percent, holding up better than Wall Street. The peso weakened 0.35 percent, finishing at 63.15 to the dollar, pressured by that jump in US yields. Why it matters: a softer peso raises imported fuel and food costs just as the Fed signals more hikes.

On the corporate side, Jollibee Foods delivered a strong third quarter, but nine-month profit still dipped to 8.13 billion pesos. Megaworld lifted nine-month earnings 6 percent to 19 billion pesos on recurring income and steady residential bookings. Manila Water's nine-month net income fell 5 percent as higher costs bit. OceanaGold Philippines is leaning on new technology to improve safety and efficiency at its Didipio gold and copper mine in Nueva Vizcaya.

In policy, the Board of Investments says the Philippines and the United States are unlikely to sign the proposed Pax Silica artificial intelligence industrial hub framework at the ASEAN Summit, but still expect a signing within this year. And state-run Maharlika Investment Corporation may delay up to 4 billion pesos of prospective investments to 2027, though it expects to announce two or three transactions before year-end.

Turning to India. Indian equities closed marginally higher and out of step with Wall Street. The Nifty 50 ended at about 25,045, up 0.1 percent. The Sensex finished at about 81,107, also up 0.1 percent. The rupee was steady at 93.92 to the dollar, firmer by 0.03 percent. GIFT Nifty was pointing to a strong open this morning, trading around 25,319, roughly 1.1 percent above the previous futures close. There are no fresh signals from the Reserve Bank of India.

On earnings, tyre maker MRF reported a 22.8 percent fall in second-quarter net profit even as revenue rose 17.4 percent to 8,660 crore rupees, squeezed by sharply higher raw material costs. On the macro side, official briefings highlight India's foreign exchange build-up and a 50 billion dollar balance of payments surplus, a cushion that helps explain the rupee's stability. Politically, the release of Sonia Gandhi's memoir has triggered a national debate, with the BJP accusing the book of courting the Babri vote bank and glossing over Bofors. And strategically, analysts flag China steadily eroding India's dominance of Bhutan's economy. Why it matters: both stories shape the backdrop for New Delhi's domestic and regional positioning.

Now to the world. The Iran conflict remains the dominant geopolitical story. The US House blocked an attempt to impeach Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth, with Democrats able to try again next year, and Iran denied holding talks with Washington. A fire on a platform in Iran's South Pars gas field has been contained, with officials weighing a gas leak or an operational mishap as the cause. Energy markets are so far calm: US gasoline prices have fallen below 4 dollars a gallon for the first time since July.

Security services are busy elsewhere. The US Justice Department says three Iran-backed suspects plotted a string of attacks, including a major incident near RAF Fairford, the UK airbase used by American forces. Separately, a 41-year-old senior US Army soldier has been arrested in London on suspicion of spying for Russia.

In Israel, the new parliament has been sworn in, with Benjamin Netanyahu's main rival Gadi Eisenkot getting the first chance to form a government. In the occupied West Bank, Israeli settlers burned down a Palestinian business near Nablus. Why it matters: a change of government in Israel would reset the region's diplomatic math.

On trade, the White House says it will press ahead with tariffs despite a midterm backlash over rising prices, with executives in Washington warning of higher costs and shifting supply chains. Chipmakers, meanwhile, warn that AI demand is diverting capacity away from mature chips, creating shortages for carmakers and industrial users. Elsewhere, Bangladesh saw violence as supporters of ousted prime minister Sheikh Hasina demanded her return from exile in India, defaults are rattling Brazil's shadow banking sector, and Dangote's refinery drew 4.1 billion dollars of orders in Africa's largest ever IPO.

That's your Morning Briefing for Wednesday, November 11. Trade carefully.
