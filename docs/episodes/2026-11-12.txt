Good morning. It's Thursday, November 12, 2026, and here's your Morning Briefing on finance and geopolitics. Today: Wall Street rebounds as CoreWeave steadies the AI trade, the Fed talks about a bigger hike, and the peso slides past 63.50.

To markets. US stocks closed broadly higher. The Dow Jones ended at about 52,486, up 0.5 percent. The S&P 500 finished at roughly 7,619, up 1.3 percent, and the Nasdaq Composite closed near 25,466, up 1.6 percent. The driver was CoreWeave, which jumped more than 24 percent for its best day since April 2025 after a strong third quarter and a raised forecast. Its chief executive, Mike Intrator, also cancelled his planned share sale program. Why it matters: the so-called neoclouds had been the market's proxy for whether AI spending is sustainable, and one good print was enough to calm nerves.

But bonds did not join the party. The US 10-year Treasury yield sits at 5.00 percent, up 2 basis points.

Among the big-tech names, Nvidia rebounded 2.9 percent to about 173.73 after plunging as much as 10 percent in late trading on a Wall Street Journal report that its board is preparing to oust co-founder Jensen Huang. That follows Huang's admission of an affair with a woman US intelligence believes is a Chinese spy. Nvidia says no secrets were compromised and has hired a major US law firm to investigate, but Washington is already moving: there are resignation calls and fresh pressure to tighten AI chip export controls to China, which Huang had opposed. Nvidia reports earnings shortly, so this is not background noise.

Elsewhere, Tesla rose 2.9 percent to about 390.84, Alphabet gained 2.1 percent to 293.45, and Apple added 2.0 percent to 355.63. On the downside, Microsoft fell 2.3 percent to 521.13, Meta dropped 2.0 percent to 628.77 after its head of infrastructure, Santosh Janardhan, announced he will leave in 2027 and be replaced by former AWS executive David Brown, and Amazon slipped 1.7 percent to 266.17.

Now central banks. The Federal Reserve is debating not whether to hike, but how fast. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee called October's hotter-than-expected consumer price index "borderline disturbing." Governor Christopher Waller said he is leaning toward a December increase and could support a half-point move depending on the data. Goldman Sachs has changed its Fed forecast. The result: the dollar index rose 0.63 percent to 102.85, a one-month high, the yen is drifting toward 160, and gold is wobbling around the 4,000-dollar line. For the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, no decision this week, but officials have publicly admitted they face a difficult combination of elevated inflation and slowing growth, and local analysts say a jumbo hike is still possible despite an anemic third quarter.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi fell 1.0 percent to close at about 5,636, erasing recent gains, and the peso weakened 0.94 percent to 63.54 to the dollar. Two things did the damage: US rate hike fears, and weak investment data. Bangko Sentral figures show foreign direct investment net inflows for the first eight months down 19.2 percent to 4.768 billion dollars, even though August alone rebounded nearly 25 percent. Why it matters: a softer FDI pipeline plus a hawkish Fed leaves the peso with little support. Second, El Niño farm losses have climbed to almost 10 billion pesos, and Luzon faces higher power rates this month, both of which feed straight back into the inflation problem the BSP is trying to contain. Corporate confirmation came from Globe Telecom, whose profit fell 7 percent on higher interest costs and peso depreciation losses. Politically, President Marcos's trust and satisfaction ratings hit record lows in the latest OCTA survey, just as the Philippines takes the ASEAN chairship for 2026.

Turning to India. Indian equities closed near nine-month highs. The Nifty 50 ended at about 25,255, up 0.8 percent, and the BSE Sensex at roughly 81,879, up 1.0 percent, led by large caps and driven by surging foreign institutional inflows. The rupee firmed 0.26 percent to 93.68 to the dollar, bucking the stronger dollar elsewhere. GIFT Nifty points to a muted open today. On policy, Moody's lifted its view of India, holding the FY27 growth forecast at 7 percent and saying the economy is well positioned to absorb global shocks. Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said the fundamentals remain strong. The Reserve Bank of India has consolidated its domestic money transfer rules, allowing walk-in cash NEFT transfers up to 50,000 rupees while capping cash transfers and cash delivery at 10,000 rupees per transaction. Why it matters: it tightens the cash-in, cash-out channel regulators see as a laundering risk. In corporates, Bajaj Finance launched a share sale to raise up to 1.25 billion dollars, about 117 billion rupees, to repay loans and debentures, and Yes Bank bondholders filed a contempt petition over the restoration of 84 billion rupees of AT-1 bonds.

Now world and geopolitics. Russia-Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi turned to post-war European security arrangements but ended without a breakthrough. US envoys are due to meet Vladimir Putin and Volodymyr Zelensky today. Combined with stalled but quiet US-Iran hostilities, that pushed oil down more than a dollar, with Brent softer on peace-deal hopes. The knock-on: US gasoline has fallen below 4 dollars a gallon for the first time since July, a rare disinflationary offset for the Fed.

In Gaza, an Israeli strike hit the intensive care unit at Nasser Hospital, killing at least six people according to Gaza's Civil Defence.

In Spain, tens of thousands joined a general strike called by the main unions over housing and the cost of living, with clashes erupting in Barcelona. Why it matters: cost-of-living politics is now producing street-level disruption in a core euro-zone economy.

Closer to home, Indonesia's vice president has resigned amid signs the ruling alliance is fraying, with more than 100 lawmakers signing an impeachment petition and a widening rift between President Prabowo and his predecessor Joko Widodo. That is political risk in ASEAN's largest economy, right as the Philippines chairs the bloc.

Three quick ones. Anthropic, the maker of the Claude chatbot, is set for a 1.3 trillion dollar market debut, pricing shares at 70 to 78 dollars, below expectations. Canada's ambassador warned that Trump's tariffs are raising costs and threatening US investment, while Washington watchers say a US-India trade deal could land by year end. And Alibaba announced plans to list T-Head, its Nvidia chip rival, in Hong Kong, having filed confidentially back in September.

That's your Morning Briefing. Watch the Nvidia board, the December Fed meeting, and the peso at 63.50.
