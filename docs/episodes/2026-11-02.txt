Good morning. It's Monday, November 02, 2026. Here's your Finance and Geopolitics briefing: a brutal week-ending selloff on Wall Street led by chips and AI, Philippine inflation heading toward a three-year high, and a US midterm vote that lands tomorrow.

To the markets. Wall Street closed the week in retreat. The Dow Jones Industrial Average finished at about 51,770, down 1.1 percent. The S&P 500 ended near 7,580, down 1.8 percent. The Nasdaq Composite took the worst of it, closing around 25,437, down 2.9 percent. The driver, per CNBC, was Big Tech earnings reigniting fears about the scale of AI capital spending, pushing investors into defensive stocks after a week that started with promise and ended in despair.

The damage in megacap tech was broad but uneven. Nvidia fell 4.2 percent to about 204.64, the single biggest drag. Microsoft lost 1.6 percent to roughly 521.91, Meta fell 1.5 percent to about 614.99, Alphabet dropped 1.4 percent to 279.84, Amazon also fell 1.4 percent to 240.35, Apple slipped 1.2 percent to 344.63, and Tesla was the most resilient, down 0.9 percent at 399.81. One structural point from the earnings season: the gap between Meta's advertising revenue growth and Google's search growth was the widest since 2021, as AI reshapes where ad dollars land. Separately, OpenAI has pared its fundraising goal and is now seeking up to 20 billion dollars in its current round, a deal still open and not yet closed.

Bonds told the other half of the story. The US 10-year Treasury yield fell 6 basis points to 4.93 percent, even as oil keeps climbing above 117 dollars a barrel. Why it matters: falling yields alongside rising crude suggests traders fear an AI-driven equity crash more than they fear inflation. Also watch Treasury issuance; Wall Street is bracing for the biggest overhaul of US debt sales guidance since early 2024 under Secretary Scott Bessent.

On central banks, the Fed picture in the weekend coverage is genuinely mixed: some outlets report rates held steady despite elevated inflation, while others report a unanimous 25 basis point hike to 3.75 to 4.00 percent, with Kevin Warsh resisting White House pressure. Economists remain openly divided on whether another increase lands this year. Here at home, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas has moved to raise its policy rate to blunt inflation, and its own forecast range puts October inflation as high as 8.1 percent.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi closed at about 5,680, down 0.4 percent, and the peso weakened again to 63.72 to the dollar, off 0.19 percent. Brokers expect sideways trading this week, with peso and oil risks keeping investors cautious.

The headline number lands shortly: economists polled by The STAR see October inflation accelerating to 7.6 percent, a three-year high, driven by transport fares, electricity, fuel and the weaker peso, with the BSP's own upper estimate at 8.1 percent. Why it matters: that locks in pressure for further BSP tightening even as growth sentiment cools.

Indeed, the BSP's business expectations survey shows optimism dipping for the quarter and the year ahead, blamed on prices, geopolitics and the peso. The Federation of Philippine Industries says squeezed manufacturers are putting expansion plans on hold. Airlines are asking airport operators to waive terminal fees as a weak peso doubles the effective cost of flying. And a Japanese business chamber has flagged the new Energy and Environment department rule requiring asset surrender in mining contracts, warning it damages investor confidence. One more: an audit found 69 percent of ODA-backed projects are behind schedule, and Manila is seeking an oil storage hub deal with the UAE.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed at about 23,053, up 0.5 percent, and the BSE Sensex at roughly 74,460, up 0.4 percent, both finishing the week up around half a percent on resilient large-cap IT and healthcare names. Midcaps and smallcaps fell as much as 2 percent on crude above 117 dollars and the global tech selloff. The rupee firmed slightly to 94.94 to the dollar, up 0.22 percent.

On the Reserve Bank of India: its net short dollar forward position hit a record 239.2 billion dollars in September, and it has proposed a blunt fix to steady the rupee. One report sees government bond yields easing to 6.75 to 7 percent by March 2027 even if the RBI hikes. Foreign portfolio investors pulled 34,843 crore rupees out of Indian equities in October, though they turned net buyers in the final week. Why it matters: flows, not earnings, are setting the market's direction.

Corporately, state fuel retailers Indian Oil, BPCL and HPCL posted a combined 30,106 crore rupee second-quarter profit, reversing a year-earlier loss, despite frozen pump prices. ITC reports this week with profit seen falling nearly 30 percent on cigarette tax hikes. Politically, the opposition held a mega rally in Delhi demanding the chief election commissioner resign over alleged voter-roll rigging.

Now to the world. US midterm elections are tomorrow. In Michigan, Democrat Haley Stevens endorsed Republican Mike Rogers for Senate after losing the nomination to Abdul El-Sayed, and was heckled as a traitor. Money is flooding in: 22 late-forming pop-up super PACs spent about 637 million dollars on congressional races, often before voters could see the donors, and crypto-linked groups have poured in more than 200 million dollars to pressure Congress on friendly rules. Why it matters: control of Congress sets the tax, crypto and trade agenda markets will trade on from Wednesday.

Russia struck Ukraine's grid, cutting power to thousands, as a rare humanitarian ceasefire around Kherson that allowed evacuations from Russian-occupied Oleshky expired. In the Middle East, Israeli strikes in Gaza killed eight, including a child and five Hamas-run police officers, and nearly 5,000 people have been displaced by Israel's military siege of Hebron in the West Bank. Why it matters: any widening escalation, especially involving Iran, is the clearest upside risk to crude already above 117 dollars, and to the peso and the rupee.

Europe is restive. Cost-of-living protests turned violent across Germany, with shops looted in Berlin, Hamburg and Frankfurt, on the same day hundreds of thousands marched in France. Police clashed with housing-crisis protesters in Portugal. Bulgaria's acting president Iliana Iotova leads run-off exit polls and would be the country's first elected female president.

In crypto, Binance has resumed phased withdrawals after a 6.3 billion dollar hack the FBI attributes to North Korea, saying it has covered 90 percent of affected user assets. And Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney says talks on a Canada-US trade deal are progressing, timed just ahead of the US vote. In corporate news, Virgin Media O2 is cutting thousands of jobs, and a judge dismissed with prejudice President Trump's defamation suit against Rupert Murdoch and the Wall Street Journal over the Epstein letter story.

That's your Morning Briefing. Markets reopen cautious; trade accordingly, and have a productive Monday.
