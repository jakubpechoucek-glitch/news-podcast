Good morning. It's Tuesday, November 10, 2026. Today: a powerful Wall Street rebound led by everything except Nvidia, a weak Philippine growth print that knocked the PSEi toward a one-year low, and oil sliding as Donald Trump declares the Iran war effectively over.

To markets. US stocks closed sharply higher. The Dow Jones Industrial Average finished at about 52,390, up 2.2 percent. The S&P 500 closed at about 7,550, up 2.4 percent. The Nasdaq Composite ended at about 25,253, up 2.7 percent. The US 10-year Treasury yield was unchanged at 4.86 percent. Why it matters: this was a risk-appetite move driven by cheaper oil and renewed chip buying, not a shift in rate expectations, because bonds didn't move at all.

Among the big names, Tesla gained 5.5 percent to about 378 dollars, Meta rose 5.1 percent to about 641, Amazon added 2.9 percent to about 267, Microsoft 2.8 percent to about 545, Apple 2.4 percent to about 351, and Alphabet 1.2 percent to about 288. The exception was Nvidia, down 4.3 percent to about 179, wiping hundreds of billions of dollars off its value after Trump said the FBI is investigating chief executive Jensen Huang's extramarital affair with a woman alleged to be a Chinese spy. Trump says Huang told him on Monday he did not know she was a spy. Nvidia's board is running its own probe, with lead independent director Stephen Neal expected to play the central role. Why it matters: Nvidia has no clear successor to Huang, so this is now a governance and succession risk sitting on top of the entire AI trade, though prediction markets still price low odds of a near-term exit.

Asia tracked the US rebound: South Korea's Kospi jumped more than 5 percent in early trade, Japan's Nikkei nearly 3 percent, and SoftBank surged 13 percent. Elsewhere in AI, Anthropic retracted IPO filing details it had sent to bankers — roughly 867 million shares at 70 to 80 dollars — calling the pricing inaccurate, and analysts are questioning the valuation. Goldman Sachs chief David Solomon says investor appetite for AI infrastructure financing remains strong despite the reset.

On central banks: no Federal Reserve decision, but San Francisco's Mary Daly signalled more rate hikes are likely and Richmond's Tom Barkin said the policy direction is clear while the pace must be careful. US consumer price data lands later today; gold slipped and the dollar eased ahead of it. No fresh move from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas either, though Manulife Investments says Philippine bond yields should decline over the next six to 12 months as inflation peaks and the BSP nears the end of its tightening cycle.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi closed at about 5,644, down 0.6 percent and near a one-year low. The peso weakened slightly to 63.06 to the dollar. The trigger: third-quarter GDP grew just 2.1 percent, the weakest since the pandemic. Why it matters: Citi now describes the Philippines as already in stagflation, and warns inflation has yet to peak — a bad combination for a central bank that markets had hoped was close to done hiking.

Relief at the pump, though: gasoline prices drop by 16.80 pesos a litre starting today, the biggest leg of a broad fuel rollback. The Department of Agriculture has halted pork imports for 60 days to shield local hog raisers, who also want higher tariffs, and banned poultry from Minnesota over bird flu while lifting its ban on German poultry. SM Prime reported lower nine-month profit as real estate sales slumped on higher borrowing costs and cautious consumers, and says it will stay deliberate on capital spending next year. Also watch Metro Manila traffic and flight disruptions around the ASEAN Summit this week, and the Bureau of Internal Revenue's investigation into an alleged data breach.

Turning to India. Indian markets are closed today for Diwali-Balipratipada, so there is no trading on the NSE or BSE. At the last close, the Nifty 50 stood at about 25,045, up 0.1 percent, and the Sensex at about 81,107, up 0.1 percent — both at their highest levels in more than eight months. The rupee was at 93.92 to the dollar, having broken a six-day winning streak on state banks' dollar bids.

From the Reserve Bank of India: supervisors have told some lenders to change how they value bond-forward derivative trades, pushing them toward Level 3 inputs. Why it matters: that could affect billions of dollars of assets across the banking sector and dent reported valuations. The RBI also extended the supersession of Abhyudaya Co-operative Bank's board by another year, and the governor said global growth should be marginally higher in 2026, supported by tech-related investment.

On the macro side, Citi and HSBC are both turning more positive on India, citing the slide in oil prices and a rotation out of crowded AI trades. India and the European Union are stepping up technology and investment ties ahead of a landmark trade pact, and after a call with Prime Minister Modi, Ukraine's Volodymyr Zelenskyy said India is ready to help set up a food corridor through the Black Sea. And a note for Philippine listeners: Qantas is outsourcing 850 jobs, most of them moving to India.

Now to the world. Ukraine peace talks resume in the United Arab Emirates, the first trilateral meeting of Kyiv, Washington and Moscow delegations since February, with US officials reporting "some flexibility" on both sides. Zelenskyy says Russia is suffering record losses; Trump says he sees a strong inclination to end the war. Why it matters: a credible ceasefire path would reprice European energy and defence assets quickly.

On Iran, Trump says the war is "effectively over" militarily and further strikes are highly unlikely, though the US stays "locked and loaded" and keeps a naval presence at the Strait of Hormuz for now. Oil fell on that: Brent dropped to about 92.33 dollars a barrel and WTI to about 86.50. Why it matters: that oil slide is the single biggest reason global equities rallied — and why Philippine pump prices are falling.

But the region isn't calm. Saudi forces intercepted two missiles fired toward Riyadh hours after a Houthi attack damaged the key East-West pipeline. And France's foreign minister has formally blamed Iran's Revolutionary Guard for an October cyberattack that heated up fridges and freezers in hundreds of French supermarkets, spoiling tons of food. Why it matters: critical-infrastructure hacking is now an explicit part of the Iran confrontation, even with the shooting phase paused.

In Washington, Republican Representative Thomas Massie has introduced articles of impeachment against Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth, with a House vote likely this week. It is expected to fail, but incoming Democrats say accountability for Hegseth is a priority next Congress. Elizabeth Warren is set to chair the Senate banking committee. And PepsiCo is cutting about 1,000 North American jobs as its home market struggles.

That's your Morning Briefing for Tuesday, November 10. Trade carefully.
