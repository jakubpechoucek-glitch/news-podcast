Good morning. It's Monday, November 16, 2026. Here's your Morning Briefing: Nvidia's leadership crisis is still dragging on tech, the Fed is being priced for a December hike, and Manila is hosting ASEAN as the peso slides past 63.

To the markets. US stocks closed split. The Dow Jones Industrial Average ended at about 52,348, up 0.1 percent. The S&P 500 closed at about 7,562, down 0.4 percent. The Nasdaq Composite finished at about 25,136, down 0.9 percent. Even so, it was a good week overall: the S&P 500 secured a weekly gain of 2.5 percent and the Nasdaq added 2.3 percent. The US 10-year Treasury yield rose 5 basis points to 5.15 percent. Why it matters: a 10-year above 5 percent keeps pressure on equity valuations and on emerging-market currencies, the peso included.

Big tech was mixed, and the split was all about Nvidia. Nvidia fell 2.0 percent to about 162.85, still bleeding after Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive, president and board member on November 12, amid a widening scandal over whether advanced AI chips were diverted to China. Two investors holding roughly 2.13 billion dollars of stock have urged the board to consider a potential return of Huang. Treasury Secretary Bessent went the other way, calling for a dramatic shakeup and an outside chief executive. Meta bucked the trend, up 2.9 percent to about 642, as ETF strategists argued AI demand could rotate to other big tech names. Tesla fell 1.8 percent to about 390, Alphabet 1.0 percent to about 296, Amazon 0.3 percent to about 267. Apple rose 0.4 percent to about 356 and Microsoft 0.2 percent to about 524.

Central banks. No Fed meeting, but the tone is hawkish. The Kansas City Fed president says the Fed has some work to do on inflation, and Boston's Collins says steady, gradual rate hikes are appropriate. Markets are now pricing a December rate rise, and Asian bond yields have climbed with them. Gold edged higher as investors wait on remarks from Warsh. At the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas there is no rate move yet, but it has temporarily suspended the 1-million-peso threshold that triggers enhanced due diligence on large cash withdrawals until January, to ease holiday cash demand, and kept the 3 percent cap on credit card finance charges. It is also moving to link QR payment systems with Cambodia's central bank.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi last closed at about 5,593; the daily change isn't available. The peso weakened sharply, with the dollar up 1.25 percent to 63.76 pesos. Analysts are flagging 5,500 as the next downside risk ahead of the BSP's rate decision, with stagflation fears in the frame and one strategist warning the index hasn't hit bottom. Why it matters: a weak peso plus a hawkish Fed narrows the BSP's room to cut.

Manila is hosting the 49th ASEAN Summit and related summits this week, with dialogue partners in town and Donald Trump expected to get a warm welcome. The Philippines and Australia have signed an updated defence cooperation deal. On the economy, the Asian Development Bank is reviewing its Philippine growth forecast after a slower third quarter, and the Development Budget Coordination Committee will revisit its assumptions in the third week of December, factoring in El Niño. Energy is the soft spot: the country's main coal mine is in limbo, the Visayas grid went on yellow alert, and a 700-megawatt coal plant is being eyed in Camarines Norte. Government subsidies to state firms fell 33.4 percent in September to 6.13 billion pesos.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed at about 25,144, down 0.6 percent, and the BSE Sensex at about 81,592, down 0.4 percent. The rupee slipped, with the dollar up 0.2 percent to 94.23 rupees. The driver is inflation: retail inflation came in above 6 percent, higher than expected, which has put the Reserve Bank of India's easing cycle under strain and raised talk of a rate hike rather than a cut. The RBI also drained liquidity, selling 25,000 crore rupees of government securities through open market operations. Separately, it says the cap on microfinance loan repayments stays, it may review the 3 lakh rupee income threshold for microfinance borrowers, and it has flagged rising microloan costs, calling for fair and competitive lending rates.

On growth, an Economic Times poll sees second-quarter growth at 7.7 percent and 7.3 percent for the full fiscal year, and Goldman Sachs expects India to remain the fastest-growing major economy in 2027. The offset: India's trade deficit with China is projected to hit 150 billion dollars this fiscal year. Fund managers caution it's too early to call foreign institutional buying a durable trend, though lower crude and a steadier currency help.

Now the world. The Iran war remains the biggest macro risk. Tehran has warned it may strike first ahead of another US attack, while the Yemeni army says it is repositioning troops away from the Bab al-Mandeb strait and other coastal areas. Yet energy markets are reading de-escalation: US diesel has fallen below 6 dollars a gallon, a two-month low, on easing tensions and weaker demand. Why it matters: cheaper fuel is the single biggest relief valve for Philippine and Indian inflation right now.

Japan's economy unexpectedly contracted in the third quarter as elevated energy costs bit, after household energy subsidies expired in September. The yen held steady against the dollar and euro after the data. Why it matters: it complicates any Bank of Japan tightening and keeps Asian currencies under pressure.

In the United States, the fallout from the nationwide FAA ground stop continues. Transport Secretary Sean Duffy has tied the outage to a router in southern Arkansas linked to updates on an older communication system managed by L3Harris, though the FAA says the root cause is still unknown. Trump has blamed the Biden administration for aging systems and requested 50 billion dollars for upgrades; Democratic lawmakers want transparency before more spending.

Three quick ones. Volodymyr Zelensky has urged the United States and Elon Musk to lift Starlink restrictions, saying slowed internet is making it harder to stop Russian attacks. In Israel, Gadi Eisenkot says Benjamin Netanyahu cannot remain prime minister amid allegations that Arab party members were arrested before elections on unfounded evidence from his office. And on the corporate side, Anthropic's initial public offering lands with an embattled AI trade looking for a bright spot, while SpaceX caught both the booster and the upper stage of Starship for the first time on Test Flight 15.

That's your briefing for Monday, November 16. Trade carefully, and we'll see you tomorrow morning.
