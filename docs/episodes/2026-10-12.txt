Good morning. It's Monday, October 12, 2026. Today: a firm close on Wall Street against a rising rate backdrop, a strong session for the PSEi, India snapping an eight-week losing streak, and a Trump-announced energy ceasefire that Moscow has not confirmed.

We start with markets. US stocks closed the week higher across the board. The Dow Jones Industrial Average finished at about 51,655, up 0.8 percent. The S&P 500 closed at roughly 7,812, up 0.6 percent. The Nasdaq Composite ended near 27,366, up 0.6 percent. But the bond market is the story underneath: the US 10-year Treasury yield rose another basis point to 5.24 percent. Why it matters: equities are grinding higher even as the cost of money keeps climbing, and that leaves little room for disappointment on earnings.

Big tech was split, which fits the pattern analysts describe as a volatile AI trade that produced both new highs and sharp declines last week. Amazon was the standout, up 3.3 percent to about 262 dollars. Microsoft rose 2.4 percent to roughly 535 dollars, with chief executive Satya Nadella making headlines by calling for an AI "emergency brake" that humans control. Tesla gained 2.1 percent to about 383 dollars, and Alphabet added 1.0 percent to about 352 dollars. On the downside, Apple fell 1.1 percent to about 337 dollars, Nvidia slipped 0.5 percent to about 229 dollars, and Meta eased 0.3 percent to about 719 dollars. Why it matters: money is rotating within the AI complex rather than out of it. One data point on that: an industry researcher now counts more than 300 so-called neoclouds offering Nvidia GPU capacity, up about 55 percent in under a year.

To central banks. The latest Federal Reserve minutes point to another rate hike likely coming this year to combat persistent inflation, and economists remain openly divided on whether it lands. Citadel Securities has flagged that Kevin Warsh could surprise with a hike this week. Separately, Jerome Powell says he will stay on the central bank's board after his term as chair expires next month. The Wall Street Journal's read on all this: economists increasingly think higher bond yields are here to stay.

On the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, there is no fresh policy decision, but the signals point one way. Nomura says the Philippines has the most widespread inflation pressures of any Asian economy it tracks, strengthening the case for further BSP hikes, and local reports say red-hot inflation is seen extending the tightening cycle. BSP bill yields climbed last week despite an oversubscribed auction.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi closed at about 5,701, up a strong 1.6 percent, and the peso firmed 0.2 percent to 62.72 to the dollar. That rally sits awkwardly with the flow data: foreign investors turned net sellers of Philippine stocks and bonds in August, with hot money swinging to a 315 million dollar outflow. Why it matters: the local bid is domestic, and it is fragile if rate expectations keep rising.

Three stories to watch. First, the Energy Regulatory Commission approved Meralco's first distribution rate reset in over a decade, but set it roughly 37 percent below what the utility proposed. Why it matters: a smaller pass-through to household and business power bills than feared, at a time when the government has declared a national energy emergency as Asia faces supply risk from the Iran war. Second, capital markets are busy: GCash's IPO takes the exchange into new territory and PLDT is pushing a 24.2 billion peso data centre REIT, the first of its kind here. Third, President Marcos met Mitsubishi Corporation and Ayala leadership on a 700 million dollar investment, while Cebu Pacific took delivery of its 15th long-haul aircraft ahead of holiday capacity.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed at about 22,520, up 1.3 percent, and the BSE Sensex at roughly 72,472, up 1.2 percent, with the Sensex rallying 879 points. That snapped an eight-week losing streak, the longest in 25 years, led by IT stocks and helped by easing crude. More than 4 lakh crore rupees was added to BSE-listed market value. The rupee was close to flat at 96.78 to the dollar.

The Reserve Bank is actively defending it. The RBI has tightened rupee derivatives rules and cut the dollar transaction limit to 5 million dollars to curb speculation. Economists warn the measures may drain banking liquidity and that pressure on the rupee can resume after temporary gains. Why it matters: a currency defence that tightens domestic liquidity is a hidden rate hike for Indian borrowers.

The flow picture is the key risk. Foreign investors sold 45,125 crore rupees of Indian equities in just the first six sessions of October, nearly matching all of September, pressured by elevated US yields and crude. Elsewhere, the IPO pipeline is alive: investors including Ashish Kacholia and Mukul Agrawal put 120 crore rupees into Fusion CX ahead of its 702 crore rupee offering opening October 14. And TCS, Vedanta and Anand Rathi Wealth all have dividend record dates this week.

Now to the world. The biggest headline: President Trump has declared an energy ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine. Volodymyr Zelensky says Kyiv would support such a truce but is waiting on details from Washington. Moscow has said nothing. In the same breath, Trump announced a deal for the United States to buy Russian diesel amid surging fuel prices, which Zelensky sharply criticised as an investment in war that must be ended, not prolonged. Why it matters: cheaper diesel for American consumers, but a direct crack in the sanctions architecture. The Kremlin also says Putin relayed an Iran war proposal to Trump on the same call.

Ukraine, meanwhile, struck a Yandex data centre, the third attack on the Russian tech giant this week, forcing it to suspend operations.

In the Gulf, Houthi forces hit Riyadh's airport for a second time in two days, part of weekend attacks the group says killed 13 people and targeted two airports and oil facilities. Trump says he is going to look at joining Saudi Arabia against the Iran-backed group. Why it matters: that is a direct threat to Saudi export infrastructure and the single clearest upside risk to oil and to Philippine fuel and electricity costs.

In Sudan, an air strike on an artisanal gold mine near the Egyptian border killed at least 72 people and injured more than 50, with neither Cairo nor Khartoum commenting. In Europe, Poland has detained a man suspected of planning to assassinate Prime Minister Donald Tusk and his defence minister. And on sovereign risk, Goldman Sachs is flagging Italy as the next bond market worry after France, citing wider 2027 and 2028 deficit targets, rising yields and election uncertainty.

That's your Morning Briefing for Monday, October 12. Trade carefully.
