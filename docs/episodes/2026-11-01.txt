Good morning. It's Sunday, November 1, 2026. Here's your Morning Briefing on finance and geopolitics. Today: a brutal end to the week for US tech, the peso slips past 63.70, and Binance restores most of a record $6.3 billion hack.

Start with markets. Wall Street closed the week in retreat. The Dow Jones Industrial Average finished at about 51,770, down 1.1 percent. The S&P 500 ended at roughly 7,580, down 1.8 percent. The Nasdaq Composite took the worst of it, closing near 25,437, down 2.9 percent, and opening at its lowest level in more than two months. The trigger was Big Tech earnings reigniting fears about artificial intelligence spending, which is now approaching 1 trillion dollars industry-wide, with investors questioning when, or whether, that capital expenditure pays off. Money rotated into defensive stocks.

The damage among the megacaps was broad. Nvidia was the clear laggard, down 4.2 percent to about 204.64. Microsoft fell 1.6 percent to roughly 521.91. Meta dropped 1.5 percent to about 614.99. Amazon lost 1.4 percent to 240.35, and Alphabet fell 1.4 percent to 279.84. Apple slipped 1.2 percent to about 344.63, and Tesla was the mildest decliner, off 0.9 percent at 399.81. Outside the top names, Intel suffered its worst single day since the 1970s, wiping roughly 12 billion dollars off the US government's equity stake. And OpenAI has pared its fundraising goal, now seeking up to 20 billion dollars in its latest round, a round that has not yet closed.

Bonds told the other half of the story. The US 10-year Treasury yield fell 6 basis points to 4.93 percent, as the equity selloff pushed money into safety. That capped the largest monthly drop in the 10-year yield in nearly three years. In commodities, silver is now down more than 58 percent from its February record of nearly 120 dollars an ounce, trading around 50 dollars, on profit-taking and weaker industrial demand.

Why it matters: lower yields usually support stocks, but this time they are a flight-to-safety signal, not a green light.

On central banks, there is no fresh Federal Reserve decision or new guidance from officials in the last 36 hours, and nothing new from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas either. The week ahead is the catalyst: the October US jobs report, the US midterm elections, and earnings from Apple, AMD and Palantir, all of which feed directly into the Fed rate outlook. Expect more volatility.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi closed at about 5,680, down 0.4 percent, tracking the softer global tone but holding up better than Wall Street. The peso weakened 0.2 percent to 63.72 to the dollar, a fresh test of the central bank's tolerance for currency slippage ahead of the next policy meeting.

On the corporate side, BDO Unibank reported net profit of 63.6 billion pesos for January to September, a marginal increase, supported by strong lending growth and higher interest income despite what the bank called a challenging economy. Why it matters: the country's largest lender is still growing volumes, but margin pressure is now visible at the top of the banking sector.

In telecoms, the National Telecommunications Commission commended DITO, Globe and Smart for network performance at key Metro Manila bus terminals during the Undas travel rush. Separately, Globe is pressing the government to sustain faster issuance of infrastructure permits to improve coverage and quality. Why it matters: permitting speed, not capital, is now the binding constraint on Philippine network buildout.

Also watch energy. Semirara Mining and Power's 50-year coal operating contract for Semirara Island in Antique expires next year, and the company is questioning the propriety of auctioning the coal blocks. And the Philippine Disaster Resilience Foundation is seeking greater private sector participation on water security, warning that climate change, rising demand and infrastructure gaps threaten supply and business operations.

Turning to India. Indian equities bucked the global selloff. The Nifty 50 closed at about 23,053, up 0.5 percent, and the BSE Sensex at roughly 74,460, up 0.4 percent. Over the full week the Nifty gained around 2.6 percent and the Sensex 1.6 percent, led by pharmaceuticals and IT, with the Nifty recovering above its 200-week moving average at 22,700. Analysts flag immediate resistance at 23,300 to 23,450 and support at 22,750 to 22,525. The rupee firmed 0.2 percent to 94.95 to the dollar. There are no fresh Reserve Bank of India signals in this cycle.

On regulation, SEBI chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said a consultation paper on foreign investor compliance requirements will be released within weeks, aimed at aligning Indian norms with global standards. Why it matters: it is a direct response to heavy foreign institutional outflows, and easier compliance is the lever SEBI can pull without touching rates.

Earnings were solid. Muthoot Finance posted second-quarter consolidated net profit up 36 percent year-on-year to 3,278 crore rupees, with assets under management up 42 percent, though standalone gross non-performing assets rose to 3.87 percent from 2.28 percent, a clear asset-quality warning. NTPC's standalone profit rose 14 percent to 5,320 crore rupees with revenue up about 17 percent, and an interim dividend of 3 rupees a share. CDSL's net profit jumped 24 percent to 173 crore rupees, having opened roughly 75 lakh new demat accounts in the quarter.

Now to the world. The biggest market-adjacent story is the Binance hack. The exchange says it has now restored backing for 90 percent of the 6.3 billion dollars in assets stolen, pledges to cover the rest, and plans to resume withdrawals today, subject to security checks. Two analytics firms point to a North Korean state-backed group, and investigators describe a cat-and-mouse race to recover funds before they are laundered. Crypto prices fell on Friday. Why it matters: it is believed to be the largest hack of its kind ever, and it lands as crypto interests pour more than 200 million dollars into the 2026 US midterms to push for favourable rules.

On Iran, a source says Qatari mediators held more than 12 hours of talks in Tehran on Friday aimed at restarting direct US-Iran negotiations and avoiding a resumption of American-led strikes. Why it matters: that is the single biggest swing factor for oil right now, with gasoline, diesel and jet fuel all having surged and refining earnings at the oil majors climbing sharply.

On Ukraine, Turkish President Erdogan hosted Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Istanbul, urging Russia and Ukraine to halt Black Sea attacks, with the two agreeing to work on a new Black Sea grain corridor. That matters for global grain pricing.

In Europe, more than 320,000 people demonstrated across France against the cost of living and poor public services, with clashes between protesters and police, and similar confrontations in Albania. In Spain, a train disaster is clouding Prime Minister Sánchez's re-election chances weeks before a snap general election.

Finally, health: more than 5,000 people have died in the Democratic Republic of Congo's deadliest Ebola outbreak, with over 10,000 infections, and Nigeria is investigating two suspected cases in its northeast.

That's your Morning Briefing for Sunday, November 1. Have a good day.
