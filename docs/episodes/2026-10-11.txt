Good morning. It's Sunday, October 11, 2026. Here's your Morning Briefing on finance and geopolitics: Wall Street closed the week higher, the Philippine index reclaimed 5,700, and Washington's surprise diesel deal with Moscow is reshaping both oil prices and the war in Ukraine.

Start with markets. US stocks finished the week firmly in the green. The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at about 51,655, up 0.8 percent. The S&P 500 ended near 7,812, up 0.6 percent. The Nasdaq Composite closed around 27,366, up 0.6 percent. But the bond market is the story investors can't ignore: the US 10-year Treasury yield rose another basis point to 5.24 percent. Reuters this weekend listed four signs conditions in the bond market could get uglier as yields climb. Why it matters: equities are grinding higher against the most expensive cost of money in years, and that is a fragile combination.

Among the big technology names, Amazon led with a gain of 3.3 percent, closing at about 262 dollars. Microsoft rose 2.4 percent to roughly 535 dollars, as chief executive Satya Nadella publicly called for an "emergency brake" on artificial intelligence that humans control, joining other executives urging stronger safeguards. Tesla added 2.1 percent to about 383 dollars, even as it dropped the "Full Self-Driving" brand name in Europe after German regulators called the term somewhat misleading. Alphabet rose 1 percent. On the downside, Apple fell 1.1 percent to about 337 dollars, Nvidia slipped 0.5 percent to 229 dollars, and Meta eased 0.3 percent. That split captures a volatile week in the AI trade, with new highs and sharp declines inside the same five sessions.

On central banks, there was no Federal Reserve policy decision this weekend. The Fed news is political: President Trump has established a committee to investigate Fed Governor Lisa Cook, the latest step in his effort to remove her. Why it matters: perceived threats to Fed independence show up as a risk premium in long-dated yields, exactly where stress is building. Markets are now debating a possible December rate hike, not a cut, and Tuesday's September US consumer price index plus the start of bank earnings will set the tone.

For the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, there was no rate decision either. Officials said confidence in the banking system remains intact, signalled they will "do the right thing" as El Niño pressures food prices, and stressed the economy comes before public sentiment. The BSP also pushed back on RCBC's conditional free InstaPay transfers, and the Philippines confirmed it will align local bond pricing with global convention from 2027.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi jumped 1.6 percent to close at 5,701, reclaiming the 5,700 line after touching an 11-month low earlier in the week. Bargain hunting and easing global oil prices drove the rebound. The peso firmed 0.1 percent to 62.80 to the dollar, recovering from a record low. Why it matters: cheaper crude is the single biggest swing factor for both Philippine inflation and the currency right now. Three domestic stories stand out. First, the Energy Regulatory Commission approved a Meralco rate increase, the first in about a decade, which feeds directly into household and business costs. Second, Mount Kanlaon in the central Philippines erupted on Saturday, sending an ash plume 6 kilometres high, with aviation and agriculture in Negros exposed. Third, UK fintech Revolut is seeking a wholly owned Philippine entity and licences for digital banking and payments, a vote of confidence in the local digital finance market. Also watch: the Senate approved a ban on social media for minors, the Department of Energy hit Solar Philippines with 24 billion pesos in penalties for undelivered power, and a submarine cable disruption is slowing internet speeds nationwide.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed at about 22,520, up 1.3 percent, and the BSE Sensex at roughly 72,472, up 1.2 percent, snapping an eight-week losing streak as crude prices subsided and IT shares led the rally. The rupee firmed slightly to 96.73 to the dollar. The Reserve Bank of India raised rates midweek and has now unveiled tough curbs on dollar demand, plus a special oil window, to defend the currency. SBI Capital warns the full cycle could total 75 to 100 basis points of hikes, with another move possible in December. Why it matters: India is tightening into a currency and oil squeeze, the opposite of the easing story investors priced in a year ago. Foreign institutional investors sold a net 30,294 crore rupees of Indian equities last week, an eighth straight week of outflows, almost exactly offset by domestic institutions buying 30,313 crore. In corporate news, DMart operator Avenue Supermarts reported second-quarter profit up 8.5 percent to 743 crore rupees on revenue up 17.8 percent, though margins narrowed on wage inflation. Adani Green lifted operational capacity 24 percent to 20,763 megawatts. Politically, the opposition INDIA bloc marches in Delhi on Monday.

Now the world. The dominant story is President Trump's deal for the United States to buy Russian diesel, aimed at pulling down American fuel prices before next month's midterms. President Zelensky called it an investment in war that must be ended, not prolonged, and legal analysts say it contradicts US sanctions law. Trump responded that it is "time for Ukraine to get a new president." Why it matters: this is the main reason crude and fuel prices eased last week, and it is the reason Asian equity markets, including Manila and Mumbai, rallied. The human cost was immediate: a Russian glide bomb attack on Zaporizhzhia killed at least 20 people, including children, which Zelensky called Moscow's thanks for the deal. Separately, the Kremlin says Putin relayed an Iran war proposal to Trump in a call, a second channel that could move oil sharply in either direction.

In the Gulf, King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh was attacked for the third time in a week, with several people injured and visible damage inside the terminal, days after Yemen's Houthis claimed a strike. Why it matters: a sustained threat to Saudi aviation and energy infrastructure is the clearest upside risk to oil from here.

Two weather events: Hurricane Isaias killed four people and cut power to thousands in the United States before weakening, while Hurricane Simon strengthened to Category 4 off Mexico's Pacific coast, with Puerto Vallarta boarding up.

In crypto, bitcoin reclaimed 82,500 dollars on Saturday as exchange-traded fund flows improved, though it ended the week lower. And in Washington, Nobel peace laureate Navi Pillay called sweeping US sanctions on the International Criminal Court unacceptable and unheard of.

That's your briefing for Sunday, October 11. Trade carefully, and have a good week ahead.
