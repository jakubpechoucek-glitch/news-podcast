Good morning. It's Saturday, November 14, 2026. Today: Wall Street splits as the Nvidia scandal deepens, rate-hike talk hardens at the Fed, the PSEi sinks to a 2026 low just as Manila hosts the ASEAN summit, and a Microsoft outage ripples from Teams to the Philippine Supreme Court.

To the markets. US stocks closed in two directions. The Dow edged up 0.1 percent to about 52,348. The S&P 500 fell 0.4 percent to 7,562. The Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.9 percent to 25,136, dragged down by technology. The US 10-year Treasury yield rose 5 basis points to 5.15 percent, a clear signal that bond investors are pricing in tighter policy, not easier.

Among big tech, Nvidia was the story. It fell 2.0 percent to 162.85 and closed below a 4 trillion dollar market capitalisation for the first time since July 2025, after Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive on Thursday amid reports of two extramarital affairs, one with a woman described as a suspected Chinese spy. Nvidia's board tells CNBC it has not even selected a search firm for a permanent successor. Tesla fell 1.8 percent to 390.22, Alphabet lost 1.0 percent to 295.77, Meta slipped 0.8 percent to 623.61, and Amazon eased 0.3 percent to 266.66. Apple rose 0.4 percent to 356.27 and Microsoft added 0.2 percent to 523.88, even as it apologised for a near 24-hour global outage. Why it matters: the AI trade now has a governance risk attached to it, not just a valuation one.

On central banks, the direction of travel has flipped. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee says the central bank "has definitely got work to do" on inflation and warns that AI-driven demand is lifting prices across services and health care. San Francisco's Mary Daly says the Fed will likely need to raise rates. Bank of America has added a January 2027 hike to its forecast after a hot October CPI, and Bloomberg reports the Fed is on track to raise next month. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is pushing back, arguing the Fed should not hike over an energy shock tied to the Iran war. Separately, the Fed confirmed its revised 2027 calendar: seven voting meetings, with October 2027 set aside for deliberation without a rate vote. And a Fed survey named AI risks the biggest threat to financial stability.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi fell 1.18 percent to 5,593, its lowest close of 2026 and near a one-year low, with local desks blaming disappointing third-quarter GDP and corporate earnings, soft oil-linked names, a strong dollar and Fed rate-hike worries. The dollar eased 0.46 percent to 62.97 pesos, with the currency described locally as closing nearly flat after a run of weakness. No rate move from the Bangko Sentral: the BSP reaffirmed its price stability mandate amid rising inflation, even as slow GDP growth tests that resolve. It also signed a cooperation agreement with Bank Indonesia, moved to ease branch licensing for well-rated banks while tightening oversight of riskier lenders, and assured the public that banks are sound and cyber-secure through the ASEAN holiday break. Elsewhere, Microsoft's outage knocked out email at the Supreme Court and at businesses. Earnings were mixed: GMA Network's profit plunged 99 percent and ABS-CBN's loss widened 32 percent, while LT Group posted record nine-month earnings and Cosco Capital earned 11.96 billion pesos. The Treasury seeks at least 200 million dollars from a retail dollar bond sale next week. Why it matters: weak growth plus imported inflation leaves the BSP boxed in.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 fell 0.61 percent to 25,144 and the Sensex lost 0.39 percent to 81,592, snapping a three-day rally on rate-hike fears, rising CPI and crude pressure. The rupee weakened, with the dollar up 0.36 percent at 94.03 rupees. From the Reserve Bank of India, the tone is constructive on growth: the RBI raised its FY27 GDP growth projection by 40 basis points to 7.1 percent and revised bank capital adequacy norms. It also gave Paytm preliminary approval for a wallet licence, sending the stock to a record high, and fined MobiKwik 900,000 rupees over prepaid instrument and fraud reporting lapses. The RBI is expected to issue an FAQ clarifying expected credit loss implementation. In corporate news, SEBI cleared Razorpay's IPO, with the fintech reportedly targeting 600 million dollars, and Bharti Airtel signalled a possible Airtel Payments Bank listing in three to four years. YES Bank has filed a Supreme Court review plea over the 8,415 crore rupee AT-1 bond write-down. Why it matters: strong growth forecasts are colliding with an inflation problem that is global, not local.

Now the world. The Nvidia fallout has moved to Washington. Huang has been summoned to testify before Congress, with the Republican-led House wanting him in before Democrats take control in January. Treasury Secretary Bessent publicly blamed Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, saying several agencies favoured putting the firm Megaspeed on the entity list for months; Lutnick also faces questions over his sons' firm's ties to the chipmaker. US officials say President Trump does not plan to raise the Nvidia spy issue at next week's summit with President Xi. Why it matters: export-control credibility is now a live variable in US-China trade talks.

Second, a bad day for infrastructure. The FAA issued a nationwide ground stop on Friday afternoon over a telecommunications outage, lifting it after four hours, but cancellations will stretch through the weekend. That came alongside Microsoft's roughly 24-hour global outage of Outlook, Teams and Copilot, which experts call a stark reminder of concentration risk in cloud providers.

Third, energy and conflict. Oil rose after Trump said no Iran deal is needed, saying "we've got their Strait," though crude is still set for a second straight weekly decline on signs of a supply surplus. Fighting grips Yemen, while Qatar and Turkey signed a defence pact pledging joint action against external attacks. On Ukraine, the BBC's Steve Rosenberg reports Vladimir Putin is raising his demands, with European security now on the table, which Moscow treats as a win.

Finally, three quick ones. Gold is heading for its worst week since June as rate-hike bets build. The US Justice Department seized 1.2 billion dollars in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, tied to North Korean state theft. And SpaceX aborted the booster catch on Starship's double-catch test, bringing it down in the ocean instead.

That's your Morning Briefing for Saturday, November 14. Trade carefully, and enjoy your weekend.
