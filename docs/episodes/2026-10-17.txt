Good morning. It's Saturday, October 17, 2026. Today: a hard tech selloff on Wall Street, oil near $110 as the Strait of Hormuz turns dangerous, and a strong rebound on the Philippine Stock Exchange even as the peso sits near record lows.

Start with markets. US stocks closed the week lower, led by technology. The Dow Jones ended at about 52,002, down 0.3 percent. The S&P 500 finished at about 7,659, down 1.1 percent. The Nasdaq Composite was the worst hit, closing near 26,140, down 2.2 percent. The US 10-year Treasury yield edged up 1 basis point to 5.10 percent, which is a punishing level for anything that depends on cheap borrowing.

Two things drove the selling. First, renewed doubts about returns on artificial intelligence spending, with a deepening chip selloff and investor anxiety over debt-laden AI infrastructure companies. CoreWeave was the flashpoint, its stock hit by climbing bond yields and default fears. Second, higher oil prices feeding worries about inflation and growth.

Among the big names, Meta fell 3.7 percent to about 709 dollars, the worst of the group. Alphabet dropped 2.5 percent to about 341. Tesla fell 2.1 percent to about 355, even after Elon Musk unveiled the long-delayed Roadster, developed with SpaceX, a reveal that has fuelled fresh merger speculation between the two companies. Amazon lost 1.9 percent to 250 dollars and Microsoft lost 1.9 percent to about 529. Nvidia fell 1.4 percent to about 214. The lone gainer was Apple, up 0.4 percent to about 343, with early demand reported for its 1,999 dollar foldable iPhone Duo. Why it matters: the AI trade is still the single biggest swing factor for global equities, including the funds that flow into Asian markets.

One more warning light. Big US bank stocks are heading for their longest weekly losing streak since 2008, with the KBW Bank Index down roughly 13 percent from its August peak on mixed earnings and rising inflation.

On central banks, Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh spoke this week and deliberately gave nothing away. He said price stability is the focus but offered no signal on the next move. Traders responded by firming up bets on a hold at the October meeting. Why it matters: with the 10-year at 5.10 percent, a Fed that stays on hold keeps the dollar firm and Asian currencies under pressure.

At the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, there was no rate decision this week. Analyst previews point to a central bank still focused on curbing price pressures, and PNB expects the BSP may end its tightening cycle after one more hike. The BSP also approved a 540 billion peso provisional loan to the national government.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi rebounded sharply on Friday, closing at about 5,783, up 2.1 percent, its second straight gain. Traders cited bargain hunting, lower local oil prices, softer yields, and potential foreign fund reallocation after an MSCI warning on Indonesia. The peso, though, remains the pressure point: the dollar closed at 62.99 pesos, up 0.09 percent, with local reports describing a record low driven by geopolitical risk. Why it matters: a peso at 63 sharpens the BSP's trade-off between defending the currency and supporting growth.

On the data side, overseas Filipino remittances hit a three-month low in August, a worry for household spending. At the pump, next week brings a gasoline rollback and a slight diesel increase. In the West Philippine Sea, the Philippine Coast Guard drove Chinese boats off Lawak Island, and Manila and Beijing traded barbs after a Chinese envoy said China could "easily win" a confrontation. Separately, Microsoft ranks the Philippines the fifth most targeted country by Chinese cyber operations. And press freedom groups condemned the killing of broadcast journalist Adrian Loterte, the eighth journalist murdered under President Marcos.

Turning to India. Indian equities rallied. The Nifty 50 closed at about 22,997, up 1.7 percent, and the BSE Sensex at about 74,316, up 2.1 percent, with a second straight session of gains led by information technology and media stocks. Nifty IT hit an eight-week high after a four-day surge, logging its biggest weekly gain in 18 years and adding about 30 billion dollars in market value.

The rupee is the counterweight. The dollar closed at 94.95 rupees, up 0.45 percent on the day, though the rupee still posted its best week in seven months thanks to Reserve Bank of India intervention and new curbs. The RBI is now widening its rupee defence into interest rate policy, cautiously, and the new forex risk reserve requirement is already denting bank profitability. Forecasters still see the rupee drifting toward 100 per dollar.

Three more from India. The IMF raised India's fiscal 2027 growth forecast to 7 percent on strong domestic demand. Yes Bank flagged a hit of about 250 basis points to its core capital ratio after the Supreme Court upheld the ruling against its 2020 write-down of 8,415 crore rupees of AT-1 bonds. And politically, the founder of the youth-led "cockroach" party was detained ahead of a planned Delhi protest, after mass detentions and the jailing of two leaders last week.

Now to the world. Oil is the story. Brent crude touched 110 dollars as Iran escalated attacks in the Strait of Hormuz. BBC Verify, using UK maritime agency data, counted 12 vessels struck in a single week, a record high, and oil exports through the strait are slowing. Ukraine has also vowed to hit Russian refineries. Why it matters: Philippine inflation, the fuel subsidy bill and the peso all key off this number. President Trump said he will not use nuclear weapons against Iran and will not send ground troops, while the US reportedly weighs new strike options.

In Ukraine, Kyiv's Operation Vivaldi is retaking territory in the Donbas, but speed curbs on Starlink left Ukrainian defences exposed during heavy Russian strikes on Tuesday night. Trump denies the White House ordered any restriction. A Ukrainian minister told CNBC the curbs have emboldened Russia. The market read-through: Europe's Eutelsat jumped 18 percent this week as a potential alternative.

In the Middle East, commercial goods including fresh produce, meat and winter clothing have not entered Gaza for two days, reviving food affordability fears, and masked Israeli settlers injured Palestinians and a US journalist during the olive harvest in the West Bank. In Yemen, the government says Houthis fired two missiles at Aden airport.

On trade, US officials pressed China on market access for Mastercard and Citigroup during bilateral talks in Bangkok this week. And in Europe, anti-government protests in Albania's capital turned violent, part of a wider wave of student-led unrest across the continent.

That's your Morning Briefing for Saturday, October 17. Watch oil and the peso into Monday's open.
