Good morning. It's Thursday, November 26, 2026. Here's your Morning Briefing on finance and geopolitics: US tech stocks slide again on AI nerves, a hot inflation print hardens December Fed hike bets, and Washington proposes tariffs on 14 trading partners.

Start with markets. The three main US indexes split. The Dow Jones closed at about 50,183, up 0.3 percent. The S&P 500 finished near 7,183, down 0.3 percent. The Nasdaq Composite ended around 23,586, down 0.6 percent, its weakness again driven by artificial intelligence names after an earlier session in which the index was down almost 4 percent intraday. The US 10-year Treasury yield fell 6 basis points to 4.85 percent, with sliding yields tempering the dollar's safe-haven bid.

Among the big names, Nvidia was the worst performer, down 3.9 percent to about 149 dollars, after S&P Global Ratings warned the chipmaker's credit rating could be cut by one notch, flagging rising management and governance risk following Jensen Huang's return as chief executive. Vice President JD Vance said separately he had been assured Huang is not under Justice Department investigation. Amazon fell 3.1 percent, Alphabet lost 2.1 percent, Microsoft slipped 1.3 percent and Tesla eased 1.2 percent. Apple rose 0.4 percent and Meta was the standout, up 3.4 percent to about 610 dollars. Why it matters: the selling is concentrated in AI-linked megacaps, so index losses understate how narrow and how sharp the repricing is.

On central banks, the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation gauge rose again in October, and markets now treat a rate hike at the Fed's final 2026 meeting in December as close to settled. Gold fell below 3,500 dollars an ounce, near a 15-month low, as those hike bets built. The Fed also published minutes of its October 19 and October 28 discount rate meetings. Chair Kevin Warsh's stated preference for reading market signals is being tested by the Nvidia-driven volatility. At the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, there was no rate decision overnight. The BSP did tighten supervision of banks' agricultural loan books amid El Niño losses, said uptake of its regulatory relief measures remains low, called the decline in gross international reserves "scary but manageable," and defended earlier gold sales as the metal hit record highs before this week's drop.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi closed at about 5,475, down 0.3 percent, pausing after a three-day bargain-hunting rally driven partly by MSCI index positioning that had carried it near 5,500. The peso firmed slightly to 63.60 to the dollar. On the fiscal side, the government raised 1.26 billion dollars from its retail Treasury bond offer, and Bloomberg reports emerging Asia's best bond rally is seen extending here. Why it matters: strong domestic demand for government paper gives Manila funding room even as reserves slip. The Department of Agriculture suspended meat and live animal imports from four countries, while the Sugar Regulatory Administration warned El Niño, pests and high costs may cut sugar output further, and the agrarian reform department earmarked 600 million pesos for El Niño response in 2027. On security, the Armed Forces says Chinese military patrols are moving closer to the Philippines. And in corporate news, Ayala Corporation says it is open to exiting and delisting more businesses, DigiPlus is taking legal action to recover its Brazil investments after an online gaming ban, and India's largest airline will enter the Philippine market by the first quarter of 2027.

Turning to India. The Nifty 50 closed flat at about 25,820, holding Wednesday's near 2 percent surge, while the BSE Sensex added 0.3 percent to roughly 84,164. The rupee strengthened 0.4 percent to 92.41 to the dollar, supported by foreign equity inflows and softer crude; strategists say it has room to rally toward 90 to 91 before the Reserve Bank of India steps in. The RBI's bulletin said the Indian economy stands out amid uneven global performance, but warned of broad-based and more generalised food price pressures, and confirmed it was a net dollar buyer in September. The central bank's dollar-rupee swap facility has pulled in 73 billion dollars from banks in 11 weeks, and it has moved 60 tonnes of gold back into India in six months. On growth, the economy expanded 7.7 percent in fiscal 2025-26 with fourth-quarter growth of 7.8 percent, and S&P Global projects 7 percent growth in each of the next two fiscal years. Why it matters: strong growth plus sticky food inflation points to an RBI that stays on hold rather than easing. India is also on the US tariff list, which will be the key risk for exporters today.

Now the world. The United States has proposed new tariffs on 14 trading partners, including China, the European Union and India, aimed at what Washington calls unfair, discriminatory or burdensome trade practices and structural excess industrial capacity. Why it matters: this is the single biggest macro variable for Asian exporters and for global inflation expectations heading into December. In Asia, the Bank of Korea resumed tightening, raising its benchmark rate by 25 basis points to 3.25 percent, the highest in two years, and upgraded growth forecasts to 3.9 percent this year and 4.1 percent next. The Kospi led a regional tech rebound, with Japan's Nikkei 225 up 1.2 percent and Australia's ASX 200 up 0.2 percent. North Korean leader Kim Jong Un said the country should widen deployment of tactical nuclear weapons and warned that denying its nuclear status would be treated as an open declaration of war.

In Europe, Ukraine says it struck a Russian airbase in the Nizhny Novgorod region east of Moscow, hitting five aircraft. Britain faces a 26 percent jump in energy bills from January after a surge in gas prices, lifting a typical annual household bill from 1,723 to 2,166 pounds, and discount chain Poundland has collapsed into administration, putting 11,000 jobs at risk. The UK government is also set to publish new treaties with the EU containing a 10-year termination clause. In the Middle East, Israeli judges recommended prosecutors drop the most serious charges against Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, saying there is little prospect of conviction in one of the three cases against him. And in Egypt, 31 people were killed and 21 injured when a fuel tanker overturned and caught fire on a Cairo ring road.

One more for the tech file: SpaceX won FCC approval to deploy up to 1 million orbital data-centre satellites, a plan opposed by Amazon and others, and prediction market Kalshi now puts an OpenAI initial public offering later into 2027. Why it matters: capital is still chasing AI infrastructure even as AI equities sell off.

That's your Morning Briefing for Thursday, November 26. Trade carefully, and have a productive day.
