Good morning. It's Saturday, November 28, 2026. Here's your briefing: a tech-led rally closes a wild week on Wall Street, the PSEi punches back above 5,600, and the Iran conflict widens after a deadly drone strike in Kuwait.

To markets. Wall Street ended the week with a strong bounce. The Dow closed at about 50,775, up 1.2 percent, or roughly 600 points. The S&P 500 finished near 7,297, up 1.6 percent, and the Nasdaq Composite at about 24,130, up 2.3 percent. Important caveat: that rally came inside a November sell-off that has already pushed the S&P 500 into a correction, so one good session does not reverse the month. Bonds went the other way. The US 10-year Treasury yield rose 9 basis points to 4.94 percent, a reminder that the squeeze on equity valuations is coming from rates, not just AI doubts.

Big tech led. Tesla jumped 5.2 percent to exactly 400 dollars. Amazon rose 4.0 percent to about 254, as reporting showed Amazon and other retailers blocking AI shopping agents from OpenAI, Meta and Google, protecting its own checkout. Meta gained 3.1 percent to about 629. The megacap complex was more subdued: Nvidia up 0.8 percent to about 150 after a whipsaw week around Jensen Huang's surprise return as chief executive, Apple up 0.8 percent to about 359, Alphabet up 0.7 percent to about 275, and Microsoft up 0.6 percent to about 493. Privately traded SpaceX shares rose more than 4 percent after Elon Musk said he would donate shares to American children annually. Bitcoin diverged, slipping back below 60,000 dollars.

On central banks: no Federal Reserve decision this week, but the pressure is visible. Inflation is running hot enough that Chair Kevin Warsh's credibility is now the story, gold hit a 15-month low before paring losses to a weekly gain of roughly 2.5 percent, and some economists, including Gregory Daco, now expect a rate hike next month rather than a cut. The dollar is heading for its longest monthly winning streak since 2024. Separately, President Trump floated a 10 percent cap on credit card interest rates and demanded a probe into US GDP revision data. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas made no policy move, but flagged November inflation at 7.2 to 8.2 percent. Why it matters: both the Fed and the BSP are now fighting inflation, not slowdown, so cheap money is off the table into 2027.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi rebounded 2.22 percent to close at 5,611.97, lifted by a regional rally and by MSCI rebalancing flows. International Container Terminal Services led the charge, jumping 7.27 percent. The peso slipped again, closing at 63.69 to the dollar, 0.13 percent weaker, pressured by the broad dollar strength we just described.

Three stories to watch. First, the BSP's November inflation forecast of 7.2 to 8.2 percent keeps price pressure firmly in double-digit-adjacent territory, and the central bank blamed a weak export push for the current crisis. Why it matters: a weak peso plus high inflation leaves the BSP little room to support growth. Second, AirAsia Philippines delayed staff salary payments to month-end amid retrenchments, with the Department of Labor stepping in to mediate, though the airline says flights are normal. Third, the Energy Regulatory Commission ordered Meralco to refund 1.99 billion pesos to consumers, and oil firms are expected to cut diesel and gasoline prices by about 3 pesos a liter, with airline fuel surcharges also reduced for early December. Politically, Vice President Sara Duterte said resignation is out of the question as her impeachment trial proceeds. Also notable: metallic mineral output hit a record 323.84 billion pesos in nine months, and Philippine banks were singled out in Asia for an improving outlook.

Turning to India. Indian equities snapped a two-day rally and a four-week winning run. The Nifty 50 closed at about 25,607, down 1.1 percent, and the BSE Sensex at about 83,339, down 1.2 percent, a fall of 989 points, driven by profit booking. The rupee weakened 0.58 percent to 92.99 to the dollar. Government bonds slumped, with the benchmark yield touching 7.2650 percent, as a spike in oil prices and rising US Treasury yields fed bets the Reserve Bank of India may have to hike.

From the RBI: the central bank cut the minimum daily cash reserve ratio maintenance requirement to 90 percent, easing short-term liquidity for banks, and its dollar-rupee swap facility has pulled in 73 billion dollars from banks in 11 weeks. Governor-level commentary flagged that Indian firms use only 20 to 30 percent of available free trade agreement benefits, and pegged AI's potential value to Indian small businesses at 685 billion dollars. On growth, India's economy expanded 7.7 percent in fiscal 2025-26, with fourth-quarter growth accelerating to 7.8 percent, and S&P Global projects 7 percent for the next two fiscal years. Why it matters: strong growth plus inflation and oil risk means India's rate cycle, like everyone else's, is turning hawkish.

To the world. The biggest story is Iran. Italy says the drone used in the deadly Kuwait attack, which killed two Italians, was Iranian-made. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi called the incident suspicious and "a precooked recipe for war", while President Trump said the Iran war ends "whenever I want it". Why it matters: this is now the main driver of the oil spike feeding global bond yields. OPEC+, meanwhile, is expected to hold production targets steady for the first quarter of 2027.

Second, China stepped in to support its markets. A rare intraday state media readout signalled liquidity support, sending Chinese chip stocks to their biggest ever one-day gain and lifting copper, with Chinese industrial profits posting their strongest growth in five months. Why it matters: Beijing is putting a floor under the AI trade that Asia, including the Philippines, is levered to.

Third, Europe. Spain votes Sunday in a snap election that could end Pedro Sánchez's eight years in power and pull Madrid closer to Washington. EU leaders failed again to break the deadlock over the bloc's 1.7 trillion dollar budget, with talks resuming in December. Budget carrier Norse Atlantic filed for bankruptcy after soaring fuel costs and a failed funding round, and German software giant SAP is reportedly cutting at least 10,000 jobs in an AI restructuring. The UK confirmed it will pay a participation fee toward EU running costs to deliver its new deals.

Finally, Ukraine's Volodymyr Zelensky told the BBC he finds the Trump-Putin friendship "strange" and says Washington is not pressing Moscow hard enough in peace talks, as Kyiv braces for another punishing winter. And Taiwan votes today in local elections, with the Taipei mayoral race a key test for the governing Democratic Progressive Party.

That's your Morning Briefing for Saturday, November 28. Have a good weekend.
