Good morning. It's Friday, October 23, 2026. Today: an AI-driven selloff on Wall Street, a jumbo rate hike from the Bangko Sentral, a record-low peso, and oil above $112 a barrel.

To markets. Wall Street split sharply along technology lines. The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at about 52,868, essentially flat, up 0.02 percent. The S&P 500 fell 1.2 percent to roughly 7,663. The Nasdaq Composite dropped 2.6 percent to about 26,009, its steepest one-day fall since June. The trigger was renewed doubt about the scale of artificial intelligence spending. Meta was the epicentre, down 7.0 percent to about 649 after it raised capital spending guidance to as much as $145 billion, pushing free cash flow negative despite revenue beating expectations. Prices on Meta's five-year credit default swaps hit all-time highs, meaning bond investors are now pricing credit risk at a company that used to be a cash machine. Amazon fell 4.6 percent, Nvidia 4.4 percent, Alphabet 3.2 percent, Tesla 3.1 percent and Microsoft 2.2 percent. Apple was the lone gainer, up 1.2 percent to about 348, and is now up 28 percent this year as investors seek Big Tech exposure without heavy AI infrastructure bills. Short sellers betting against neocloud and data centre names, including CoreWeave and Nebius, are sitting on roughly $6 billion in paper profits this month. Nvidia's Jensen Huang pushed back, saying neocloud demand is "off the charts" but capital is scarce, and that he sees no sign of a memory glut by 2029.

Money fled to bonds. The US 10-year Treasury yield fell 19 basis points to 4.95 percent, the biggest single-day drop in over a year, even with oil surging. Why it matters: that is a growth scare, not a disinflation story. Traders also trimmed bets on a December Fed hike. The Fed has already raised rates once this cycle, its first increase since 2023, and the debate now is whether AI capital spending forces more. US 30-year mortgage rates eased to 7.32 percent from 7.40 percent, the first pullback since late August.

Closer to home, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas surprised with a 50-basis-point hike, taking the policy rate to 5.50 percent. That is its biggest move since February 2023 and the fourth straight increase this year, and the BSP signalled further tightening. Why it matters: Manila is now defending the peso and fighting inflation at the same time, and borrowing costs for Philippine households and companies are going up again.

Now to the Philippines. The PSEi fell 0.72 percent to about 5,780, snapping a five-day rally as the BSP decision and profit-taking hit sentiment. The peso closed at 63.43 to the dollar, weaker by 0.11 percent, its third straight record-low close, with local reports flagging intraday trade past 63.50. The bigger market story is Mynt, the parent of GCash, which rose on debut after raising 92.3 billion pesos in the largest IPO in Philippine history. Exchange officials call it proof of market resilience; it is also a rare liquidity event in a thin market. Weather is the near-term risk: Typhoon Rosal, internationally named Champi, is inside the Philippine Area of Responsibility and generating 10-metre waves east of Luzon, with another low pressure area being tracked outside. Separately, the government is bracing for about 20 billion pesos in El Niño-related losses, power outages may hit Visayas consumers, and the Department of Finance has cleared an administrative order to ease port congestion. Exports are still forecast to break $100 billion this year.

Turning to India. Indian equities fell for a third straight session. The Nifty 50 ended at about 22,811, down 0.3 percent, and the BSE Sensex at roughly 73,710, down 0.2 percent, with around 3 lakh crore rupees wiped from listed market value in the session. The rupee weakened 0.53 percent to 94.81 to the dollar. The common thread is crude: Brent above $110 is a direct hit to India's import bill, and media, FMCG, IT, power and metal stocks led the declines. The Reserve Bank of India has turned hawkish, raising rates as crude, bond yields and portfolio outflows pressure the currency. One SBI economist estimates the RBI has deployed roughly $80 billion defending the rupee, while a former central bank official warned publicly that using the exchange rate as a stabiliser risks damaging the real economy. The bright spot is primary issuance: the Jio IPO saw overwhelming demand on day one even as the broader market struggled. Watch Yes Bank too, which looks unlikely to pay coupons on its additional tier-1 bonds this fiscal year despite a Supreme Court ruling that annulled the earlier write-off.

Now the world. Oil is the dominant macro variable. Brent pushed above $112 a barrel, a five-month high, as fallout from the Iran war continues to scramble shipping. Chartering a tanker for the US-to-Asia crude run has hit a record of nearly $80 million, and US crude exports to Asia are heading for multi-year lows. Why it matters: this is an energy-import shock for the Philippines and India simultaneously, and it complicates every central bank's inflation path.

Second, Europe. France is in a deepening fiscal crisis that, alongside AI debt worries, is stressing bond markets; the yen jumped as investors sought safety. At least four people died after storms and flooding in southern France, including two women found drowned in a cellar in Bouches-du-Rhône.

Third, cyber. Developers of the programming language PHP disclosed critical vulnerabilities, uncovered with AI assistance and described as the most significant ever found that way. Governments pulled websites offline worldwide and millions of sites have data at risk. If you run anything web-facing, patching is today's priority.

Fourth, Musk and Ukraine. President Trump says Elon Musk has restored Ukraine's Starlink access after reports Musk ordered a slowdown. Governments are now hunting for Starlink alternatives and finding almost none, with Amazon and Eutelsat far behind. Musk is also pouring $366 million into US midterm campaigns, with SpaceX and Tesla regulatory exposure riding on Senate control, and has invested about $1 billion in Skydance.

Also worth knowing: OpenAI is close to a funding round of up to $30 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation, led by UAE investors, as a bridge to a 2027 IPO. Barclays is cutting 5,000 jobs, about 5 percent of staff, citing greater reliance on AI. A magnitude 7 earthquake in China's Tibet has killed 16 and injured 32. And North Korea's Kim Yo Jong has warned Seoul that border loudspeakers will be ready within days, while Taiwan's security agency flags rising Chinese disinformation ahead of local elections.

That's your Morning Briefing for Friday, October 23. Stay safe in the storm, and we'll see you Monday.
